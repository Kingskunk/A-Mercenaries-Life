HELPING YOUR ELDERS
An old lady (stat block of a Commoner), carrying a heavy
basket tries to pass the road. She is visibly struggling —
but no one stopped to help her so far.
The party can decide to help, and the lady will engage
in conversation — sharing that she is planning to go to
the market, to sell the vegetables in her basket.
She will thank the party at the end of the journey with
a payment of 3 SP.

## The Marlcross Milling Fair: Pie-Eating Contest
**Best location:** Marlcross. The Grain Green already has market awnings and public space, the ridge windmill and grain barns make flour central to the village, and the Wayfarer's Rest is the natural place to register, collect the entry fee, and hear gossip before the contest.

**Timing:** a once-per-year autumn fair, announced about two months ahead on notice boards in Port Valen, Timbermouth, and the villages. It should run once in a campaign year, rather than become a repeatable tavern activity. The pies, local sponsor, prize, and minor contestants can change from year to year.

**Set-up:** The Wayfarer's Rest registers contestants and keeps the prize purse. On the fair day, the Grain Green is dressed with trestle tables, flour-sack banners, and benches for spectators. The miller provides the flour; farm kitchens provide the fillings. The prize can be coin, a sack of first-grade flour, or a useful village favor rather than a generic magical reward.

**Contest:** The player may enter for a small fee or watch. Three pies make one round. At the end of each round, remaining contestants make a Constitution check: DC 5 for the first round, then +5 for every round they survive. Failure means withdrawing in embarrassment. The player does not need full monster-stat-block opponents; use local figures such as a mill hand, a hedge healer, a road warden on leave, and a visiting pit fighter.

**The returning champion:** Last year's champion, Boro Broadbelt, arrives in an enormous padded coat and boasts about the record of twenty-four pies. "Boro" is actually three dwarf cousins—Tov, Rann, and Kel Broadbelt—stacked in the coat and changing places behind the table whenever the crowd's attention turns elsewhere. Their effective DC remains 5 because each cousin only eats a few pies.

**Discovery and choice:** A character with Wisdom/Perception 15 or better notices the coat settling oddly, mismatched boots below the hem, or a different voice answering from inside. On discovering it, the player can expose the cheat, quietly ask for a share of the prize, or let the cousins keep their harmless title. Each choice should have a small social consequence in Marlcross; none needs to turn the festival into a fight or a major quest.

**Variation:** Keep the core trick, but vary the pie filling, prize, and one or two opponents in later campaign years. The fair can also supply small hooks: a missing prize purse, an argument over mill flour, or a contestant who needs help getting to Marlcross.
