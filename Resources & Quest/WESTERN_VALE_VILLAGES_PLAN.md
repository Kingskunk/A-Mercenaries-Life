# The Three Villages of the Western Vale - Regional Quest Plan

## Status

Planning document only. None of the village names, map positions, characters, markets, quests, commodity additions, or political arrangements proposed below are implemented canon until they are approved and added to the game. Do not take or implement any infomation from this file unless stritcly permitted to do so. Anything and everything in this file could change on a time / and this entire file can be deleted at will.

The four-place limit is a design constraint for each village. Two of those four places must be governmental or otherwise belong to House Gault's authority. The G.U.A.R.D.S. categories are functions to cover, not a requirement for six separate destinations.

## Regional Purpose

These villages should give visible origins to goods already passing through Langston and make the Western Vale feel like a governed agricultural region rather than empty fields surrounding one market town.

The strongest placement is to make them the three unnamed villages on Langston's east road already served by Edric Merrow from the True Sun temple. The road and its feeder lanes can support three different agricultural communities without turning each one into a miniature town:

- **Harrowfield**, nearest Langston, supplies grain, vegetables, and hemp.
- **Vinebank**, on a sheltered chalk rise farther east, supplies wine and orchard goods.
- **Broadmead**, reached by a pasture lane near the road's eastern end, supplies wool, dairy goods, and horses.

Their goods move into Langston's covered scales and wagon bays. Langston remains the regional market, contract centre, and caravan assembly point rather than losing those functions to three new hubs.

## Existing Canon to Preserve

- The Countess of the Western Vale rules from Dunmow and appoints Langston's mayor.
- Langston Hall is House Gault's administrative hall. It handles petitions, disputes, market tolls, road levies, militia service, House Gault contracts, and the House Gault page of the Contractor's Insignia.
- Langston is a prosperous walled market town among open pasture and cultivated fields.
- Wool bales, small wine casks, bound hemp, horses, farm food, and other rural traffic already pass through Langston.
- Edric Merrow says he covers Langston's temple and three villages on the east road.
- The Western Vale road is maintained by House Gault and crosses level grassland and tilled fields between Langston and Fellgate.
- The current commodity system treats tagged commodities as regional goods bought by every open commodity market. Maker locations and freight distance establish local supply and price.
- Wool, cheese, butter, animal fat, grain, vegetables, eggs, poultry, orchard apples, honey, and beeswax already exist as commodities.
- Wine and hemp are visible in Langston's fiction but are not currently bulk commodities.
- Wine and hemp should each receive one generic market entry. Casks, vintages, bales, cordage, and local grades may appear in prose, but must not create additional commodity names or separate market rows.
- Hemp rope remains an individual adventuring item rather than an additional bulk commodity.
- Horses use the existing mount, stable, and saddlebags systems. They should not become stackable commodity inventory.

## The House Gault Model

These villages should feel materially different from settlements governed through the Gilded Scales.

Authority descends through a personal and landed hierarchy:

**The Countess at Dunmow -> the Mayor of Langston -> village heads -> local clerks, retainers, tenants, and labourers.**

Recommended characteristics:

- Each village has an appointed **village head** who handles daily administration and answers to the Mayor of Langston. The mayor appoints or confirms the village head under the Countess's seal.
- The first authority destination in every village is called **Village Head's Hall**. It combines the village head's residence, public office, record room, and petition space. Its architecture, records, and local business may differ, but the player-facing location name remains standardized for easier navigation.
- The village heads attend appointed meeting days at Langston Hall and may be summoned there during an emergency. They bring local registers, petitions, shortages, road concerns, militia returns, and disputes that exceed their authority.
- The mayor coordinates the three villages, hears serious appeals, issues local orders under the Countess's seal, and sends matters reserved to the Countess onward to Dunmow. Between meetings, village heads communicate with Langston Hall through clerks, sealed letters, and runners.
- House Gault's steward remains a senior estate officer at Dunmow, not another routine rank between the mayor and the village heads.
- Each village's second authority place is practical rather than monumental: a retainer lodge, tithe granary, bonded cellar, remount yard, or toll post.
- Markets operate on appointed days under a House Gault charter. The estate controls the official scales, seals, and measures.
- Rents may be owed in coin, produce, animals, or defined labour. Exact obligations should be settled before implementation rather than assuming every tenant is a serf.
- Retainers answer to House Gault rather than to an elected village council. They keep order, carry warrants, guard stores, escort rents, and organize militia service.
- Villagers may possess customary rights, testify in disputes, and assemble in a moot. Those practices give them a voice without making the village sovereign.
- The system should provide real benefits as well as costs: maintained roads, standard measures, protected stores, organized relief, escorts, and reserve supplies alongside tolls, levies, rents, and restricted trade rights.
- Ordinary buying and selling can remain open to travellers. Official work, estate petitions, and sealed contracts should use the existing House Gault Insignia page rather than creating a separate reputation system.

The rural underworld should grow out of attempts to evade or exploit this system. Untithed wine, concealed grain, altered livestock brands, poaching, forged toll slips, false weights, illegal grazing, and retainers selling confiscated goods fit better than an urban thieves' guild.

## G.U.A.R.D.S. Design Rule

For this plan, G.U.A.R.D.S. means:

- **G - Government:** where authority is encountered and decisions are recorded.
- **U - Underworld:** illicit activity, evasion, corruption, or informal opposition.
- **A - Altars:** local worship and the place served by Edric's circuit.
- **R - Resources:** what sustains the village and enters regional trade.
- **D - Defenses:** retainers, militia equipment, stores, walls, watches, or other safeguards.
- **S - Social:** where residents, travellers, and rumours gather.

Each village covers all six functions through four destinations:

1. A civil or estate authority place.
2. A second authority place with a defensive or economic role.
3. A combined resource and religious place.
4. A combined social and underworld place.

This shared use is intentional. A village should not possess six fully independent institutions merely to complete the framework.

## Village One: Harrowfield

### Identity and Landscape

Harrowfield is the nearest of the three villages to Langston, provisionally four miles east along the maintained road. Broad strip fields of barley and mixed grain surround the settlement. Hemp grows in smaller plots, while onions, potatoes, cabbage, and other durable vegetables occupy household ground. Shallow retting ponds lie downwind and away from the wells. Bound hemp dries in the fields before travelling to Langston.

Harrowfield should feel closely tied to Langston. Carts can reach the town and return in a day, so its disputes concern weights, rents, storage, and access to the market more often than isolation.

### Four Places of Interest

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one.

Harrowfield's village head keeps the holding rolls, records transfers of land, hears boundary complaints, and announces labour and road obligations. A covered petition bench stands outside so residents can wait without entering the village head's private rooms. A customary village moot may offer testimony or a collective recommendation, but the village head states the ruling under House Gault's seal and answers for it at the next heads' meeting in Langston Hall.

Possible interactions:

- Read notices of field closures, road labour, rents, and missing stores.
- Petition the village head or bring evidence in a tenancy or boundary dispute.
- Take House Gault work appropriate to the village.
- See the tension between the village head's local knowledge and the orders arriving from Dunmow or Langston.

#### 2. The Tithe Granary and Levy House

**Functions:** Defenses; government; resources; authority place two.

A heavy timber granary stands on stone pads behind a fenced yard. It stores rent grain, reserve seed, and emergency food. The attached levy house contains a clerk's desk, fire-fighting tools, spear racks, shields, and a room for a small retainer detail. A cistern and strict lamp rules protect the store from fire.

Possible interactions:

- Inspect a shortage between the village head's tally and the grain physically present.
- Help contain a granary fire or prevent one during a dry season.
- Escort collected rents to Langston.
- Muster with or assist the local levy during a regional threat.

#### 3. Ropewalk Green

**Functions:** Resources; altar; daytime social activity.

A long ropewalk, grain scale, seasonal stalls, drying frames, and loading rails share the village green. A modest True Sun shrine stands beside the official scale. Edric can hold a brief service there during his circuit, while a local keeper maintains the lamp and reports serious needs to Langston's temple.

The ropewalk works hemp into cordage, but its ordinary output is local and regional equipment. The proposed bulk commodity remains simply **Hemp**. Finished hemp rope continues to use the existing individual item and does not create another commodity row.

Possible interactions:

- Use the commodity market on its appointed day.
- Observe an official weighing or challenge a suspicious measure.
- Meet Edric during one of his riding days.
- Learn which fields, households, and carters are behind or ahead on the harvest.

#### 4. The Bound Sheaf

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Bound Sheaf is a small alehouse and lodging house used by carters waiting for a load or an early start. Its loft and rear sheds make it the natural place for untallied hemp, substituted sacks, false scale weights, or grain threshed at night before the village head's clerk can count it.

The proprietor should not automatically be the leader of every crime. They may tolerate some dealings to keep the house alive while refusing violence or the theft of reserve food.

### Trade Identity

Existing major candidates:

- Grain.
- Onions, potatoes, cabbage, and root-vegetable bundles.
- Farm eggs and dressed chicken.
- Barley loaves as a smaller worked good.

Proposed new major commodity:

- **Hemp.** Harrowfield is a major maker. Bales and loose fibre are descriptions of the same commodity, not separate goods.

Likely imports include salt, iron tools and bars, wool cloth, timber, and wine.

### Quest Seeds

#### The Empty Measure

The tithe granary is short, but the cause is initially uncertain. Possible explanations include false weights used by an official, a retainer selling grain off the books, desperate tenants hiding food after a poor yield, spoilage concealed to protect a clerk, or more than one of these at once. The player should establish who acted, who benefited, and who will bear the shortage rather than solving the problem through a generic accusation.

#### Fire in the Drying Frames

A fire begins among the drying hemp during dangerous weather. The immediate objective is rescue and containment. The later question is whether it was accident, negligent lamp use, retaliation against the village head, or an attempt to destroy evidence attached to a shipment.

#### The Road Levy

The village head has orders to send workers to repair the east road during a narrow harvest window. The work is genuinely needed, but taking too many hands will damage the harvest. The player can investigate the repair need, carry a petition, expose an inflated labour demand, or help arrange a narrower and workable levy.

### G.U.A.R.D.S. Coverage

- **G:** Village Head's Hall; Tithe Granary clerk and House seal.
- **U:** Bound Sheaf's hidden stores, substitutions, and false weights.
- **A:** True Sun shrine on Ropewalk Green.
- **R:** Grain, vegetables, hemp, poultry, ropewalk, and granary.
- **D:** Retainers, levy equipment, fire tools, cistern, and protected stores.
- **S:** Bound Sheaf and market-day activity on Ropewalk Green.

## Village Two: Vinebank

### Identity and Landscape

Vinebank lies provisionally nine miles east of Langston where a low chalk rise gives the Vale one of its few pronounced slopes. Vineyards occupy the sheltered, sunward ground. Grain and grazing continue across the flatter land, while small orchards, hedges, and beehives break the vines.

The village's wine should be good regional table wine: valuable enough to regulate and transport, but common enough to appear in small casks beneath Langston's market awnings. Vintage, quality, and origin may matter in dialogue or quests, but the inventory and market must always aggregate it under the single commodity name **Wine**.

### Four Places of Interest

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one.

Vinebank's village head keeps vineyard leases, inheritance records, harvest tallies, and the local register of approved cask marks. The village head hears ordinary disputes over boundary vines, access paths, water, neglected plots, and the division of a vintage owed as rent. The mayor's clerks or a House wine steward may audit those records, while the mayor hears serious appeals at Langston Hall.

Possible interactions:

- Present a petition concerning a lease or confiscated vintage.
- Examine the legal cask marks and compare them with a suspected forgery.
- Take a sealed inspection, escort, or recovery contract.
- See whether the village head treats the office as public responsibility, estate extraction, or personal opportunity.

#### 2. The Cask Gate

**Functions:** Defenses; government; resources; authority place two.

A retainer lodge and toll arch stand beside a bonded cellar at the roadward edge of the village. Retainers inspect departing casks, verify the estate seal, keep seized goods under lock, and hold offenders in a small two-cell lockup until the village head conducts an initial hearing or sends a serious case to the mayor.

The Cask Gate should not make the village physically walled. It controls the reliable cart road and legal wine trade, while footpaths and vineyard lanes remain harder to watch.

Possible interactions:

- Show papers or a House Gault commission.
- Inspect seized wine or question a detained carter.
- Discover corruption in the return or resale of confiscated casks.
- Defend the bonded cellar against raiders or a deliberately set fire.

#### 3. The Common Press and Sun Chapel

**Functions:** Resources; altar.

Small growers bring grapes to a large communal press rather than each maintaining expensive equipment. A simple True Sun chapel occupies one end of the press yard. Its harvest rites emphasize light, patience, honest measure, and shared work. Edric visits on his circuit; a lay keeper tends it in his absence.

Possible interactions:

- Observe or join the press during harvest.
- Meet growers who will not speak freely inside Village Head's Hall.
- Receive healing or religious guidance when Edric is present.
- Discover adulteration, sabotage, disease, or contamination through the work itself.

#### 4. The Red Stave

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Red Stave is a vintners' taproom, small inn, and cooper's gathering place. Its cellar is where untithed casks may acquire copied seals, weak wine may receive a prestigious name, and discreet buyers ask which loads will avoid the Cask Gate.

The room should also be the village's honest social centre: harvest meals, marriage cups, seasonal hiring, and growers comparing weather. Illicit dealings occupy part of its life rather than defining every person inside.

### Trade Identity

Proposed new major commodity:

- **Wine.** Vinebank is a major maker and Langston is its first aggregation market. Casks and vintages are prose distinctions, not separate commodity entries.

Existing secondary candidates:

- Orchard apples.
- Honey and beeswax from vineyard-edge hives.
- Limited grain or cheese from mixed holdings.

Likely imports include timber and iron for casks and hoops, salt and preserved food, wool cloth, and tools.

### Quest Seeds

#### The Borrowed Mark

Several casks bearing the mark of a respected grower contain inferior or adulterated wine. The grower may be a victim, participant, or debtor forced to lend the mark. The player must trace responsibility through the press, cooperage, Red Stave, and Cask Gate without letting the steward assign guilt solely by ownership of the seal.

#### The Last Good Slope

A House wine steward orders an old tenant vineyard cleared and reassigned after several weak harvests, leaving the village head to announce and enforce the order. The vines may be diseased, the tenant may have been deprived of water or labour, or a favoured household may want the sheltered land. The decision should involve evidence and named costs rather than a simple good-tenant versus evil-steward split.

#### Cellar Under Seal

A bonded cellar begins leaking fumes or produces sounds after being sealed with a disputed load inside. The immediate danger may be mundane fermentation pressure, poison, a trapped person, or a creature drawn to the warmth. The official seal makes opening the cellar a legal decision as well as a practical one.

### G.U.A.R.D.S. Coverage

- **G:** Village Head's Hall and the Cask Gate's official records.
- **U:** Untithed casks, copied seals, false vintages, and corrupt resale.
- **A:** Sun Chapel in the common press yard.
- **R:** Vineyards, press, wine, orchards, hives, and cooperage traffic.
- **D:** Retainer lodge, bonded cellar, controlled cart road, and lockup.
- **S:** Red Stave and communal harvest work.

## Village Three: Broadmead

### Identity and Landscape

Broadmead lies on a pasture lane branching from the eastern part of the Langston road, provisionally near the Fellgate end of the Vale. Open grassland supports sheep, dairy cattle, hay ground, and House Gault's remount herd. The village spreads around folds and fenced lanes rather than forming one tight street.

Broadmead's best horses are not ordinary market goods. Some belong directly to the Countess, while others are raised by tenants under breeding agreements or purchased under House right of first offer. Wool and dairy goods provide the steadier village economy.

### Four Places of Interest

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one.

Broadmead's village head records livestock brands, breeding claims, grazing rights, winter pasture, strays, and damage done when animals cross a boundary. An enclosed petition yard lets residents bring disputed animals before the village head. The mayor's clerks may audit the register, and the mayor hears serious appeals at Langston Hall.

Possible interactions:

- Bring evidence in a grazing, ownership, or breeding dispute.
- Inspect the brand register and movement papers.
- Petition against a requisition or unlawful enclosure.
- Take local House Gault work involving missing stock, damaged pasture, or an official escort.

#### 2. The Countess's Remount Yard

**Functions:** Defenses; government; resources; authority place two.

A retainer lodge, stable, smithy bay, tack room, and militia muster field serve House Gault's mounted retainers. A mounted sergeant manages the Countess's horses, inspects animals offered under contract, and carries sealed authority to requisition mounts during a genuine military need.

Any player horse purchase here should reuse or deliberately extend the existing mount-selection and ownership system. Horses must not be implemented as stackable commodities.

Possible interactions:

- Seek a mount through the established horse system.
- Assist the mounted retainers or question a requisition.
- Investigate missing tack, altered papers, or an injured remount.
- See the militia muster during a threat without making Broadmead a permanent army camp.

#### 3. Longfold Fair and the Sun Pillar

**Functions:** Resources; altar; daytime social activity.

Permanent sheep rails, weighing pens, shearing sheds, dairy stalls, and a long watering trough surround the market ground. A weathered solar pillar serves as the village shrine. Edric can conduct rites outdoors in fair weather or beneath the shearing shed in rain.

Possible interactions:

- Use the commodity market on its appointed day.
- Inspect wool, cheese, butter, or animal-fat lots.
- Meet drovers, shepherds, dairy workers, and Edric.
- Resolve an animal panic, disease concern, or disputed weighing without requiring another location.

#### 4. The Crooked Halter

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Crooked Halter is a drovers' inn with a stable yard and a private back room used for livestock bargains. False brands, stolen breeding papers, unlicensed grazing, concealed animals, and suspiciously cheap horses form its underworld.

The innkeeper should know the difference between a tenant hiding one ewe from a ruinous count and an organized horse thief. Their loyalties can depend on the harm being done rather than on a blanket commitment to either law or crime.

### Trade Identity

Existing major candidates:

- Wool.
- Cheese.
- Butter.

Existing minor candidates:

- Animal fat.
- Raw hides.
- Eggs or dressed chicken from mixed household production.

Horses remain a mount, breeding, quest, and political resource rather than a commodity stack. Hay may remain scenery and operating cost unless a later economy pass establishes a clear reason to add it as a bulk item.

Likely imports include grain, salt, ironwork, timber, hemp, wine, and finished cloth.

### Quest Seeds

#### The Countess's Grey

A valuable grey remount disappears before inspection. The obvious suspect has a history of petty livestock theft, but the horse's papers, route, and handling suggest help from someone who knows the yard. Recovery matters, but so does determining whether the horse was stolen for profit, hidden from an unlawful requisition, or removed to conceal an injury.

#### The Winter Count

The village head's recorded flock numbers and the animals on the meadow do not agree. False brands, dead animals left on the rolls, illegal grazing, predation, and a clerk accepting payments can all contribute. The player must identify the discrepancy before winter pasture is assigned and innocent households lose access.

#### Muster on the Meadow

A sealed order calls for horses and levy hands. The order may be genuine but based on bad numbers, genuine and urgent, or copied from an older writ. The player can verify it, carry objections, expose forgery, or help fulfill a legitimate muster without deciding that the village may simply ignore the Countess.

### G.U.A.R.D.S. Coverage

- **G:** Village Head's Hall and the Remount Yard's sealed orders.
- **U:** False brands, stolen papers, concealed animals, and unlawful grazing.
- **A:** Sun Pillar at Longfold Fair.
- **R:** Wool, dairy, pasture, livestock, and remount breeding.
- **D:** Mounted retainers, stable yard, smithy bay, tack stores, and muster field.
- **S:** Crooked Halter and fair-day activity at Longfold.

## Regional Trade Loop

The three villages should form one legible supply chain:

**Harrowfield grain, vegetables, and hemp + Vinebank wine and orchard goods + Broadmead wool, dairy goods, and horses -> Langston's scales, counting rooms, inns, and wagon bays -> House Gault's roads and the wider trade network.**

Recommended implementation principles:

- Give each village a commodity market only where the resource place supports it.
- Let maker tags and freight distance create price identity instead of scripting arbitrary local price bonuses.
- Add only the generic commodities **Wine** and **Hemp** after their weight, retail value, seasonal production, reach, storage target, market depth, and maker tiles are decided. Do not create regional, vintage, cask, bale, rope, or quality variants as additional market goods.
- Reuse existing commodities everywhere else.
- Keep ordinary equipment sales limited. These are producer villages, not alternate general-store hubs.
- Keep Langston's Hall as the primary regional contract board. A village authority may issue a focused local request, but should not reproduce the entire Hall interface.
- Credit official work to the existing House Gault Insignia page when it qualifies. Do not create village-specific standing systems without a demonstrated need.
- Preserve open trade where practical. House credentials should matter for official records, commissions, seized goods, and estate decisions rather than blocking a traveller from buying food.

## Development Guardrails

- Keep every village at four player-facing places unless a later quest demonstrates a real need for another permanent destination.
- Preserve two authority places in each village. Make the second one economically or defensively useful so it does not feel like a duplicate town hall.
- Do not add independent councils that contradict House Gault's centralized rule. Customary moots and collective petitions are compatible with appointed authority.
- Do not make every House Gault officer corrupt. Centralized rule should generate duty, competence, loyalty, fear, patronage, and abuse in different proportions.
- Do not make every tenant helpless or rebellious. Residents should make choices, organize, bargain, conceal, cooperate, and bear consequences.
- Do not turn every underworld contact into a professional criminal. Rural illegality often grows from survival, customary practice, private profit, or resistance to an unpopular demand.
- Name who orders a levy, collects a rent, issues a warrant, hides a load, pays a reward, or bears a loss. Avoid attributing consequential actions vaguely to "the village" or "the Hall."
- Let Langston remain the place where regional goods are aggregated and outward contracts are organized.
- Keep Edric's circuit consistent with his existing schedule and the Order's limited staffing.
- Reuse the existing market, trade, Insignia, horse, time, and settlement-event systems rather than introducing parallel mechanics.
- Before implementation, test first arrival, re-entry, market closure, trade, local quest resolution, refresh safety, and travel back to Langston.

## Decisions Before Implementation

1. Approve or replace the names Harrowfield, Vinebank, and Broadmead.
2. Fix their exact map positions, road branches, mileage, and travel times.
3. Decide whether each settlement needs a full overworld tile or is reached through an east-road regional menu.
4. Name the three appointed village heads, any visiting House wine steward, the Hall clerk who receives their reports, retainer officers, innkeepers, shrine keepers, and principal producers.
5. Define House Gault's tenancy, rent, labour, appeal, market-charter, and requisition rules closely enough for dialogue to remain consistent.
6. Decide which village markets are open on which weekdays and whether their schedules complement or compete with Langston's Marketday.
7. Approve the proposed generic Wine and Hemp commodities, including makers, prices, weights, seasonal curves, storage, and freight reach. Preserve one market row for each good.
8. Decide whether Vinebank has a cooper as scenery, a named NPC inside the Red Stave, or a future service without adding a fifth place.
9. Decide whether Broadmead sells player mounts and, if so, how it reuses the established horse-selection flow.
10. Choose one village quest or one shared regional quest as the first implementation target.
11. Define what official work counts toward House Gault standing and who is authorized to pay each reward.
12. Add lorebook coverage only after the names, authority model, and regional geography are approved.
