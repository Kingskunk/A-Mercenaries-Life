The up-to-four-place limit is a design constraint for each village. Two permanent player-facing places should normally be governmental or otherwise belong to House Gault's authority. The G.U.A.R.D.S. categories are functions to cover, not a requirement for six separate destinations.

## Regional Purpose

These villages should give visible origins to goods already passing through Langston and make the Western Vale feel like a governed agricultural region rather than empty fields surrounding one market town.

The strongest placement is to make them the three unnamed villages on Langston's east road already served by Edric Merrow from the True Sun temple. The road and its feeder lanes can support three different agricultural communities without turning each one into a miniature town:

- **Harrowfield**, nearest Langston, supplies grain, vegetables, and hemp.
- **Barolo**, on a beautiful sheltered rise farther east, supplies wine and orchard goods and is surrounded by vineyard manors and countryside homes.
- **Broadmead**, reached by a pasture lane near the road's eastern end, supplies wool, dairy goods, and horses.

Their goods move into Langston's covered scales and wagon bays. Langston remains the regional market, contract centre, and caravan assembly point rather than losing those functions to three new hubs.

## Existing Canon to Preserve

- The Countess of the Western Vale rules from Dunmow and appoints Langston's mayor.
- Langston Hall is House Gault's administrative hall. It handles petitions, disputes, market tolls, road levies, militia service, House Gault contracts, and the House Gault page of the Contractor's Insignia.
- Langston is a prosperous walled market town among open pasture and cultivated fields.
- Wool bales, small wine casks, bound hemp, horses, farm food, and other rural traffic already pass through Langston.
- Edric Merrow says he covers Langston's temple and three villages on the east road.
- The Western Vale road is maintained by House Gault and crosses level grassland and tilled fields between Langston and Fellgate.
- The current commodity system treats tagged commodities as regional goods bought by every open commodity market. Maker locations and freight distance establish local supply and price.
- Wool, cheese, butter, animal fat, grain, vegetables, eggs, poultry, orchard apples, honey, and beeswax already exist as commodities.
- Wine and hemp are visible in Langston's fiction but are not currently bulk commodities.
- Wine and hemp should each receive one generic market entry. Casks, vintages, bales, cordage, and local grades may appear in prose, but must not create additional commodity names or separate market rows.
- Hemp rope remains an individual adventuring item rather than an additional bulk commodity.
- Horses use the existing mount, stable, and saddlebags systems. They should not become stackable commodity inventory.

## The House Gault Model

These villages should feel materially different from settlements governed through the Gilded Scales.

Authority descends through a personal and landed hierarchy:

**The Countess at Dunmow -> the Mayor of Langston -> village heads -> local clerks, retainers, tenants, and labourers.**

Recommended characteristics:

- Each village has an appointed **village head** who handles daily administration and answers to the Mayor of Langston. The mayor appoints or confirms the village head under the Countess's seal.
- The first authority destination in every village is called **Village Head's Hall**. It combines the village head's residence, public office, record room, and petition space. Its architecture, records, and local business may differ, but the player-facing location name remains standardized for easier navigation.
- The village heads attend appointed meeting days at Langston Hall and may be summoned there during an emergency. They bring local registers, petitions, shortages, road concerns, militia returns, and disputes that exceed their authority.
- The mayor coordinates the three villages, hears serious appeals, issues local orders under the Countess's seal, and sends matters reserved to the Countess onward to Dunmow. Between meetings, village heads communicate with Langston Hall through clerks, sealed letters, and runners.
- House Gault's steward remains a senior estate officer at Dunmow, not another routine rank between the mayor and the village heads.
- Each village's second authority place is practical rather than monumental: a retainer lodge, tithe granary, bonded cellar, remount yard, or toll post.
- Markets operate on appointed days under a House Gault charter. The estate controls the official scales, seals, and measures.
- Rents may be owed in coin, produce, animals, or defined labour. Exact obligations should be settled before implementation rather than assuming every tenant is a serf.
- Retainers answer to House Gault rather than to an elected village council. They keep order, carry warrants, guard stores, escort rents, and organize militia service.
- Villagers may possess customary rights, testify in disputes, and assemble in a moot. Those practices give them a voice without making the village sovereign.
- The system should provide real benefits as well as costs: maintained roads, standard measures, protected stores, organized relief, escorts, and reserve supplies alongside tolls, levies, rents, and restricted trade rights.
- Ordinary buying and selling can remain open to travellers. Official work, estate petitions, and sealed contracts should use the existing House Gault Insignia page rather than creating a separate reputation system.

The rural underworld should grow out of attempts to evade or exploit this system. Untithed wine, concealed grain, altered livestock brands, poaching, forged toll slips, false weights, illegal grazing, and retainers selling confiscated goods fit better than an urban thieves' guild.

## G.U.A.R.D.S. Design Rule

For this plan, G.U.A.R.D.S. means:

- **G - Government:** where authority is encountered and decisions are recorded.
- **U - Underworld:** illicit activity, evasion, corruption, or informal opposition.
- **A - Altars:** local worship and the place served by Edric's circuit.
- **R - Resources:** what sustains the village and enters regional trade.
- **D - Defenses:** retainers, militia equipment, stores, walls, watches, or other safeguards.
- **S - Social:** where residents, travellers, and rumours gather.

The usual pattern covers all six functions through no more than four destinations:

1. A civil or estate authority place.
2. A second authority place with a defensive or economic role.
3. A combined resource and religious place.
4. A combined social and underworld place.

This shared use is intentional. A village should not possess six fully independent institutions merely to complete the framework. A feature with no independent interaction, such as Barolo's Common Press, remains background scenery rather than being promoted to a destination.

## Village One: Harrowfield

### Identity and Landscape

Harrowfield is the nearest of the three villages to Langston, provisionally four miles east along the maintained road. Broad strip fields of barley and mixed grain surround the settlement. Hemp grows in smaller plots, while onions, potatoes, cabbage, and other durable vegetables occupy household ground. Shallow retting ponds lie downwind and away from the wells. Bound hemp dries in the fields before travelling to Langston.

Harrowfield should feel closely tied to Langston. Carts can reach the town and return in a day, so its disputes concern weights, rents, storage, and access to the market more often than isolation.

### Four Places of Interest

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one.

Harrowfield's village head keeps the holding rolls, records transfers of land, hears boundary complaints, and announces labour and road obligations. A covered petition bench stands outside so residents can wait without entering the village head's private rooms. A customary village moot may offer testimony or a collective recommendation, but the village head states the ruling under House Gault's seal and answers for it at the next heads' meeting in Langston Hall.

#### 2. The Tithe Granary and Levy House

**Functions:** Defenses; government; resources; authority place two.

A heavy timber granary stands on stone pads behind a fenced yard. It stores rent grain, reserve seed, and emergency food. The attached levy house contains a clerk's desk, fire-fighting tools, spear racks, shields, and a room for a small retainer detail. A cistern and strict lamp rules protect the store from fire.

#### 3. Ropewalk Green

**Functions:** Resources; altar; daytime social activity.

A long ropewalk, grain scale, seasonal stalls, drying frames, and loading rails share the village green. A modest True Sun shrine stands beside the official scale. Edric can hold a brief service there during his circuit, while a local keeper maintains the lamp and reports serious needs to Langston's temple.

The ropewalk works hemp into cordage, but its ordinary output is local and regional equipment. The proposed bulk commodity remains simply **Hemp**. Finished hemp rope continues to use the existing individual item and does not create another commodity row.

#### 4. The Bound Sheaf

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Bound Sheaf is a small alehouse and lodging house used by carters waiting for a load or an early start. Its loft and rear sheds make it the natural place for untallied hemp, substituted sacks, false scale weights, or grain threshed at night before the village head's clerk can count it.

The proprietor should not automatically be the leader of every crime. They may tolerate some dealings to keep the house alive while refusing violence or the theft of reserve food.

### Trade Identity

Existing major candidates:

- Grain.
- Onions, potatoes, cabbage, and root-vegetable bundles.
- Farm eggs and dressed chicken.
- Barley loaves as a smaller worked good.

Proposed new major commodity:

- **Hemp.** Harrowfield is a major maker. Bales and loose fibre are descriptions of the same commodity, not separate goods.

Likely imports include salt, iron tools and bars, wool cloth, timber, and wine.

### G.U.A.R.D.S. Coverage

- **G:** Village Head's Hall; Tithe Granary clerk and House seal.
- **U:** Bound Sheaf's hidden stores, substitutions, and false weights.
- **A:** True Sun shrine on Ropewalk Green.
- **R:** Grain, vegetables, hemp, poultry, ropewalk, and granary.
- **D:** Retainers, levy equipment, fire tools, cistern, and protected stores.
- **S:** Bound Sheaf and market-day activity on Ropewalk Green.

## Village Two: Barolo

### Identity and Landscape

Barolo lies provisionally nine miles east of Langston where a low chalk rise gives the Vale one of its few pronounced slopes. Vineyards occupy the sheltered, sunward ground. Grain and grazing continue across the flatter land, while small orchards, hedges, flowered lanes, and beehives break the vines. From the higher ground, the open Vale stretches away beneath long rows of vines and scattered manor roofs. The beauty of the land is part of Barolo's identity and one reason wealthy people choose to keep homes there.

The village's wine should be good regional table wine: valuable enough to regulate and transport, but common enough to appear in small casks beneath Langston's market awnings. Vintage, quality, and origin may matter in dialogue and social standing, but the inventory and market must always aggregate it under the single commodity name **Wine**.

### The Manor Country and Three Families

Barolo is both a working village and the centre of a wider vineyard country. Several manors stand among the surrounding estates rather than forming one palace quarter inside the village. Nobles and rich merchants keep permanent or seasonal countryside homes there, with walled gardens, private presses, household stables, and views across the vines.

Three leading families own the three largest vineyard estates: the noble **House Veyre**, the merchant **Durnell family**, and the locally rooted **Avenel family**. None possesses public jurisdiction over Barolo. They petition, bargain, hire, lend, entertain, and exert pressure, but the village head and Mayor Anathram exercise House Gault's lawful authority.

#### House Veyre - Old Nobility

House Veyre is a lesser noble line sworn to House Gault. Its grant on Barolo's upper ridge predates the current Countess, and its oldest vines, stone cellars, water rights, and place at formal tables give it prestige beyond its present income. **Veyre Manor** occupies the highest of the three great estates, an old stone house above formal terraces and aging cellars.

**Lady Maelin Veyre** is the widowed head of the family. She treats the estate as a duty inherited from the dead and measures success through continuity, reputation, and the condition in which she leaves the land. She can be exacting and entitled, but she will spend money the family cannot comfortably spare to meet an obligation made under the Veyre name.

Her heir, **Lucan Veyre**, believes prestige without ready coin will eventually destroy the house. He wants larger contracts, new planting, and a useful marriage or business compact. Maelin fears that his plans would make Veyre dependent on the Durnells or turn an old noble house into another commercial concern.

House Veyre's strength is recognized obligation: its people expect the family to keep promises, shelter dependants, and defend old rights. Its weakness is that it is land-rich and increasingly coin-poor. Repairs, hospitality, old pensions, and ceremonial expectations consume income before the next harvest is sold.

#### The Durnell Family - Merchant Wealth

The Durnells made their fortune in Langston through wagon contracts, casks, rural credit, and the movement of agricultural goods. Two generations ago they bought neglected vineyard parcels and an indebted country house. They have since joined those holdings into **Durnell Manor**, the most modern estate in Barolo, with improved drains, a large cask yard, orderly accounts, and the greatest wine output of the three families.

**Master Cellan Durnell** heads the family. He pays wages promptly, invests in roads and equipment when they improve trade, and expects every favour to appear somewhere in an account. He wants the Countess to recognize the Durnells as landed gentry and believes a title would merely acknowledge the power and responsibility they already possess.

His daughter and intended successor, **Ressa Durnell**, is the estate's sharper commercial mind. She considers her father's pursuit of noble recognition expensive vanity. Ressa would rather control contracts, transport, and credit than imitate House Veyre's ceremonies.

The Durnells' strength is capital: they can survive a weak harvest, employ many labourers, and improve land other owners cannot afford to repair. Their weakness is consolidation. They buy debts, leases, and distressed parcels so aggressively that a fair contract can still leave a smaller grower with no land when it ends.

#### The Avenel Family - Local Gentry

The Avenels rose from Barolo's substantial growers rather than arriving with a title or outside fortune. Generations of marriage, careful purchases, service to House Gault, and agreements with neighbouring growers built a broad but scattered estate. **Avenel Manor** remains visibly descended from a prosperous farmhouse, enlarged around a working courtyard rather than rebuilt as a show house.

**Mistress Tressa Avenel** heads the family. She knows which slope drains first, which households share tools, who can be trusted with a late payment, and where every old footpath ran before the hedges grew over it. She supports the common press and presents the Avenels as defenders of Barolo's smaller growers.

Her nephew and likely successor, **Garran Avenel**, believes local affection has not earned the family enough formal respect. He wants a concentrated estate, a recognized place at Dunmow, and eventually a title. Tressa fears that pursuing those things would require treating neighbours as parcels to be acquired rather than people bound to the family.

The Avenels' strength is local trust and agricultural knowledge. Their weakness is that personal obligation can become nepotism or quiet coercion. They may forgive a debt without paper, but they remember who received the favour and expect loyalty when Barolo divides over an issue.

#### The Balance of Rivalry

The families compete over harvest size, reputation, labour, access to water and roads, contracts with Langston buyers, influence over the village head, invitations to Dunmow, and whose hospitality attracts the most important guests.

- Veyre and Durnell contest whether birth or wealth deserves precedence.
- Durnell and Avenel contest the purchase of debts, leases, and small vineyard parcels.
- Avenel and Veyre contest old water, path, and grazing rights that were never recorded as clearly as either claims.
- Any two families may cooperate against the third when their interests align. No alliance is permanent, and no family should function as Barolo's fixed heroic or villainous faction.

All three families produce the same generic **Wine** commodity. Estate names, vintages, cask marks, and claims of superior quality may matter in prose, evidence, and social standing, but never create additional market rows or inventory goods.

The surrounding manors do not count as additional permanent hub destinations. **Veyre Manor**, **Durnell Manor**, and **Avenel Manor** remain visible regional scenery beyond Barolo's four-place hub.

### Player Discovery of the Manor Country

The material above establishes author background; it is not knowledge granted to the player on arriving in Barolo. The player initially encounters a handsome vineyard country: three differently worked roads, terrace walls, distant manor roofs, carts, labourers, estate gates, and marked casks. That establishes that there are large neighbouring holdings, not who owns them or how their households stand with one another.

The Vineyard Roads should initially use only descriptive route labels:

- **The upper terrace road** toward an older stone house above the vines.
- **The broad cart lane** toward a busy, recently improved estate yard.
- **The lower vineyard lane** through smaller holdings and working courtyards.

A family name becomes known only when the player encounters it in the world: on an estate gate, a cask seal, a posted notice, a spoken introduction, or another clear in-world source. A named family member becomes known only after that person is met or identified by a person or document the player has encountered. The route label may then change to reflect that discovery; it must not use **Veyre**, **Durnell**, or **Avenel** in advance.

Each estate's history, finances, internal disagreements, and rivalry must emerge gradually through what the player can see, hear, read, or learn over time. Do not present the three family biographies, their rivalries, or a lorebook entry as arrival narration. Any future lorebook entry for an estate or family must be individually gated by the specific discovery that establishes it.

### Background Scenery: The Common Press and Cask Gate

Small growers bring grapes to the communal press rather than each maintaining expensive equipment. It is a broad stone-floored workhouse opening onto a cart yard, with crushing tables, heavy screw presses, covered vats, drainage channels, scales, and numbered storage bays. During harvest, carts queue outside while growers, hired hands, carters, and press workers argue over weight, turn, spoilage, and fees.

The communal press gives Barolo's smaller growers a practical counterweight to the three great estates, whose private presses and storage would otherwise let them dominate every part of production. It is visible in Barolo's descriptions, but it is not a selectable hub destination, menu option, or permanent point of interest.

At the roadward edge of Barolo, the **Cask Gate** is a retainer lodge and toll arch beside a bonded cellar. House Gault retainers inspect departing casks, verify the estate seal, and keep seized goods under lock. The gate controls legal wagon traffic without making Barolo physically walled; footpaths and vineyard lanes remain harder to watch. Its retainers serve House Gault, not the surrounding manors, and cannot enforce one family's private rivalry as though it were public law.

The Cask Gate is visible authority scenery rather than a selectable destination. Ordinary business at the gate is reflected through the Village Head's Hall and Barolo Market instead.

Barolo has no permanent chapel, temple, or altar. Religious practice does not receive a separate player-facing location here.

### Player-Facing Places

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one; local Contract Board.

Barolo's village head keeps vineyard leases, inheritance records, harvest books, and the local register of approved cask marks. The village head hears ordinary disputes over boundary vines, access paths, water, neglected plots, and the division of a vintage owed as rent. The mayor's clerks or a House wine steward may audit those records, while the mayor hears serious appeals at Langston Hall. The three leading families may pressure, flatter, or petition the village head, but none of them lawfully replaces House Gault's authority.

Barolo's **Contract Board** hangs inside the public chamber, on the wall between the entrance and the locked record-room door. A shallow wooden canopy and two hinged shutters protect the papers when the hall is being cleaned or closed. House Gault's painted shield stands above the board, while the village head's clerk keeps the posting book at a sloped desk beside it.

The board is divided into three clearly labelled sections:

- **House and Village Contracts:** Local work accepted and countersealed by the village head under House Gault's authority. Each notice names the patron, objective, term, payment, and person authorized to confirm completion. A posting states explicitly whether successful completion is credited to the player's House Gault Insignia page.
- **Private Contracts:** Work offered by House Veyre, the Durnell family, the Avenel family, smaller growers, merchants, or households. Each notice bears the patron's own mark and names who pays. Private work grants no House Gault standing unless the village head has separately countersealed it as official work.
- **Public Notices:** Official informational notices and orders sent from Mayor Anathram. These notices are not automatically paid jobs.

The Contract Board is an option within Village Head's Hall, not a separate place of interest. It carries locally scoped postings. Langston Hall remains the board for regional contracts involving the wider Vale or more than one settlement.

#### 2. Barolo Market

**Functions:** Resources; trade; government; daytime social activity; authority place two.

Barolo Market occupies the broad paved yard where the village lane joins the road from the Cask Gate. Tiled canopies shelter produce tables, sample casks, baskets of orchard fruit, hives' wax, and carters' small lots. A raised stone scale platform stands at its centre beneath a House Gault canopy, with a market clerk recording official weights, fees, and disputes from a shuttered counter.

The market is Barolo's trading-simulation point of interest. It uses the shared commodity market system, with **Wine** as the village's major good and its existing secondary goods available through the same market. Estate names, vintages, cask marks, and family claims of quality can colour the lot descriptions and conversations, but never produce separate market rows.

The official scale and market clerk make this a House Gault authority place as well as a market. The clerk can refer a contested weight, fee, or seal to the village head; serious cases go onward to Mayor Anathram. The three leading families may dominate supply, but no family owns the market or writes its official measures.


#### 3. The Red Stave

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Red Stave is a substantial coaching inn and wine house built for the traffic created by Barolo's vineyards and country manors. A broad two-storey stone-and-timber house faces the road behind a covered carriage arch. The arch opens into a cobbled stable court with stalls, carriage sheds, a pump, ostlers' rooms, and covered storage for travellers' loads. Inside, a large common hall is joined by several private dining rooms, an upstairs gallery of guest chambers, and deep wine cellars beneath the rear wing.

Visiting merchants, wine buyers, estate agents, minor nobles without a Barolo residence, household guests travelling ahead of their hosts, and officials from Langston can all lodge there. Harvest workers and carters fill the common hall, while wealthier guests hire private rooms. The great families normally sleep at their own manors, but their agents, relatives, workers, and rivals use the Red Stave as nominally neutral ground.

The inn's cellars and private rooms also support Barolo's underworld. Untithed casks may acquire copied seals, weak wine may receive a prestigious name, and discreet buyers ask which loads will avoid the Cask Gate.

The room should also be the village's honest social centre: harvest meals, marriage cups, seasonal hiring, and growers comparing weather. Illicit dealings occupy part of its life rather than defining every person inside.

#### 4. The Vineyard Roads

**Functions:** Regional exploration hub; access to the vineyard country.

The Vineyard Roads is one player-facing hub for the lanes, terrace paths, estate walls, workers' tracks, and vineyard gates surrounding Barolo. It prevents every vineyard and manor from becoming a separate choice on the main village hub while still letting the player travel out among the vines.

From here, the player can take the upper terrace road, broad cart lane, or lower vineyard lane. These routes lead into vineyard country and may later provide access to a vineyard or estate gate when that access is actually available. Seeing or visiting a vineyard does not automatically grant entry to a manor house; a manor interior remains separate from the road hub and should only exist if later approved.

### Trade Identity

Proposed new major commodity:

- **Wine.** Barolo is a major maker and Langston is its first aggregation market. The three leading estates, smaller growers, casks, and vintages all feed the same commodity entry rather than producing separate goods.

Existing secondary candidates:

- Orchard apples.
- Honey and beeswax from vineyard-edge hives.
- Limited grain or cheese from mixed holdings.

Likely imports include timber and iron for casks and hoops, salt and preserved food, wool cloth, and tools.


## Village Three: Broadmead

### Identity and Landscape
    
Broadmead's best horses are not ordinary market goods. Some belong directly to the Countess, while others are raised by tenants under breeding agreements or purchased under House right of first offer. Wool and dairy goods provide the steadier village economy.

### Underlying Tension: Paper Against Custom (decided 2026-11-02)

Broadmead's tension is deliberately not Barolo's. Barolo has three ranked households and their heirs. Broadmead is divided by livelihood and by a rule, with the grass as the stake. There is no family triad and no heir with a private ambition.

- **The agreements.** House Gault lends mares and stud rights to tenant holdings. The tenant raises the foals, and the Remount Yard has first offer on each at a fixed price. The agreement also carries the right to the Home Meadow for summer grazing and the sheltered winter pasture. About eight or nine ordinary households hold them. They are not a faction. They share a stallion, help each other through foaling nights, and mostly get along. Their friction is small: whose mare was covered first, a foal that turned out too good.
- **The other side.** The wool and dairy households are roughly three times as many. They have grazed the lower commons longer than the agreements have existed, but they hold no paper. Each time the Home Meadow is fenced for the horses, their flocks lose ground. They resent it, and they also depend on the horse people (the stallion's fees, the yard's custom, the road).
- **The village head** keeps the brand and grazing registers and cannot favour paper or custom openly. The head should be nothing like Barolo's Hobart Thring: a loud, weathered former drover, not a slow clerk.
- **The Crooked Halter's root.** One agreement-holder has quietly sold a good foal past the yard because the fixed price would not cover a debt. The plan's false brands and stolen breeding papers grow from that, not from a plain villain.
- **Naming rule.** The agreement-holders stay nameless for now. They are known by their holding ("the holding at the ford") or job, and a name is given only when a place or conversation earns it.
- **Tone.** Blunt and practical, with weather and animals in the talk, where Barolo is polite and status-minded. Conversations are about which side of the fence a person stands on.

### Four Places of Interest

#### 1. Village Head's Hall

**Functions:** Government; authority place one.

Broadmead's village head records livestock brands, breeding claims, grazing rights, winter pasture, strays, and damage done when animals cross a boundary. An enclosed petition yard lets residents bring disputed animals before the village head. The mayor's clerks may audit the register, and the mayor hears serious appeals at Langston Hall.

#### 2. The Countess's Remount Yard

**Functions:** Defenses; government; resources; authority place two.

A retainer lodge, stable, smithy bay, tack room, and militia muster field serve House Gault's mounted retainers. A mounted sergeant manages the Countess's horses, inspects animals offered under contract, and carries sealed authority to requisition mounts during a genuine military need.

Any player horse purchase here should reuse or deliberately extend the existing mount-selection and ownership system. Horses must not be implemented as stackable commodities.

**Decided 2026-11-02: the yard is barred to the player for now.** It is a military encampment, and the Countess's horses are not sold to strangers. A stone wall and an iron-barred gate (invented) stand between the lane and the yard; two troopers guard the gate at every hour of the day and night, and turn a stranger away in the street layer. The player sees the lodge, stables, smithy bay, tack room and muster field only through the bars. The user will think of a hook later that lets the player buy a horse here.

*Dormant, kept for that hook:* Sergeant Dagny Marren (human woman, forty-odd, ex-rider, calm and level, scar through her left eyebrow, rein-lines on both palms; eleven years riding for the Countess until a fall on the ice at Coombe Gap) and her horse sale are still written (`bm_ry_sgt`, `bm_ry_sgt_menu` in `remount_yard.txt`, and `horse_place "bm"` in `horse_dealer.txt`: a retired cull at 34 silver marks, fixed price, buy-back 15), but nothing reaches them. Olof's talk about her at the Crooked Halter is unchanged. The yard's guard is small and unfortified: order comes from habit and the Countess's name, and its numbers are deliberately not stated beyond the two at the gate.

**Decided 2026-11-02 after a roleplay test of the gate:** the village lane button now reads "The Remount Yard Gate"; the guard says a stranger needs a sealed writ from the Hall in Langston or the sergeant's own word, and points buyers to the village; Olof says the same when asked, and names the Fair's horse line. The writ is a hook for later (idea: the Hall clerk issues it at Vouched). **Where the player buys a horse instead:** Longfold Fair's horse line, Marketday only, Morning through Afternoon, through the shared dealer flow (`horse_place "bf"`). The seller is drawn from the day's five-day seed. The horse is one the yard looked at and passed over, with a small holding brand (no family or holding named). Price is 32 silver marks, or 29 on a good bargain; the buy-back is 15, as everywhere.

#### 3. Longfold Fair and the Sun Pillar

**Functions:** Resources; trade; altar; daytime social activity. (Widened 2026-11-02 from a small fair ground into Broadmead's trading-simulator market. It has no government function, since Broadmead already has two authority places.)

Permanent sheep rails, weighing pens, shearing sheds, dairy stalls, and a long watering trough surround the market ground. A covered wool hall, where fleeces are graded and weighed, and a cheese and butter hall with a cool undercroft stand beside them. A weathered solar pillar at the edge of the ground serves as the village shrine. Edric can conduct rites outdoors in fair weather or beneath the shearing shed in rain.

Longfold Fair is Broadmead's trading-simulation point of interest, in the same way Barolo Market is Barolo's. It uses the shared commodity market system. **Wool, Cheese, and Butter** are the major goods; animal fat, raw hides, and eggs or dressed chicken are the minor goods. Horses stay out of the commodity system and are handled at the Countess's Remount Yard. Sheep and other livestock are scenery, lot descriptions, and rumour, never market rows.

The fair is also the district's collection point, so carters and drovers from outside bring grain, salt, ironwork, timber, hemp, wine, and finished cloth. That makes it a place to sell and to buy, and it should not be only a producer's stall. Keep ordinary equipment sales limited, as in the other villages.

The season sets the ground's rhythm. Late spring is shearing and fleece-grading, summer is cheese-making, and autumn is the cull and the drove before winter pasture. Marketday is the busiest day. A quiet day shows a few pens and stalls.

A market warden or weigh-master keeps order and the weights. The warden is not a House Gault officer and holds no separate authority role, and a contested weight or brand is referred to the Village Head's Hall. Agreement-holders and custom grazers both sell here, and it is the one ground where they trade side by side. That tension is shown by who stands where and not through a staged conflict.

#### 4. The Crooked Halter

**Functions:** Social hub; underworld.

The Crooked Halter is a drovers' inn with a stable yard and a private back room used for livestock bargains. False brands, stolen breeding papers, unlicensed grazing, concealed animals, and suspiciously cheap horses form its underworld.

The innkeeper should know the difference between a tenant hiding one ewe from a ruinous count and an organized horse thief. Their loyalties can depend on the harm being done rather than on a blanket commitment to either law or crime.

### Visual Direction (draft 2026-11-02, for scenery prompts)

## Regional Trade Loop

The three villages should form one legible supply chain:

**Harrowfield grain, vegetables, and hemp + Barolo wine and orchard goods + Broadmead wool, dairy goods, and horses -> Langston's scales, counting rooms, inns, and wagon bays -> House Gault's roads and the wider trade network.**

Recommended implementation principles:

- Give each village a commodity market only where the resource place supports it.
- Let maker tags and freight distance create price identity instead of scripting arbitrary local price bonuses.
- Add only the generic commodities **Wine** and **Hemp** after their weight, retail value, seasonal production, reach, storage target, market depth, and maker tiles are decided. Do not create regional, vintage, cask, bale, rope, or quality variants as additional market goods.
- Reuse existing commodities everywhere else.
- Keep ordinary equipment sales limited. These are producer villages, not alternate general-store hubs.
- Keep Langston Hall as the primary regional Contract Board. Barolo's Village Head's Hall contains a smaller local Contract Board for Barolo-scale work; it should reuse the established board and House Gault Insignia conventions without reproducing Langston's full regional contract selection.
- Credit official work to the existing House Gault Insignia page when it qualifies. Do not create village-specific standing systems without a demonstrated need.
- Preserve open trade where practical. House credentials should matter for official records, commissions, seized goods, and estate decisions rather than blocking a traveller from buying food.

## Development Guardrails

- Keep every village at no more than four player-facing places. Background scenery does not become a permanent destination unless it gains a distinct recurring interaction.
- Preserve two authority places in each village. Make the second one economically or defensively useful so it does not feel like a duplicate town hall.
- Do not add independent councils that contradict House Gault's centralized rule. Customary moots and collective petitions are compatible with appointed authority.
- Do not make every House Gault officer corrupt. Centralized rule should generate duty, competence, loyalty, fear, patronage, and abuse in different proportions.
- Do not make every tenant helpless or rebellious. Residents should make choices, organize, bargain, conceal, cooperate, and bear consequences.
- Do not turn every underworld contact into a professional criminal. Rural illegality often grows from survival, customary practice, private profit, or resistance to an unpopular demand.
- Name who orders a levy, collects a rent, issues a warrant, hides a load, pays a reward, or bears a loss. Avoid attributing consequential actions vaguely to "the village" or "the Hall."
- Let Langston remain the place where regional goods are aggregated and outward contracts are organized.
- Keep Edric's circuit consistent with his existing schedule and the Order's limited staffing.
- Reuse the existing market, trade, Insignia, horse, time, and settlement-event systems rather than introducing parallel mechanics.
- Before implementation, test first arrival, re-entry, market closure, trade, approved local content, refresh safety, and travel back to Langston.
