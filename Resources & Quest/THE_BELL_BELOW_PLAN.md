# The Bell Below — Redvein Quest Plan

## Status

Planning document only. This is a five-room adventure structure, not five literal chambers or an implemented quest. Creature identity, combat statistics, exact checks, reward, and trigger conditions remain to be decided before writing the scene.

## Quest Identity

- **Origin:** Redvein's Eastcut mine-gate yard.
- **Form:** An urgent mine rescue with combat at its opening and climax, and a social decision that changes the rescue.
- **Core objective:** Bring the missing lower-shift miners out alive.
- **Core pressure:** The player can drive back the creatures and still lose people if the rescue line is not ready in time.
- **Theme:** The village's work depends on people trusting one another when the ground becomes unsafe.

The five rooms are *functions in one adventure*: entrance and guardian, roleplay challenge, setback, climax, and reward or revelation. Most of the action happens around the same working mine, with the aftermath at the Gather House and Red Lantern. There is no requirement for five mapped rooms, a dungeon crawl, or a new explorable mine hub.

## Existing Redvein Foundation

- The Eastcut Market occupies the mine-gate yard. Its ore tables, scale platform, timbered mine mouths, gate bell, and wheel over a sheltered shaft are already in the scene.
- The crews handle safety below ground. Two Scales road wardens watch the strongbox, scale, gate, and loaded wagons; they do not command the mine or replace its rescue crew.
- Mara Venn keeps the Gather House's notices, lamps, blankets, and household relief collection. She can organize help and record the aftermath without becoming the mine's owner or commander.
- Ilyra Fen runs the Red Lantern. Its warm room and stable yard make it a credible place for survivors and families to gather after the rescue.
- Redvein retains its own local council. Alderford's Factor handles outward contracts and disputed trade agreements, not every decision made at the mine.

The threat is provisionally **creatures from a newly broken natural seam**. They are animals defending an occupied space, not evidence of sabotage, a curse, or a secret faction. Their precise species and abilities are open design choices; the scene should reveal them through what the player sees rather than naming them in advance.

## Discovery and Commitment

The hook comes from the Eastcut's visible work, not a posted monster contract or an NPC recognizing the player as a mercenary. During an active shift, the bell above the gate rings out of pattern. Workers run toward the mine mouth; an injured miner emerges with a creature following. The player can intervene in the immediate emergency or withdraw. If they withdraw, the village handles it at a cost and the quest receives a genuine losing outcome rather than waiting indefinitely for the same alarm.

After the yard is safe, a lower-shift worker reports that others remain below. The player chooses whether to join the rescue. This is the explicit commitment to the descent; fighting the first creature does not silently volunteer them for the rest of the quest. The hook's exact eligibility and timing should be chosen during implementation so the alarm does not interrupt an unrelated market transaction or appear when a rescue cannot be played fairly.

## Five-Room Structure

### Room 1 — The Bell at the Gate

**Function: Entrance and guardian.**

An injured miner staggers into the Eastcut yard while smaller creatures pursue. The player fights near the sorting tables as workers pull the injured clear. Carts, timber frames, and the scale give the opening fight a distinct setting; the wardens protect the crowd and market money while the mine crew responds to the danger below.

The fight establishes that the threat is physical and immediate. It also offers the first evidence of the new seam: fresh stone dust on the miner, an unfamiliar claw mark, or a broken piece of support timber. These are practical warnings, not a separate investigation. A player who loses or flees cannot receive the full-rescue result.

### Room 2 — Who Goes Back Down

**Function: Roleplay challenge and preparation.**

The surviving miners know the lower-shift crew's usual route, but disagree about going back after the first attack. One experienced worker can guide the rescue; another fears that sending more people into an unstable cut will only add casualties. The player hears both concerns and chooses a concrete safety plan rather than making a generic speech about bravery.

The player can secure help through honest reassurance, a workable rescue plan, or by accepting the danger of going with less support. A failed appeal or broken promise closes off that person's help; it does not strand the quest or offer the same request again under a different label.

The crew has time to prioritize one preparation: a sound hauling line, timber for bracing, or extra covered lamps. Each should have a clear use in Room 3 or Room 4. The player may still proceed without ideal preparation, but that choice carries a rescue cost. This beat gives the social decision mechanical weight without letting conversation erase the planned combat.

### Room 3 — The Way Back Closes

**Function: Setback.**

The rescue party finds the first survivors and begins moving them toward the surface. They learn that at least one miner is still at the lower landing. A section of the walking passage then shifts and drops. The injured survivors cannot be carried back through it; the wheel and shaft lift are now the viable way out.

The movement of the ground is foreshadowed by the damaged support and falling grit in the earlier beats. Preparation can limit its cost, but cannot cancel the setback or turn it into a surprise punishment for a successful player. The guide's condition, the surviving miners' ability to help, and the chosen equipment now determine how quickly the lift can be made ready.

The player's next decision is whether to continue for the last miner or order a retreat with those already found. Retreat preserves whoever is currently safe but ends the quest with a named partial or failed rescue. Continuing leads to the climax.

### Room 4 — Hold the Lower Landing

**Function: Climactic battle and rescue.**

The larger creature and any remaining young or smaller creatures press toward the shaft while the crew rigs the lift. The player must hold the approach long enough to get the last miner and the injured survivors out. The terrain matters: ore carts and supports provide cover, but the hauling line and workers cannot be left undefended.

The objective is **evacuation under attack**, not simply reducing every enemy to zero health. A successful fight with a failed lift can still lose a miner. Conversely, an effective rescue may let the crew withdraw once the people are clear without slaughtering animals that are no longer pursuing them. The final encounter should be tuned for the player's likely level and available allies when the creature type is selected.

Room 2's decisions must pay off visibly here. A guide might steady the crew or warn of a dangerous approach; a sound line might speed the lift; braces might hold the landing; lamps might prevent a blind approach. Each benefit should change a specific risk or option, not grant a vague bonus that the player cannot see.

### Room 5 — Names at the Gather House

**Function: Reward, revelation, and aftermath.**

At the Gather House, Mara records who came back while Ilyra makes room for the wounded and their families at the Red Lantern. Survivors describe the new break in the rock: the mine reached a space the creatures already occupied. The workers' account, the player's testimony, and the casualty list inform the village's decision about closing or securing that part of the Eastcut.

The player may speak plainly about the danger, minimize it to favor a quick reopening, or decline to give an opinion. Redvein's people and council make the final mine decision. The result should change later yard and village prose: who is present, whether the lower cut is closed, and how Mara and the families receive the player.

Any payment should come from an appropriate mine or village source, not automatically from Mara's household relief box. Exact coin, items, reputation effects, and any follow-up access remain open until the full quest and economy are designed.

## Outcome Contract

| Outcome | What it means | Continuing world state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full rescue** | The last miner and the earlier survivors reach the surface. | The dangerous cut is addressed; the village remembers the player's help. |
| **Costly rescue** | The threat is driven back, but a miner is lost or left behind because the evacuation failed. | The Gather House records the loss; the mine's reopening is more contentious. |
| **Retreat** | The player leaves with those already safe instead of risking the lower landing. | The missing miner is not quietly saved offscreen; the choice has an explicit aftermath. |
| **Defeat or withdrawal at the gate** | The player cannot lead the rescue. | The village responds without them and suffers a defined loss. |

These are narrative outcomes, not a promise that every branch awards quest-completion credit. A successful completion flag should be set only for the outcome selected as successful when implementation defines the progression rule. All outcomes need one-time resolution and reward guards so refreshing a page cannot repeat casualties, time, combat, payment, or standing changes.

## Design Guardrails

- Keep the quest centered on combat and rescue. Physical evidence explains the danger; it should not expand into a long investigation or a hidden-saboteur reveal.
- Give the player a clear chance to help, decline, retreat, or fail. Do not award a full rescue after the last miner is abandoned or the decisive fight is lost.
- Let failed social approaches cost support or preparation, then move forward. Do not block the quest behind a single persuasion roll.
- Make the mine crew competent in its own work. The player's value is fighting and making hard choices under pressure, not teaching miners how a mine operates.
- Keep the wardens' market-security role, Mara's relief role, Ilyra's hospitality, and the council's local authority distinct.
- Use the existing combat and time systems when this is implemented. Set exact opponents, checks, timing, rewards, and eligibility only after checking the likely player level and testing the complete route.

## Decisions Before Implementation

1. Choose the creature species and establish what the broken seam and its inhabitants look and sound like.
2. Decide when the alarm can trigger and what nearby activity must be paused or completed first.
3. Set the number of missing miners and define exactly which outcomes count as full, costly, or failed rescue.
4. Decide which preparation can affect which rescue risk, and how the climax tracks evacuation alongside combat.
5. Set a proportionate reward source and post-quest changes to the Eastcut, Gather House, and Red Lantern.
