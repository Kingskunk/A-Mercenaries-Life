# The Hale Ring

*Implemented as a one-time overworld discovery and tracked quest.*

## Premise

On a quiet stretch of the River Road, where low stone rises beside the verge, a blue-and-yellow bird flits from thorn to thorn with something bright clamped in its beak. It makes for a narrow nest-hole in the rock, strikes the stone with one wing, and drops its prize into the loose gravel below.

Half buried among the pale chips is a small gold ring. The band is worn smooth on the outside and carries the initials **R. H.** inside. A single diamond sits in a plain old setting: small, but clean and sharply cut.

The ring is not magical. It is a personal thing, old enough to have been carried often, and valuable enough that it does not belong forgotten beneath a bird's nest.

If the player examines it closely and succeeds on a difficult check (roughly DC 16), they notice that the diamond's cut is unusually deliberate: narrow, bright facets arranged in a pattern that is not common in ordinary market jewellery. This is a clue, not a gate. The player can still return the ring without passing the check.

## Following the Clue

The player can simply keep the ring, sell it to Angel at the Open Hand in Dredge-End, or ask after its owner.

At the Bright Setting in Port Valen, Cassia Rell turns the ring over beneath her glass and studies the stone before looking at the initials.

> “That setting is mine,” she says. “Or it was, some years back. The band was brought to me as two pieces of old family gold. I melted them together and reset the stone.”

She taps the inner edge with one clean fingernail.

> “Ressa Hale's. Keeper of the Split Pine at Timbermouth. She and her brother had the work done after their parents died. It was meant to keep them both close while she was on the road.”

Cassia does not demand the ring or make the discovery into a grand mystery. She gives the player Ressa's name because the work is distinctive and because the initials leave little room for doubt.

If the player already knows Ressa, the initials alone are enough reason to take it to Timbermouth. Cassia merely confirms it.

Oswin Hale, at the Wagoner's Rest in Port Valen, can also recognize the ring if shown it. He knows the shallow seam along the inside of the band and falls quiet when he sees it.

> “Ressa lost that before she took the Split Pine,” he says. “Last run we made together, I think. She wore it on a cord when she drove.”

He does not take it from the player.

> “Give it to her yourself. She ought to be the one to see it come back.”

## Returning It to Ressa

At the Split Pine, the player shows Ressa the ring. She does not grab for it. First she reads the initials; then she turns the band until she finds the old join in the gold.

> “I thought that was gone on the River Road,” she says. “The cord broke somewhere north of here. I looked until the light failed, then had a wagon waiting and a load that would not wait with me.”

She rubs her thumb across the seam.

> “My father wore one piece. My mother wore the other. Oswin and I had Cassia make this after they were gone. Nothing grand. Just something I could carry.”

There is a pause before she looks up again.

> “Thank you. Truly.”

The moment should stay modest. Ressa is grateful, but this is not a hidden political secret or the beginning of a long errand chain. It is a small recovered piece of a person's life.

## Possible Resolutions

### Return it freely

Ressa accepts it with uncomplicated gratitude. From then on, the player can take a room at the Split Pine for **2 copper bits** instead of the normal 4, provided a room is available.

> “For you, two copper for a room,” Ressa says. “So long as I have a bed empty.”

This is the warmest outcome: a permanent but bounded gesture of hospitality, not unlimited free lodging.

### Ask for payment

Ressa pays **2 gold crowns** for its return. It is a serious, practical sacrifice for an innkeeper, rather than the full value a jeweller could expect from the gold and stone. She is not hostile, but the exchange becomes businesslike rather than personal.

> “Fair enough,” she says, setting the coins down. “You found it. I am glad you brought it here.”

The player still resolves the quest and keeps Ressa as a usable contact, but does not receive the more personal inn favour.

### Sell it before identifying the owner

The player can sell the ring to Angel at the Open Hand on Lamp Stair in Dredge-End for **4 gold crowns and 2 silver marks**. This closes the return route. She values the gold and stone, not its history; Cassia identifies the owner but will not buy the ring.

There should be no trick punishment for this choice. It is simply a choice between immediate coin and returning a lost keepsake.

## Where It Can Trigger

This should be a rare overworld discovery on the old freight route between Port Valen and Timbermouth: the route Ressa and Oswin once drove together.

- **The River Road:** the strongest fit. The road already follows stone cuttings above the Grey, giving the bird a believable rocky nest-site.
- **Ashrun Mouth:** a second fit, near the grey-stone bar where the Ashrun meets the Grey. The nest can be in the rough bank above the water, with the ring dropped into dry gravel rather than near the ford itself.
- **Longshade Edge:** a third fit, on the broken, wooded-hill stretch before Timbermouth. Here the nest can be in a low exposed stone face beside the road.

It should not trigger in settlements, on the High Grey Road, or in the Crossfield country. Those places are not part of the route that explains how Ressa lost it, and placing it everywhere would make the keepsake feel generic.

When implemented, give the quest a small chance to occur while travelling through any of those three road nodes in daylight or at dusk, then permanently remove it from the encounter pool once the ring has been found. It is one discovery with several possible locations, not three separate rings or three chances to repeat it.

## Design Boundaries for Later Implementation

- The unusual-cut check is optional flavour and a shortcut clue, never a requirement to complete the quest.
- Cassia is the clearest identifier; Oswin is an optional, more personal confirmation.
- The quest should not require three separate errands or a scavenger hunt.
- The ring should be a unique, non-repeatable quest item rather than ordinary wearable jewellery.
- No combat is required. The bird and rocky outcrop are the encounter's whole discovery scene.
- The discovery can be a rare overworld event on a road with rocky verges, with a permanent completion lock once the ring is found, sold, or returned.
