Two long benches face the table for folk waiting to settle a claim or register a brand. "How does a place out on the down like Oxworth make its living?" you ask.

"Grass, salt, and bone-grease," Bram says without blinking. "Land up here's too thin and windy for heavy wheat like Langston grows down in the low fields. What it grows fine is rough turf. Herds come off the outer downs into our pens, we brine beef in casks for Hartwell's carts, render tallow, and send cord-bound hides north toward Harwick's tanners."

"Sounds like steady work."

"Steady enough when the winter holds mild. Bad frost or a wet spring, every penny hangs on whether the salt shed stays dry."
*goto ox_hl_talk_menu

*label ox_hl_talk_post
*set ox_hl_asked_post true
*set minutes_to_pass 5
*set time_advance_call_id "ox_hl_talk_post"
*gosub_scene calendar advance_time
"How did you end up behind this counter?" you ask.

Bram lifts his left forearm slightly so the stiff leather brace catches the light. "Held forty head of my own on the south ridge for twenty years. Four autumns back, a red bull pinned me against a hurdles rail in the dark and cracked this wrist in three places. Healed stiff enough I couldn't haul salt barrels or hold a wet rope."

He shrugs one wide shoulder. "Old head retired down to Esthorpe to live with his son. Hartwell's steward asked the village who could add a column straight and tell a true horn-notch from a forged one. My nephew took over my herd lease, and I took the chair."
*goto ox_hl_talk_menu

*label ox_hl_talk_herds
*set ox_hl_asked_herds true
*set minutes_to_pass 5
*set time_advance_call_id "ox_hl_talk_herds"
*gosub_scene calendar advance_time
"What gives you the most grief in this seat?" you ask.

"Hedges," Bram says right away. "Or the lack of 'em. Folk down the slope from the village sow barley and turnips on small plots. Every spring and autumn, when big herds move along the lane toward Hartwell or Carrowmark, hungry steers push through half-mended turf banks into somebody's green crop."

He taps the ash stick on the table. "Half the mornings in autumn I've got a grain farmer swearing his winter meal got eaten and a herder swearing the fence was rotted before his cattle smelled it. My job is walking out with a measuring stick, counting hoof prints, and naming a fair coin price before they settle it with clubs."
*goto ox_hl_talk_menu

*label ox_hl_talk_hartwell
*set ox_hl_asked_hartwell true
*set minutes_to_pass 5
*set time_advance_call_id "ox_hl_talk_hartwell"
*gosub_scene calendar advance_time
"What's the road like toward Hartwell, and how do they treat Oxworth?" you ask.

"Six miles east-southeast, hard chalk most of the way, easy walking unless wind brings sleet off the high ground," Bram says. "You'll also find roads branching out to Esthorpe, Millstow, Broadmead, and north for Carrowmark. As for Hartwell, we sit under the Viscounty's eye. They want their market dues on every cart passing their gate, and their tax men count our salt casks twice."

He nods across the room at the pinned notices. "Folk here pay what's owed and grumble over ale at the Yoke & Horn across the lane. If you're looking for paid road errands, check the board behind you. Hartwell's counting-house still posts courier slips out here when they want legs moving faster than salt carts."
*goto ox_hl_talk_menu
