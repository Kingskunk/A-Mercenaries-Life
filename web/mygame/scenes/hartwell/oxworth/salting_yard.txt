*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ──
*label ox_yd_place
*if (yd_first)
  A long stone salting shed with moss-patched slates and three red brick chimneys faces out across churned mud and open downland grass. Along its front wall runs a deep timber lean-to packed with iron-hooped oak salt casks and heavy brine tubs, while rough wooden racks nearby hold scraped cattle hides pegged tight to cure in the wind. Flanking the yard are split-rail holding pens with slatted gates and low stone stock troughs, scarred by horns and wagon hubs. The air hangs thick with cold brine, woodsmoke, raw hide, damp lime, and trampled earth.
*else
  The salting yard stands as before: the stone shed and its open cask lean-to, the timber hide frames in front, and the split-rail cattle pens bordering the open turf.
*return

"Where do all these casks and hides end up once they leave the yard?" you ask.

"Salt beef and tallow tubs ride south-east into Hartwell's storehouses before cold weather hits," the foreman says, thumbing toward the stacked barrels. "Raw hides go north toward Harwick's tanneries, stretched dry on these frames first so they don't rot on the wagon. Good dry autumn pays everybody. A soaking spring ruins half a curing run before we can tighten the hoops."
*goto ox_yd_talk_menu

"Much cattle and cart traffic coming off the downs right now?" you ask.

"Steers push down from the Carrowmark and Dennholt hills whenever grass thins, while meal wagons come up from Esthorpe and Millstow," he answers, spitting onto the mud beyond the sill. "Only catch is Hartwell's gate men. Half their clerks argue over whether stock grazed west of Oxworth counts as outside meat so they can charge higher gate dues. Herders bellyache here first, then drink it off across the lane."
*goto ox_yd_talk_menu

"What sort of work do you pay half-day hands for here?" you ask.

"Two jobs, same honest rate," the foreman says, holding up two salt-chapped fingers. "Inside the lean-to, we need steady backs rolling full casks, hauling sack salt off wagons, and skimming hot brine tubs. Out by the hurdles, we need folks calm enough around horned steers to sort green hides onto frames and wedge broken rails tight without spooking half the pen."
*goto ox_yd_talk_menu
