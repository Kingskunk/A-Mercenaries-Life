*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── Matches Generic_Village_Gathering_Hall_Day.png.
*label ml_gh_place
*if (gh_first)
  A wide timber-and-plaster hall stands just off the lakeside lane behind a broad timber entrance. Inside, thick oak posts rise to open rafters over a broad plank floor, with a rough fieldstone hearth swept clean at the back left and a black iron stove piped through the roof on the right beneath a wooden shelf holding three oil lanterns and folded wool blankets. Two heavy trestle tables stand down the center of the floor, with long benches lined beneath the windows along the left wall. On the back right wall, above a stack of folded tables, a slatted wooden notice rack holds pinned sheets of paper, while a small brass bell hangs from a knotted rope on the nearest post. The air smells of dry oak, chalk dust, lamp oil, and old ledger paper.
*else
  The hall is much as you saw it: two heavy trestle tables down the broad plank floor, long window benches along the left wall, the cold fieldstone hearth and iron stove, and the slatted notice rack on the back wall.
*return

*comment ── LAYER 2: SKY ──
*label ml_gh_sky
*if (weather = "Rain")
  Rain patters lightly against the roof shingles overhead and runs down the left window panes.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  Pale lake mist presses against the left windows, muting the light across the oak planks.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Cold light through the windows catches a thin track of melted slush just inside the threshold.
*elseif (weather = "Clear")
  Clean daylight slants through the row of left windows and falls brightly across the trestle tables.
*else
  Flat grey daylight fills the left windows, leaving the stone hearth at the back corner in cool shadow.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  Scraped mud from the lakeside track sits along the threshold plank.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  Damp boot prints lead a few paces from the doorway toward the first table.
*return

*comment ── LAYER 3: STREET ── Merrit Appleby introduces herself on first contact.
*label ml_gh_street
*if (gh_first)
  *set met_ml_head true
  A sturdy, round-cheeked halfling woman in her early fifties sits on a tall wooden chair behind the nearer trestle table, a worn leather satchel slung over its back. Her sun-tanned face is framed by thick auburn-grey hair tucked beneath a plain linen kerchief, and keen hazel eyes crinkle at the corners over brass spectacles perched low on her nose. She wears a faded blue wool vest over a rolled-sleeve shirt lightly dusted with chalk.

  She taps her chalk pencil against an open grain-measure book and offers a crisp, polite nod. "Merrit Appleby, village head. Welcome to Millstow. If you're after work or road errands, check the notice rack on the back wall. If you have village business, step up to the table."
  *return

      "How does Millstow make its living?" you ask.

      Merrit ticks the answer off on her fingers without hesitation. "Wind, grain, and ovens, ${name}. Those two stone mills on the rise catch the steady breeze off Azure Lake and grind wheat and barley for every farm along this corner of the shore. Our bakers turn half of it into barley loaves for the Hartwell road, the farms fill in with eggs, roots, and cheese, and the lakeside skiffs bring in fresh perch."

      "No lord's manor looking over our shoulder here, just working tenants who balance their own books."

  *if (not(ml_gh_asked_roads))

      "Where do the roads and lake routes lead from here?" you ask.

      "Four roads meet our lane," Merrit answers crisply. "Main road runs seven miles southeast straight to Hartwell. Northwest takes you six miles to Fold Crossing, and from there on to Dunmow or Reedmere. Northeast climbs seven miles to Verdant Way, and southwest runs seven miles down to Maple Turn."

      She nods toward the left windows. "On calm days, flat-bottomed skiffs cross Azure Lake between our landing, Reedmere, Dunmow, and Oatcroft across the south bay. Saves a long walk if the wind behaves, though @{(met_orik) Orik|our boatwright} down at the shed will tell you faster than I can when the water's fit to trust."

      "And three nights back, two lake-edge pastures had sheep bolt clear over a drystone wall after something moved along the water in the dark. Could be poachers, could be a big beast pushed south out of the reeds. If you hear anything down by the landing or the inn, I'd be glad to know."
