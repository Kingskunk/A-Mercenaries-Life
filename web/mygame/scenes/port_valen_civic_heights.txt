The road from the Middle Ward ends at a broad plaza of cut limestone, and the buildings change from timber to stone. The Council Hall stands at its head, tall-windowed, with a bronze balance-scale taller than a man hung above its doors and the words [i]"The Free City of Port Valen"[/i] carved beneath it. Around the plaza stand the tax hall, the records house, and the magistrates' courts, each door carved with the city's three-masted seal. Painted boards mark the public windows, with deep eaves above them where people stand holding cargo papers and tax notices. Beside the windows stand sloped pine desks where men in ink-stained cuffs write out sheets for people at their elbows. A gate signed [i]Port Watch[/i] stands beside the courts, and its yard is crowded with crates and rolled papers.

The tax hall keeps two windows. The long counter, for householders and small traders, is reached by a wide flight of worn steps. The other is a side window with a private door, where clerks in river-serpent colors hand in leather cases and take out stamped receipts.

Facing the Council Hall across the plaza stands a tall stone house, the tallest on the plaza after the Hall itself, its bronze-banded doors hung with brass balance-scales and a gilded river-serpent coiled along the lintel.

The cathedral rises above the courts on an older foundation, its bells carrying out over the roofs of the lower city. Kitchens with iron soup-kettles cluster around its steps, and thin, tired people in mended clothes queue there, within sight of the Council Hall and a long way from its side window.
