Between the merchant heights and the canal lowlands, the city gathers around its workshops, guild halls, shrines, and crowded respectable homes. Painted boards mark the council chambers and public records rooms. Clerks carry dispatch cases through barred doors while petitioners wait beneath the eaves, clutching cargo papers, tax notices, and residency tokens. The Port Watch headquarters stands nearby, its yard crowded with seized goods and complaint scrolls.

The cathedral rises above the roofs on an older foundation, its bells carrying over every district. Kitchens and charitable houses cluster around its steps, drawing the sick, the displaced, and the people who have nowhere else to ask for help. Silk-workers carry bolts of dyed cloth through the surrounding streets. Bakers, smiths, and river clerks share walls with families who have lived here for generations and newcomers who arrived with nothing but a name.
