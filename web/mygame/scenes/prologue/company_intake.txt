Rain beats steadily against the black canvas of the encampment, turning the red clay of the marsh-fringe into a miserable sea of mud.

Smoke from damp pine fires stings your eyes. All around you, the Iron Carrion, four hundred hardened sellswords, deserters, and scoundrels, make camp on the return march from the outer borderlands. For weeks since marching north out of Port Valen past Marlcross and the Limestone Cut, your patrols have cleared bandit camps, hunted border beasts, and kept the frontier trade road open for Gilded Scales wagon trains.

Now, word from the forward riders has halted the column a day's march east of the Black Sinks: the stone toll-gatehouse that stood under Scales banners when you marched out has been besieged and seized behind you by desperate squatters and deserters, cutting off the road back west to Alderford. The sound of whetstones scraping against iron fills the air, rhythmic and cold as a death knell.

You sit on an upturned ale cask beneath a sagging leather awning, picking dried clay from your boot heels, when squelching footsteps approach through the muck.

A rat-faced company clerk in a moth-eaten wool mantle ducks under your awning. He clutches a tallow candle inside a pierced-tin lantern, shielding a cracked leather ledger from the drizzle. At the top of the damp vellum page, the date is scratched in dark iron-gall ink: [b]14th of Scorpio, 428 IR[/b]. He dips a split-iron pen into a horn of lampblack gall-ink and glares at you with watery, impatient eyes.

"You on the cask," he snaps, wiping rain from his upper lip with a grimy sleeve. "Re-muster roll for the gatehouse breach. Half the eastern patrol rosters are soaked in mud or crossed through. Give me your name clean so the paymaster knows who draws assault silver at Alderford, or who gets scratched off."

*label flashback_village
*set scenery_subplace "prologue_flashback_village"
Black smoke choked the summer sky above the village of [b]Ashbrook[/b]. You remember the laughter of Baron Aldous Karr's riders as they put torches to the communal granary while his tax collectors seized an unpaid grain tax. You remember the crash of a soldier's boot kicking down the farmhouse door.

You didn't plead. You grabbed the heavy iron-headed wood-axe from the chopping block.

The memory of the impact still reverberates in your forearms: the sickening, splintering crunch as your axe split the tax-collector's collarbone clean to the ribs. The spray of hot arterial blood across your face. You fled south through the black woods of the Coldspine foothills with blood on your hands and the Baron's tracking hounds howling in the valley behind you.

For three days you wandered the border scrublands like a starving ghost, living on pond water and bitter roots. Cold, hollow-eyed, and hunted for murder, you stumbled down out of the shale gullies into the [b]Limestone Cut[/b] road junction, where the Iron Carrion's black banner fluttered above an outbound marching camp. 

Inside the waystation, Sergeant Varren was filling the last gaps in the Carrion's files with Gilded Scales charter silver before the column marched east through the Black Sinks Gatehouse. He took one look at the dried blood caked under your fingernails, slid a steaming bowl of mutton stew across the greasy oak table, and dropped a heavy iron shilling beside it.

"Eat, boy," Varren had grunted. "Then put your thumb on the parchment. The Carrion doesn't ask who you killed, and the Baron's dogs don't cross into the Valen Reach to search our baggage trains."

You ate the stew. You took the iron. And the farmer's son died at that table.

*label flashback_slums
*set scenery_subplace "prologue_flashback_dredge"
The stench of rotting fish, raw sewage, and cheap jasmine perfume in the canal slums of [b]Dredge-End[/b] in [b]Port Valen[/b], the capital where men were sold for copper and killed for silver.

You had pried open the private strongbox of a crooked [b]Black Oath[/b] landing-warden who had been skimming punt-tolls from his own river brotherhood along the cut. But the muscle he kept on his private payroll, two hundred pounds of scarred dock-brawler with gold teeth, cornered you in the blind alley behind the canal brothel. When his butcher's cleaver flashed in the lantern light, your reflexes were already moving. 

Two quick, vicious upward jabs of your bone-handled shiv beneath his floating ribs. He collapsed into the filthy gutter with a wet gurgle. You snatched his purse, scrambled over a spiked iron fence, and vanished into the fog just as the whistles of the Port Watch began to shriek through the alleys.

Until the brotherhood worked out their own warden had been skimming the count, Dredge-End was a rat-trap. You slipped into the mildewed bed of an empty grain wagon rolling northeast out of the city, hiding among flour sacks until the wagon halted at the [b]Marlcross[/b] drying sheds.

Across the pale marl tracks, the Iron Carrion's outbound column was encamped for the night on its march toward the Limestone Cut and the eastern border road. You knew their reputation: cutthroats, renegades, and veterans on Valen's road charter who answered to no magistrate or street boss while under contract.

You didn't wait for the hue and cry to reach the crossroads. You marched straight past the sentries, flipped a stolen silver mark onto the quartermaster's table, and offered your blades for the eastern campaign. You traded the gallows for a mercenary's cloak.

Silk sheets, the heavy musk of spilled wine and lavender oil, and the muffled giggles of Baron Aldous Karr's younger wife in the high towers of [b]Karr's Keep[/b]. A glorious, reckless conquest, until the heavy oak doors splintered inward with the force of a battering ram.

Four household guardsmen with drawn rapiers flooded the chamber, their lantern light catching you half-naked. You didn't panic. You grabbed your velvet doublet, parried the lead guard's thrust with a heavy silver candelabra, and hurled yourself through the stained-glass window into the night.

The fall through the keep's rose arbor bruised your ribs and shredded your skin, but you hit the cobblestones running. By morning, Baron Karr's decree had declared your noble blood forfeit, your family lands seized by the local court, and your head priced for the executioner's block. Your family had held those lands on an old imperial patent, one no crown official had inspected or renewed in your grandfather's lifetime, a formality that meant nothing until the moment a baron's grudge decided it meant nothing at all. You pawned your signet ring at a Coldspine border post for common woolens and a handful of silver, slipping south into the Valen Reach.

Sitting alone in a wayside tavern at [b]Marlcross[/b], counting the last coppers from your pawned ring while mud-splashed grain-carters jostled your elbow without a second glance, you overheard a scarred Carrion lieutenant and two company veterans drinking before their column marched east through the Limestone Cut. They spoke with the crude, terrifying freedom of people who wore no king's colors: the Gilded Scales paid the company by contract, but Captain Vane, not a factor or a baron, gave its orders in the field.

You approached their table. You offered no noble excuses and asked for no pity. You asked for a contract.

"High blood," the lieutenant had sneered, looking over your hands with amused contempt. "Can you bleed like a common dog, lad?"

"Try me," you answered coldly.

He laughed, tossed you a leather arming doublet, and had the clerk scratch your new name into the ledger. You buried the nobleman that night, trading a ruined name for the absolute freedom of the sword.
