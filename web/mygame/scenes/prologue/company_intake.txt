Rain beats steadily against the black canvas of the encampment, turning the red clay of the river valley into a miserable sea of mud.

Smoke from damp pine fires stings your eyes. All around you, the Iron Carrion—four hundred hardened sellswords, deserters, and scoundrels—prepare for tomorrow's march into the contested borderlands. The sound of whetstones scraping against iron fills the air, rhythmic and cold as a death knell.

Before the horns sound tomorrow, you are just fresh meat in a company that buries three rookies for every one that survives the winter in the Grey Marches.

You sit on an upturned ale cask beneath a sagging leather awning, picking dried clay from your boot heels, when squelching footsteps approach through the muck.

A rat-faced company clerk in a moth-eaten wool mantle ducks under your awning. He clutches a tallow candle inside a pierced-tin lantern, shielding a cracked leather ledger from the drizzle. At the top of the damp vellum page, the date is scratched in dark iron-gall ink: [b]14th of Scorpio, 428 IR[/b]. He dips a split-iron pen into a horn of lampblack gall-ink and glares at you with watery, impatient eyes.

"You on the cask," he snaps, wiping rain from his upper lip with a grimy sleeve. "Muster roll. What name am I scratching into the book so the paymaster knows who to pay—or who to bury?"

*label flashback_village
*set scenery_subplace "prologue_flashback_village"
Black smoke choked the summer sky above the village of [b]Ashbrook[/b]. You remember the laughter of Baron Aldous Karr's mounted bailiffs as they put torches to the communal granary to enforce an unpaid grain tax. You remember the crash of a bailiff's boot kicking down the farmhouse door.

You didn't plead. You grabbed the heavy iron-headed wood-axe from the chopping block.

The memory of the impact still reverberates in your forearms: the sickening, splintering crunch as your axe split the bailiff's collarbone clean to the ribs. The spray of hot arterial blood across your face. You fled into the black woods of the High Crags with blood on your hands and the Baron's tracking hounds howling in the valley behind you.

For three days you wandered the border scrublands like a starving ghost, living on pond water and bitter roots. Cold, hollow-eyed, and hunted for murder, you reached a crossroads waystation where the Carrion's black banner fluttered above the palisade. 

Inside, Sergeant Varren was buying bodies for the Carrion's next contract with the Baron's own stolen silver. He took one look at the dried blood caked under your fingernails, slid a steaming bowl of mutton stew across the greasy oak table, and dropped a heavy iron shilling beside it.

"Eat, boy," Varren had grunted. "Then put your thumb on the parchment. The Carrion doesn't ask who you killed, and the Baron's dogs don't search our baggage trains."

You ate the stew. You took the iron. And the farmer's son died at that table.

*label flashback_slums
*set scenery_subplace "prologue_flashback_dredge"
The stench of rotting fish, raw sewage, and cheap jasmine perfume in the canal slums of [b]Dredge-End[/b] in [b]Port Valen[/b]—the capital where men were sold for copper and killed for silver.

You had taken six hundred silver marks out of the strongbox of the [b]Black Oath[/b]—the river-sworn brotherhood that holds every punt berth, eel-trap and night landing along the cut. But the man they send after anyone who crosses them, two hundred pounds of him with gold teeth, cornered you in the blind alley behind the canal brothel. When his butcher's cleaver flashed in the lantern light, your reflexes were already moving. 

Two quick, vicious upward jabs of your bone-handled shiv beneath his floating ribs. He collapsed into the filthy gutter with a wet gurgle. You snatched his purse, scrambled over a spiked iron fence, and vanished into the fog just as the whistles of the Port Watch began to shriek through the alleys.

Within two hours, the Black Oath had put a blood bounty on you in every tavern in the province. Port Valen was a rat-trap. You sneaked into the mildewed bed of a grain wagon rolling north, hiding among rotting sacks for two days until the city walls were fifty miles behind you.

When the wagon stopped at the marshland border, you saw the Carrion's encampment sprawling across the mud. You knew their reputation: cutthroats, renegades, and veterans who answered to no magistrate and respected no guild.

You didn't wait to be hunted down. You marched straight past the sentries, flipped a stolen silver mark onto the quartermaster's table, and offered your daggers. The Black Oath has long reach, but even a river brotherhood doesn't send its men into an army of four hundred killers. You traded the gallows for a mercenary's cloak.

*label flashback_scandal
*set scenery_subplace ""
Silk sheets, the heavy musk of spilled wine and lavender oil, and the muffled giggles of Baron Aldous Karr's younger wife in the high towers of [b]Karr's Keep[/b]. A glorious, reckless conquest—until the heavy oak doors splintered inward with the force of a battering ram.

Four household guardsmen with drawn rapiers flooded the chamber, their lantern light catching you half-naked. You didn't panic. You grabbed your velvet doublet, parried the lead guard's thrust with a heavy silver candelabra, and hurled yourself through the stained-glass window into the night.

The fall through the keep's rose arbor bruised your ribs and shredded your skin, but you hit the cobblestones running. By morning, Baron Karr's decree had declared your noble blood forfeit, your family lands seized by the local court, and your head priced for the executioner's block. Your family had held those lands on an old imperial patent, one no crown official had inspected or renewed in your grandfather's lifetime—a formality that meant nothing until the moment a baron's grudge decided it meant nothing at all. You pawned your signet ring at a frontier outpost for common woolens and a handful of silver.

Sitting alone in a border tavern, watching commoners spit on your family's fallen crest, you overheard a scarred Carrion lieutenant and two company veterans dividing silver from a sacked border fort. They spoke with the crude, terrifying freedom of men who owed fealty to no king and knelt to no god but coin.

You approached their table. You offered no noble excuses and asked for no pity. You asked for a contract.

"High blood," the lieutenant had sneered, looking over your hands with amused contempt. "Can you bleed like a common dog, lad?"

"Try me," you answered coldly.

He laughed, tossed you a leather arming doublet, and had the clerk scratch your new name into the ledger. You buried the nobleman that night, trading a ruined name for the absolute freedom of the sword.
