The third horn cracks through the freezing pre-dawn mist before you've truly slept, dragging you bolt upright out of your damp bedroll. Around you, groggy recruits curse and scramble for their gear in the pitch dark.

"UP, YOU MAGGOTS! ON YOUR FEET!"

Sergeant Varren's boot connects with the canvas wall of the tent hard enough to rattle the pegs. Outside, torches gutter in the drizzle, and the black shapes of veteran corporals move between the rows, dragging stragglers upright by their collars.

"Column marches at first light," Varren bellows, "but no sellsword worth his iron goes to a killing ground without the Carrion knowing what he's good for. Muster in the mud, greenhorns. Time to see what you're made of before the Sinks find out for us."

You stumble out into the freezing dark, falling into a ragged line of shivering recruits beside the dying watch-fires. One by one, veterans peel names off the line and set them to trials—wrestling pells, footraces through the mud, blind knife-work, whatever an evaluator fancies testing that morning.

A grizzled veteran with a notched ear and a slate tablet under one arm jabs a thumb at your chest.

"You. ${name}. With me."

He marches you to a battered wooden pell—a scarred practice post as thick as a man's thigh, hacked and splintered from a hundred recruits before you.

"Ashbrook, eh?" the scarred veteran grunts, sizing up your calloused hands. "Word travels. Farmer's boy who split a bailiff's collarbone with a wood-axe. Let's see if that was luck, or if there's a soldier under all that mud."

He tosses you a padded practice cudgel and steps back.

"Break it down. However you like."

*label trial_disgraced
He leads you away from the pells and wire-courses entirely, toward the mist-shrouded tree line where the camp's boundary rope sags between rotting posts—out past the last watch-fire, where the dark presses in close.

"Karr's Keep, eh?" the evaluator says, a faint sneer in his voice. "High blood playing at soldiering. Let's see what a noble's schooling is actually good for. Stand the boundary rope till the fourth horn. Alone. Report anything you see."

He leaves you a guttering tallow lamp and vanishes back toward the fires, boots squelching into the dark.

Alone at the edge of the marsh, the mist curls unnaturally still around the boundary rope, and the reeds beyond it do not move with the wind.
