The column marches out at first light, four hundred strong, boots churning the frost-hardened mud into a grey soup within the first mile. Pike-shafts and crossbow cases clatter against the supply wagons. Somewhere behind you a mule screams at nothing and gets cursed back into silence. Nobody sings. Nobody talks much, past the first hour—you watch the sweat-dark back of the recruit in front of you and think about very little at all, which old hands in the files tell you is the only way anyone survives a campaign march.

By early afternoon the good road gives out, and the column slogs the last few miles along a raised dirt track through flat, half-drowned farmland gone to reed and standing water. The smell changes long before the causeway comes into view—wet rot, stagnant mud, and underneath it something faintly sweet that you don't let yourself think too hard about.

The Black Sinks Causeway is a raised spine of cracked grey stone, barely wide enough for two men abreast, flanked on both sides by waist-deep black bog choked with dead reeds and the rotted stumps of old pilings. At the far end, a squat stone gatehouse squats over the road like a tick fat on old blood, and shapes are already moving along its broken parapet—thin, ragged shapes, more scarecrow than soldier, hefting whatever passes for a weapon among people who used to be farmers and dockhands.

The company doesn't approach as one mass. Vanguard File forms up dead center on the causeway stone itself, shields locked shoulder to shoulder—first across, first to bleed, exactly the way Varren wants it. Off to either flank, barely visible against the reeds, the Scout Company is already peeling away from the column entirely, wading into the waist-deep bog to come at the gatehouse from an angle no sentry up there is watching. And well back from the stone, close enough to the baggage train to fall back if it goes wrong, the handful of Cadre recruits wait with Ysolde—too few, and worth too much, to spend on a headlong charge across open ground.
