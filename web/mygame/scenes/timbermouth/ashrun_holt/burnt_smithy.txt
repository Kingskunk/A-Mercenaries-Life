*line_break
The tracks leave the work road where the timber span crosses the Ashrun, sliding down the clay slope into the reeds. Someone came down here to wash soot off their hands or cut along the stream out of sight.

At the water's edge, the mud is torn up by a sudden scramble. A single leather boot lies half sunk in the silt, its laces snapped. Nearby, three deep parallel grooves rake across the wet clay, too wide and curved for any dog. Drops of blood lead up the incline, across the pine needles, and toward the edge of the village cabins.

[b]◈ JORY'S CABIN[/b]
*line_break
The blood drops cross the beaten edge of the clearing and stop at a low log cabin downwind of the charcoal track. The door is unlatched. Inside, the room smells of wet wool, tallow, and stale ale.

A lean man in his late twenties sits on a stool near the unlit stove, wrapping a stained strip of linen around his left forearm. Three jagged tears bleed through the cloth. His right foot wears a muddy lace boot; his left foot is bare and bruised.

When you drop the matching boot onto his table next to the charred pine knot, his hands freeze.

"Keep your voice down," he mutters, eyes darting to the shuttered window. "Name's Jory. Look, I was drunk. That dwarf comes up from Timbermouth, takes all the haulers' coin, takes work my uncle used to mend for half the price. I threw a pitch knot on his bark roof to smoke him out. Did not mean for the whole shed to go up, and then some river devil nearly took my arm off under the span."

He reaches into a tin box by the stove and slides a stack of coins across the boards. "Twenty five copper. All I have got. Tell Durgan it was a stray spark or some drifter heading south. Nobody has to know."
