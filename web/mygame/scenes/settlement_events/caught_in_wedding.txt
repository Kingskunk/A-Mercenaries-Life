*elseif (se_wedding_style = "riverside")

*elseif (se_wedding_style = "crossroads")
  The sound of a fiddle reaches you before the procession turns into the crossing. Ribbons are tied to the rail outside the Cross Hall, and the Grain Green's nearest awning has been cleared for the gathered families.
*elseif (se_wedding_style = "coastal")
  A wedding party has gathered by the Tide House steps, where the bank gives them dry ground above the flats. Salt-white ribbon knots flutter from the bell frame.
*elseif (se_wedding_style = "common")
  A wedding party has gathered by the hall at the edge of the common. Bright ribbons run from the well-head to the hitching rail, and children keep darting between the adults with flower heads clutched in both hands.
*else
  The Longhouse porch has been swept clear beneath the old ash. Knotted cords and strips of cloth hang from its low branches, and a fiddle answers the soft beat of hands on the porch rail.

A person in a new coat, cheeks red with haste, catches at your sleeve before the procession closes around you. Another waits a few steps behind, holding a spray of late flowers tight enough to bend the stems.

"I'm ${wedding_first}," the first says. They point toward the waiting figure. "That's ${wedding_second}. Our second witness has vanished, and the bell's about to ring. Will you stand with us?"

The waiting figure gives you an apologetic look. "My sister went to fetch her shawl. Then her uncle gave her something to drink. That was a while ago."

*if (weather = "Rain")
  Fine rain darkens the ribbons and gathers on the shoulders of the waiting guests, but no one has yet lost the will to celebrate.
*elseif (weather = "Snow")
  Snow catches in hair and on sleeves. Someone keeps brushing the flowers clear before the petals can disappear beneath it.
*elseif (weather = "Sleet")
  Sleet ticks against the awning and clings to the ribbons. Three people are holding the awning rope as though the weather has taken a personal dislike to weddings.
*else
  The gathered families keep glancing toward the lane, measuring how much longer they can hold the moment before ordinary traffic breaks it apart.

  # "I'll stand with you." Take the missing witness's place. [~15 min]
    You step into the small space beside the couple. "Tell me where you need me."

    The person with the flowers lets out a breath and passes you a length of ribbon. An older celebrant in a good coat asks you to hold one end while the couple takes the other between them.

    "You know why you are here?" the celebrant asks.

    You look from one anxious face to the other. "To say I saw you choose each other."

    That earns a laugh from the crowd, small at first and then warm enough to ease both of them. The vows are plain promises: to save a place at the fire, to speak straight when speaking is hard, and to carry the work when the other cannot. No one pretends that promises make a life easy. That may be why the people listening take them seriously.

    When the bell rings, it is close and bright. ${wedding_first} clasps your hand between both of theirs.

    "You saved us from a very awkward story," they say.

    ${wedding_second} presses the bent flowers into ${wedding_first}'s free hand. "Now it is only our story," they say.

    Around you, the crowd breaks into cheers and the first steps of a dance. You leave them their celebration and find the lane open again.

  # Help the procession make room in the lane. [~10 min]
    You take one end of the ribbon and wave the nearest cart around the crowd. "Give them a clear path. They'll be done soon."

    The driver grumbles, then sees the couple and softens enough to turn the team with care. You keep the impatient foot traffic from cutting through the gathering, send two children after a loose ribbon, and make room for the old celebrant to reach the couple without being jostled.

    Once the lane is clear, the ceremony finds its shape. You stand at its edge and hear the crowd answer the vows with a cheer. The fiddler catches the rhythm before the bell has stopped ringing, and the guests begin clapping in time.

    As you step aside, ${wedding_second} lifts their flowers in thanks. "You gave us a proper beginning," they call.

    It is a small thing to have done, but the lane feels kinder for it. You leave as the first dancers claim the cleared ground.
