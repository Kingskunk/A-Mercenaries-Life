The road climbs in turns from the docks, the salt smell of the harbor thinning behind it, then levels out onto the Second Terrace, the broad shelf of flat ground that holds most of the city. The lower streets fall away toward the harbor behind you, while the limestone cliffs of Civic Heights and the walled slopes of the Patrician Quarter rise in tiers ahead. This is the largest ward in Port Valen, and it spreads out on every side: craft lanes, tenement rows, wagon yards, and inns, stretching farther than the eye can follow. Sturdy timber buildings lean over narrow lanes, their upper floors nearly touching beneath steep slate roofs, while their lower shutters open as shop counters. The air smells of cooled iron, brass dust, cedar sawdust, and warm caraway bread from the public ovens.

At the center of the square, an octagonal stone public fountain sends clear mountain water through four bronze lion-head spouts into deep basins. Narrow lanes branch from the crossroads toward the different craft quarters, while the wide trunk road, paved for iron-tired wheels, runs on through the square, east and west toward the great gates in the landward wall, and down the switchbacks to the harbor. A short way along it, a white-painted wagon wheel hangs from an iron arm over the door of an inn. Opposite the fountain, a broad chimney rises above a painted sign reading [i]"Mother Marda's Communal Oven,"[/i] sending sweet rye smoke into the air.
