*comment House Gault opens its page on the player's Insignia here (Insignia.register is safe to run twice). Free: the Countess wants to know who is hired in her Vale (invented 2026-10-30).
*label lg_hall_present
*set minutes_to_pass 10
*set time_advance_call_id "lg_hall_present_1"
*gosub_scene Langston/langston lg_walk_time
*script window.Insignia.register(stats, "gault");
[b]◈ LANGSTON HALL: THE COUNTERS[/b]
*line_break
You lay your Contractor's Insignia on the sloped desk at the nearest open shutter. "I'm looking for work in the Vale," you tell the clerk behind it. "Can the Hall put my name down?"

She turns the disc over with an ink-stained finger, then passes a flat grey seal-stone across the brass. It chimes, and a line of script gathers on its face. "Gilded Scales," she reads. "Port Valen page." She sets the stone aside. "That tells me the disc is yours and nobody forged it. Past that it's worth nothing here. This is the Countess's Vale."

From a drawer she takes a second stone, its face cut with the arms of House Gault, and presses it to the metal beside the first until the brass warms under your thumb. A new page opens on the disc with your name at the head of it.

"Listed," she says. "There's no fee. The Countess likes to know who's hired in her Vale." She writes your name in the Hall's book and turns the disc back to you. "That's all the word means. The Hall knows you, and you can take the ordinary work posted here. Finish it well and the Hall remembers."

You tuck the Insignia away and ask where the work gets posted. "By the doors, when there's any," she says, and nods toward the boards along the wall. "Read them before you ask us."

*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── Fixed geography, architecture and smell. The long description prints once.
*label lg_hall_place
*if (lg_hall_first)
  Langston Hall occupies the upper side of the market square, reached by a broad flight of shallow steps. It is not much larger than the timber-framed buildings crowded around it, but its plain dressed-stone walls are far heavier. Broad pale blocks marked with worn imperial numbers form the lowest courses; darker stone and a steep tiled roof belong to later rebuilding. A weathered carving of House Gault's arms stands above the oak doors, with a newer tablet beneath it naming this the Countess's hall in Langston.

  The public chamber runs across the front of the building under dark roof beams. Benches face a long table beneath the Gault arms, where the mayor hears petitions and disputes. Along one wall, shuttered counter openings and sloped writing desks serve the town clerks. Boards beside them carry notices of court days, market tolls, road levies and militia service, each sheet bearing the mayor's mark or the Countess's seal.

  Plain doors at the back lead toward the mayor's office and the record rooms. A spear rack for the Langston Watch stands beside the inner passage, close to the bell rope that climbs through the ceiling. The worn floor, orderly desks and shelves of bound accounts give the place the compact, workmanlike weight of a town governed from somewhere larger. The chamber smells of ink, hearth ash, old paper and damp wool.
*else
  Langston Hall's public chamber lies behind its heavy oak doors, with the town clerks' shuttered counters along one wall and the mayor's long table beneath the arms of House Gault. Benches fill the worn stone floor between them. The inner passage leads toward the mayor's office, the record rooms and the watch room, while the bell rope hangs beside its spear rack.
*return

*label lg_hall_street
*if (street_life = "empty")
  The public counters are shuttered, but the Hall has not turned people back into the dangerous weather. Townspeople wait along the benches with their wet outer layers steaming near the hearth, while Watch officers hold the doors nearly closed and leave a narrow way through.
*elseif (time_period = "Pre-Dawn")

*elseif (time_period = "Night")

*elseif (time_period = "Dusk")

*elseif (day_of_week = "Hallowday")
  The public counters are closed for the rest day. The benches and writing desks stand empty, though a Watch officer still holds the entrance and answers the bell.
*elseif (street_life = "thin")
  The Hall remains open through the poor weather, but only a short line waits at the counter. Damp outer layers hang from pegs near the doors, and clerks work through petitions and town accounts behind the open shutters.
*elseif (day_of_week = "Marketday")
  Traders and townspeople fill the benches and writing desks. The counter line carries questions about stall places, market tolls and cart access, while clerks move account sheets through the open shutters.
*elseif (time_period = "Morning")
  Clerks open the counter shutters and call the first petitioners from the benches. Townspeople wait with folded notices, rent questions and requests for the mayor's hearing.
*elseif (time_period = "Midday")
  The chamber is at its busiest. Every writing desk is in use, the counter line reaches the benches, and low voices gather beneath the dark roof beams.
*else
  The afternoon line has shortened, leaving open places on the benches while clerks sort the remaining petitions and town accounts behind the counter.
