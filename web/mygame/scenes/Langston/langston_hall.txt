*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── Fixed geography, architecture and smell. The long description prints once.
*label lg_hall_place
*if (lg_hall_first)
  Langston Hall occupies the upper side of the market square, reached by a broad flight of shallow steps. It is not much larger than the timber-framed buildings crowded around it, but its plain dressed-stone walls are far heavier. Broad pale blocks marked with worn imperial numbers form the lowest courses; darker stone and a steep tiled roof belong to later rebuilding. A weathered carving of House Gault's arms stands above the oak doors, with a newer tablet beneath it naming this the Countess's hall in Langston.

  The public chamber runs across the front of the building under dark roof beams. Benches face a long table beneath the Gault arms, where the mayor hears petitions and disputes. Along one wall, shuttered counter openings and sloped writing desks serve the town clerks. Boards beside them carry notices of court days, market tolls, road levies and militia service, each sheet bearing the mayor's mark or the Countess's seal.

  Plain doors at the back lead toward the mayor's office and the record rooms. A spear rack for the Langston Watch stands beside the inner passage, close to the bell rope that climbs through the ceiling. The worn floor, orderly desks and shelves of bound accounts give the place the compact, workmanlike weight of a town governed from somewhere larger. The chamber smells of ink, hearth ash, old paper and damp wool.

Authority enters from the inner rooms. Everyone else comes through the same oak doors and remains in view from the moment they cross the threshold.
