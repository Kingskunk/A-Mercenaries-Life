*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── Fixed geography only. The long description prints once.
*label ba_hl_place
*if (hl_first)
  Barolo's village hall is a long, low house of pale stone with a steep tiled roof, set a little back from the lane behind a gravel forecourt. A covered bench runs along the front wall beside the door, where people can wait out of the rain. Inside, the public room is a wide, plain space with a flagged floor and benches along the walls. At one end a sloped desk stands under a window with a heavy posting book open on it. A notice board with a shallow wooden canopy and two hinged shutters hangs on the wall beside the entrance, under House Gault's painted shield, and past it a heavy oak door banded with iron stays locked. Two more doors lead into the rest of the house, where the village head lives. The room smells of ink, beeswax and old paper.
*else
  The public room is as before: the benches along the walls, the sloped desk under its window, the notice board beneath the painted shield and the locked oak door beside it.
*return

"Who waits on all these benches?" you ask.

"Boundaries, water, debts," he says, quick as a list. "A man whose neighbour's vine has crossed a line. A woman whose late husband's strip goes to her brother, or doesn't. Two cousins who haven't spoken since a wedding and need a path between their plots settled. Mostly they're neighbours. Mostly they like each other, you'd be surprised."

"How did you come to work here?" you ask.

"My father makes barrels," he says. "Barrels don't read. I did. Six of us, one decent hand, so the village head took me on at fifteen, and I've been on this stool since."

"Is it what you wanted?"

"I wanted a stool and a window." He tips his pen toward the one over the desk. "I've both."

"What's the village head like to work for?" you ask.

"Slow," he says, then glances at the inner doors. "Careful. Careful is what I meant. He reads every line twice, has me read it a third time, then signs. In all my years here he's never signed wrong."

"Does he ever lose his temper?"

"Once. A man had changed a number in the posting book. He didn't shout. He stopped talking for the rest of the day, and the whole hall knew."
*goto ba_hl_clerk_menu

"Most of what reaches this desk is them." He sets the pen down for the first time. "Some seventy strips round the village, give or take a cousin. They bring their grapes to the common press together, they argue over whose cart goes first, and they drink at the Stave and forget it."

"How did you come to be village head?" you ask.

"I was the clerk here, as the lad at the desk is now." He almost smiles. "The old village head died at that table, in the middle of a sentence, and nobody else could read his hand. I could. The mayor confirmed me under the Countess's seal that spring."

"I wanted the sentence finished." He looks at the page in front of him. "I've been finishing it ever since."

Hobart Thring lays the pen down and folds his hands over the page. He is quiet for a while.

"Lady Maelin asks that her family's old rights are named in any dispute that touches them, whether or not anyone ever wrote them down," he says. "She asks politely, and often. Master Cellan asks for nothing, which is how I know what he wants. He'd like this Hall to write 'landed gentry' beside his name in the posting book. I've told him a title doesn't belong in a book that holds boundaries."

"And the Avenels?"

"Mistress Tressa asks me to remember the small growers. Her nephew asks me to remember the Avenels." He picks up the pen. "I tell you because you asked like someone who might need to know which way the wind blows. Don't write it where they can read it."
