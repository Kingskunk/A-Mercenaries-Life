*label ba_rs_place
*if (rs_first)
  The Red Stave is a broad two-storey house of stone and timber, set back from the lane behind a covered carriage arch. The common hall is long and high, with dark beams overhead, a wide hearth at one end and a row of barrels lying on their sides along the back wall, each with a wooden tap. Doors along one side lead to smaller rooms with tables of their own, and a stair climbs to a gallery of guest rooms. Behind the counter a second, narrower stair drops into the cellar, and a cool breath of stone and wine rises from it. The hall smells of roasting meat, spilled wine, trodden straw from the court and the sour sweetness of old oak.
*else

"Who looks after the village?" you ask. "Somebody must settle things when two neighbours fall out."

"Hobart Thring," Odalie says. "He's the village head, going on eleven years now. Slow, fair, and he writes everything down. Folk grumble about the slow until they need the writing." She sets a glass on the shelf. "He answers to the mayor in Langston, and she answers to the Countess, so he's careful. You'll find him at the Hall most days."

"Does he drink here?" you ask.

"A cup at noon, never two. Hobart Thring doesn't care to be seen with his judgement loosened."

"I saw manor roofs from the lane," you say. "Who owns the big houses up the hill?"

"Three houses, three heads, and I pour for all of them, so mind how you ask me about any one." Odalie counts them off on her fingers. "Lady Maelin Veyre has the old stone house at the top. A widow, proud as a church bell, and she'll pay what her family owes if she has to sell a spoon to do it. Master Cellan Durnell has the big yard on the cart lane. He came up from the wagon trade, pays every wage on the day, and remembers every favour. Mistress Tressa Avenel grew up between these rows. She knows every grower by their first name and their father's."

"And do they get on?" you ask.

"The way three neighbours with good land get on." She shrugs and picks up a cloth. "I pour for all three, love. That's why I'm still pouring."

"How did you come to run the Stave?" you ask. "You don't strike me as someone who drifted into it."

"Cook, first." Odalie smiles. "I came up the river road with my mother's recipes and a cart with a bad wheel. The man who owned the house wanted to go and live on his sister's farm, and I'd saved a little coin, so he sold it to me on terms. I've paid every one of them."

"Did you always want an inn?"

"I always wanted to be standing on something." She taps the step under her boots with one heel. "A house like this, a woman my size, people think the step is for the barrels. It's for me."
