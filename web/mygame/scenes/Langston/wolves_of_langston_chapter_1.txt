You crest a low rise. Langston lies ahead.

The dirt road you have been traveling transitions to cobblestones, and a simple stone wall rings the town. Even from this distance, you can see that the timber-framed buildings are well maintained, their walls bright with fresh whitewash. It is a beautiful little town, a prosperous trading hub amid fertile fields and broad pasture, with old forest visible along the southern horizon.

A lone howl splits the silence somewhere beyond the roadside hedges. A chorus of answering howls takes up the call across the vale, some uncomfortably close. And getting closer.

You pick up your pace to put more distance between you and the wilderness in the hope of a soft bed, a hot meal, and some work for an enterprising mercenary. A town as prosperous as Langston looks like a place to do good, earn coin, and achieve glory. Maybe all three at once, so long as you are not eaten by wolves between here and there.

You square up to defend yourself as the pack of hungry wolves starts to circle, looking for an opening to attack. There are at least ten of them, and something tells you more are waiting beyond the hedges.

It looks like an early grave, but it would not do to give up without a fight. You are just about to unleash your attack and go down fighting when you hear a bellow behind you on the road.

You turn around to see a bald dwarf dressed in rough-spun clothes and animal hides pointing at you from on top of a rise in the road. Or maybe past you. Firelight shines off the skin of his head as small balls of flame dance in the air over his outstretched palms.

"Oh no you don't!" he yells, the flames flaring with his emotions. "Get back, you lot!"

The wolves immediately tuck their tails between their legs and lay their ears down flat. The apparent leader looks at you and whines.

"Not a chance," the dwarf scoffs. "Go on now."

The wolves slink quietly back through the roadside cover, and you realize you are still poised to attack.

A middle-aged human couple gets up and introduces themselves as Maybelline's parents, though the murmur of sympathy from the crowd suggests that everyone already knows them.

"Thank you so much for coming out to say goodbye to our darling girl with us," her mother says. "She was a ray of sunshine in our lives from the beginning. And we can see that she touched the lives of many of you as well. She was relentlessly kind, generous, and patient. We will all miss her dearly."

Maybelline's father clears his throat, his eyes glassy. "We are so very grateful for the people of Langston. You made our daughter's life a joy, and you are making her loss more bearable. But we will not be seeing you for some time."

A murmur runs through the crowd. He holds up his hands for quiet, but Maybelline's mother continues.

"Every brick and blade of grass in this town reminds us of what we have lost," she says with a quavering voice. "We will take some time to grieve at home, where we feel closest to our daughter's memories. But we pray you bring the killer to swift justice."

As she speaks, heads in the crowd turn toward the mayor. Anathram nods to the gathered townsfolk. "We will respect your privacy. The killer will be found."

When you turn your attention back to the grieving parents, the father's cheeks are wet with tears, but his teeth are gritted in mute rage. Maybelline's mother lays a gentle hand on her husband's arm.

"But we are not the only ones to suffer a loss," she says, her voice cracking. "We had Maybelline for her whole life. The love of her life was cheated out of the rest of it. Tomlin, darling, we would love it if you could say a few words."

Maybelline's parents step aside, their faces going slack as though they expended all their emotion in the speech. Their red-rimmed eyes fix on the floor as they move woodenly back to their seats and the apparent fiance takes their place.

Tomlin is dressed simply and clutches a bunch of flowers tightly in one hand. He takes a moment to look out over the crowd, visibly holding back tears.

"Thank you all so much for coming," he says with a sigh. "We are gathered to remember the beautiful, shining light that was my bride-to-be. Before growing to love her, I never thought I was the marrying kind. Now it seems more like fate than a choice."

"Does that mean the Wolf is back on the prowl?" one man near you whispers to a friend. "Lock up your sisters and daughters!"

"And wives!" the friend replies with a snigger.

Both are shushed by women who appear to be their wives, the two women sharing embarrassed glances. Others nearby glare at the would-be comedians and mouth insults at them.

"My darling wife-to-be was a simple soul," Tomlin continues. "Her favorite flowers were daisies. She liked the color lavender. Anything made of silk was the height of sophistication."

The men continue whispering lewdly and sniggering. The women beside them glare and exchange exasperated looks with one another. Oblivious to what is happening in front of him, Tomlin closes his eyes to collect himself with a sigh before continuing.

"To put it simply, Maybelline's death has torn a hole in my life that cannot be filled, but I will try. Work will help, I am sure. And giving back to all of you who have done so much for me, especially in these dark times. I can only try to repay your kindness. I truly love you all, and you are all welcome at my home at any time."

The audience applauds respectfully and murmurs condolences. Right away, however, something seems odd about the situation.

You immediately recognize the next speaker as Roi Sunderhammer, the dwarf you met on the road. He is dressed in hides and homespun cloth, while his hair and beard seem to struggle against the leather cords he has used to style them. He is clean now, but something in his demeanor suggests that this is his version of dressing up.

"Welcome, people of Langston," he says with a warm smile that makes his eyes sparkle. "As sad as I am to see Maybelline return to the earth, I want to remind Langston to choose life. As you all know, I am grateful my circle assigned me to your town."

"And you have all made it so easy for me. "Excuse me," he says with a deep growl in his voice.

He clears his throat and completes the spell, causing a splash of extraordinarily bright wildflowers to bloom on the dais where the casket rests. He nods in approval at his own work before turning to the crowd, raising a glass, and smiling broadly.

He downs his drink along with others in the crowd, but the strange reaction he had to the sneezing fit holds your attention.
