*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── Fixed geography only. The long description prints once. Nothing here changes with the season or the hour.
*label bm_ry_place
*if (ry_first)
  Across the stream from the village the ground rises a little, and on it stands the one place in Broadmead that looks as if it was built to a plan. A wall of dressed grey stone runs along the lane, and an iron-barred gate stands shut between two squared posts, with a small guardhouse beside it. Over the wall and through the bars the lodge shows, a long grey stone house of two storeys under a slate roof, and a tall navy banner hangs over its door, the cloth edged in narrow gold and bearing a single golden horse in profile, one foreleg raised. A stable range runs beside it, its split half-doors painted and shut in even rows. Next to the stables a smithy bay stands open to the yard, with an anvil on a stump and a dull glow in the forge, and next to that a tack room open at the front shows saddles on racks and bridles in lines. The yard between them is raked gravel, swept to the edges. Beyond the buildings a second gate opens onto the muster field, where a picket line runs along one side and a straw quintain post stands at the far end.
*else
  The yard is as before: the grey wall and the shut iron gate, the lodge under its banner beyond, the stable range with its shut half-doors, the open smithy bay and tack room, and the raked gravel running back to the gate of the muster field.
*return

*comment ── DORMANT: SERGEANT DAGNY MARREN ── Nothing reaches this section while the yard is barred (see the header).   You cross the gravel. The woman at the picket line finishes with the horse first: a murmur, a palm run down its neck, a last check of the foreleg. Then she turns, and you see the old scar through her left eyebrow and the pale lines the reins have worn across both palms.

  "Sergeant Dagny Marren," she says Her voice is low and level, and she does not raise it to be heard. "Most people who walk over here have lost something or been sent. Which are you?"

  "Neither," you say. "I'm new to Broadmead, and I wanted to see the yard."

  "It isn't for seeing." She looks you over, then gives a small nod. "But you crossed the gravel without startling a horse. Stand there, and ask what you like."
*else
  Sergeant Marren hands the horse's lead to a groom and gives you her attention.

"How did you come to run the yard?" you ask.

"I rode for the Countess eleven years. A horse went down under me on the ice at Coombe Gap, and the knee that took the weight told me what the next ten would be." She flexes one scarred hand. "The mayor wanted someone who would sort a horse by what it is and not by what it's worth. The captain in Dunmow named me. I haven't asked him why."

"Do you miss riding?"

"Every morning. Then I put a saddle on a young horse, and I miss it less."
