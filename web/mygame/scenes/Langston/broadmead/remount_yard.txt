Over the wall and through the bars the lodge shows, a long grey stone house of two storeys under a slate roof, and a tall navy banner hangs over its door, the cloth edged in narrow gold and bearing a single golden horse in profile, one foreleg raised. *comment ── SERGEANT DAGNY MARREN ── Reached once bm_ry_open is true (see the header). Human, forty-odd; calm, level and economical, never raises her voice; speaks to a horse before she speaks to a person; fair the way a good farrier is fair (Olof's words). She is the yard's only seller. Her one free topic is her own story; no tenant, holding or family is named.

  You cross the gravel. The woman at the hitching rail finishes with the horse first: a low murmur, a firm palm run along its neck, and a practiced check of the front leg. She is tall and lean in her early forties, with short greying brown hair tucked behind her ears. She turns toward you with a weathered face, grey eyes, and an old scar cutting through her left eyebrow. She wears a dark blue tunic under a leather vest, and pale grooves run across her calloused palms from years on the reins.

  "Sergeant Dagny Marren," she says. Her voice is low and level, and she does not raise it to be heard. "Most people who walk over here have lost something or been sent. Which are you?"

  "Neither," you say. "I'm new to Broadmead, and I wanted to see the yard."

  "It isn't for seeing." She looks you over, then gives a small nod. "But you crossed the gravel without startling a horse. Stand there, and ask what you like."

"How did you come to run the yard?" you ask.

"I rode for the Countess eleven years. A horse went down under me on the ice at Coombe Gap, and the knee that took the weight told me what the next ten would be." She flexes one scarred hand. "The mayor wanted someone who would sort a horse by what it is and not by what it's worth. The captain in Dunmow named me. I haven't asked him why."

"Do you miss riding?"

"Every morning. Then I put a saddle on a young horse, and I miss it less."
