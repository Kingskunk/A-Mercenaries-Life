*if (hl_first)
  The Village Head's Hall is a long, low house of timber and stone under a deep thatch, with the hurdle pens of the petition yard beside it and rows of weathered boards under its eaves, each one burned with a different mark. Inside, the public room is long and low-beamed, floored with flagstones scattered with straw. A plank counter runs along one side beneath shelves of bound books and rolled papers, and a rail on the wall above it holds a row of iron brand-marks, ring and cross and crooked bar. A long table and benches stand along the other side for people waiting, and an inner door stands open on a small study with a window onto the yard. Beside that door hangs a board of pale planks under a narrow slate roof of its own, its sheets pinned in three sections beneath headings chalked in big square letters. The room smells of hay, old paper, lamp oil and wet dog.

"How did you come to be village head?" you ask.

"Thirty years I walked cattle and sheep for the yard's buyers, Dunmow to the river and back. Knew every brand in the Vale and most of the owners' mothers." She taps the rail of iron marks above the counter. "The old head went to live with his daughter. The mayor asked who in Broadmead could read a brand in the dark, and every man in the village pointed at me."

"Did you want it?"

"I said no. Then I said yes, because a head who can't read a brand's a head who can be lied to." She slaps her knee. "I wanted my knees back. Got a desk. Hoofs and horns, it's the worse trade."

*label bm_hl_talk_holders
*set bm_hl_asked_holders true
*set minutes_to_pass 5
*set time_advance_call_id "bm_hl_talk_holders"
*gosub_scene calendar advance_time
"Tell me about the families who raise the Countess's mares," you say.

"Eight, nine holdings." She counts them off on her fingers. "One, they're good with horses, better than they know. Two, they're careful, because the yard counts every foal and they know it. Three, they're tired of folk acting like the mare's a gift."

"Do they get on with each other?"

"Mostly. They share a stud and sit up at each other's foalings." She shakes her head. "Don't take 'em for a faction. They're nine families who each got a mare."
*goto bm_hl_talk_menu

"And the folk who don't have mares?" you ask.

"Three times as many, give or take. Shepherds, dairy folk, geese, whatever'll eat grass." She holds up a hand and folds the fingers down. "Their grandmothers grazed the lower commons before there was a yard, and there isn't a scrap of paper to say so. One, they work harder. Two, they're prouder. Three..." She stops and looks at the door. "Three I'll tell you when you've sat a while."
*goto bm_hl_talk_menu

"One, they like each other fine till the grass is wet. Two, every time the Home Meadow's fenced for the mares, the commons lose a strip of spring grazing, and nobody's wrong." She folds the fingers down one by one. "The holdings have paper. The commons have custom. I've got to rule on both, and what I've got to rule on's a fence."

"Will it come to a fight?"

"Not this year." She doesn't smile. "I count that every spring." She straightens up. "Don't carry it to either side. They both think I'm half theirs, and I need 'em both to go on thinking it."
