"Who runs the grey lodge across the stream?" you ask.

"Sergeant Dagny Marren." Olof sets his mug down. "Forty-odd. Rode for the Countess till a fall put her on the ground. Been teaching horses to be ridden and troopers to ride ever since. Counts every foal the tenants raise for the yard, and she counts 'em herself."

"Is she fair?"

"Fair like a good farrier's fair. Hard on the foot, honest about it." He turns the mug a quarter turn. "Talks to the horse before she talks to the rider. I trust a woman who does that."

"Could I buy a horse from her?"

"Not as a stranger." He says it without heat. "Yard's barracks, not a market. Gate wants a writ from the Hall in Langston, or her own word, and you've neither. Horse for the road, try the fair on Marketday. Folk tie 'em on a rope line at the top of the ground."

"Does she drink here?"

"No. One of her troopers comes for a jug on the rest day. Coin's here by the afternoon."

"How did you come to run the Halter?" you ask. "You don't strike me as a man who started in a kitchen."

"Twenty-two years on the roads behind other men's flocks." Olof taps his stiff leg once with two fingers. "Wool to Langston, cattle to the river. Mare put her hoof through this outside Dunmow. Drover who can't walk's a man with a good story and no work."

"Did she mean it?"

"Horse always means it. That's the thing about horses." He almost smiles. "Fellow who ran this place before me wanted to sit down. So did I. Gave him my savings and my second-best horse, and he gave me the key."

"And the halter over the door? It hangs crooked."

"Hung it straight twice. Someone knocks it every time." He lifts a shoulder. "It's a sign for a drovers' house. Ought to look like somebody was in a hurry."
