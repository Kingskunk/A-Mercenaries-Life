*comment ── LAYER 1: PLACE ── The track and the trees, always true.
*label rg_place
Past the last field the track turns into the trees. It is two ruts with a strip of grass between them, then a footpath where the roots crowd in. The trunks are old and broad, and the air smells of leaf litter, bark and green growing things.
*return

*comment ── LAYER 2: SKY ── Weather and the light under the canopy, closing with the ground's memory of recent rain.
*label rg_sky
*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  *if (weather_cold)
    Snow drives between the trunks and the canopy roars overhead.
  *else
    Wind thrashes the canopy and rain comes through it in heavy gusts.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  Mist lies between the trunks, and the far end of the track dissolves into grey.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain patters through the leaves and drips from every branch.
*elseif (weather = "Sleet")
  Sleet ticks on the leaves and bounces off the roots.
*elseif (weather = "Snow")
  Snow sifts down between the branches and thins to a few flakes by the time it reaches the ground.
*elseif ((time_period = "Night") or (time_period = "Pre-Dawn"))
  *if (weather = "Clear")
    Moonlight finds the track in a few pale patches, and the rest is black trunks.
  *else
    The canopy shuts out the sky, and the track is a paler strip in the dark.
*elseif (time_period = "Dusk")
  The light is low and orange under the trees, and the shadows between the trunks have run together.
*elseif (weather = "Clear")
  Sunlight falls through the leaves in moving patches.
*else
  Grey light filters down through the canopy and lays no shadows.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The ruts hold standing water from the last long rain.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The ground is soft underfoot from recent rain.
*return

*label rg_talk_health
*set wol_roi_asked_health true
You ask how he is keeping now that the polypox is gone.

"Better than I've any right to be," Roi says, and blows his nose out of habit. "Still ruddy round the edges, but the sneezing's stopped, and my beard's stopped trying to be fur. Do you know what it is to wake up unsure how many legs you've got?"

He laughs, and the laugh thins out. "I'd have made a fine wolf, if I'd stayed one. Not a fine druid, mind. The forest has little patience for a druid who can't hold his own shape."

He rubs his bald head. "I'll not forget who hauled the herbs, ${name}."

*goto rg_menu

*label rg_talk_circle
*set wol_roi_asked_circle true
You ask about his circle and the wolves on the road.

"My circle sent me to Langston years back," he says. "A town needs somebody who talks to the forest for it, and the forest needs somebody who talks back to the town. Mostly it's drains and manure heaps and whose goats got into whose beans." He grins. "Funerals too, and the odd wedding."

"The wolves came down from the hills last winter, thin and hungry, and the road was the nearest thing to a larder. I've been teaching them it isn't. They're slow pupils, but they've good manners about the sausages now."

*goto rg_menu

*label rg_talk_solaine
*set wol_roi_asked_solaine true
You ask how he is taking what happened to Solaine.

Roi is quiet for a long moment, and his hands go still in his lap.

"She gave me the Silverstar herself," he says. "Said druids struggle with curses and she thought I might want one to hand. A gift for a friend, I thought." He looks at his palms. "I've been back through every talk we ever had, hunting for the moment I should've known."

You tell him he couldn't have.

"Maybe not." He closes his hands. "Whatever the court says, I hope someone who cared for her sees her through it. The lass I knew is in there somewhere, and that's what hurts."

*goto rg_menu

*label rg_talk_cleric
*set wol_roi_asked_cleric true
You ask whether he has met the temple's new cleric.

"Edric? Aye." Roi's face brightens. "Decent sort. Overworked, but decent. I took a basket of herbs down for the mortuary stair, and he looked at it like I'd handed him gold."

He chuckles. "The young one, Wynne, asked what a sprig of lavender does for the dead. I told him it's good for the flies and the mourners alike."

*goto rg_menu

*comment Sitting with Roi: ten minutes, one of four pictures by the hour (the pool rotates with the clock), under the overhang in the wet. No reward: time spent in company.
*label rg_sit
*set minutes_to_pass 10
*set time_advance_call_id "rg_sit_1"
*gosub_scene Langston/langston lg_walk_time
*temp rg_sit_v (((campaign_day * 24) + clock_hour) modulo 4)
You sit down near Roi and let him get on with his work.
*if ((weather_severity >= 3) or (weather_wet))
  You settle under the overhang beside him while the rain drums on the rock, and he hands you a strip of bark to twist into cord.
*elseif (rg_sit_v = 0)
  He hands you a bundle of leaves and a twist of grass, and you tie bundles beside him until the pile is gone.
*elseif (rg_sit_v = 1)
  He talks about nothing in particular: the moss on the north side of every tree, and a fox that has learned to open his basket.
*elseif (rg_sit_v = 2)
  He sorts bark into strips by length while the grove's small noises settle around the two of you.
*else
  Roi hums a tuneless song to the saplings and expects no audience, and you listen to it anyway.

After a while you get up, and he lifts a hand as you go.

*goto rg_menu

*comment ── WHERE ROI IS TODAY ── Sets roi_where from the campaign day alone, so a day reads the same on every look and after a reload. h cycles through 0..15 (5 is coprime to 16, so the away days are scattered, never in a run):
*comment h under 4 is a wandering day (one in four); h = 0 is a market day (one in sixteen: he walks to Langston and back). Used by rg_roi here and by the market square in langston.txt (lg_roi_sighting). replay-ok: sets a display variable.
*label rg_roi_where
*temp roi_h (((campaign_day * 5) + 2) modulo 16)
*set roi_where "grove"
*if (roi_h < 4)
  *set roi_where "wandering"
*if (roi_h = 0)
  *set roi_where "market"
*return
