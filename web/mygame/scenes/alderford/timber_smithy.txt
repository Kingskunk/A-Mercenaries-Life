[b]◈ JANNA'S BENCH[/b]

*if (js_day_beat = 0)
  Janna plunges a pair of glowing wedges into the quench trough, sending a hiss of steam rolling across her bench. She wipes soot from her brow with the back of her wrist, glances toward Torvald at the heavy anvil, and gives you a private nod over the rising vapor.

  "Good timing," she says low over the water noise. "The heat's enough to cook my boots through."
*elseif (js_day_beat = 1)
  By the shuttered mill-race door, Janna lifts a tin dipper from the drinking pail and leans against the timber post to catch the cool draft off the weir. When she sees you crossing the floor, the crease between her brows loosens.

  "Don't tell Torvald I'm standing still," she murmurs, holding the dipper out toward you first. "Even iron has to cool between heats."
*elseif (js_day_beat = 2)
  Janna has a rough iron hinge laid out over a piece of chalked plank beside the finished racks, measuring pin holes with a pair of calipers. As you step beside the bench, she shifts slightly to make room for your shoulder against hers.

  "Checking whether the holes line up straight before I punch them," she tells you, tapping the chalk mark with one thumb. "Saves an hour of swearing later."
*else
  At the scrap bin near the door, Janna turns over a bundle of flat iron bars, tapping each one lightly with a small hammer to listen for cracks in the metal. When she spots you standing clear of the forge arc, she sets the bundle down and rests both hands on the bin rim.

  "Didn't expect to catch you in town at this hour," she says, warmth softening the crisp edge of her voice. "You look steadier in daylight than half the carters who come through that door."

*if (janna_last_evening = "axe")
  A timber wedge beside her oilstone already carries two crisp new bevels. "Been testing that grind on harder stock," she adds, catching your glance.
*elseif (janna_last_evening = "buckle")

*elseif (janna_last_evening = "quench")
  The blackened bar at the corner of her rack shows an even blue sheen along the edge.
*elseif (janna_last_evening = "night")
  Her eyes linger on yours for a quiet half-beat longer than a customer's—unhurried and certain.

  # Lend her a hand at the bellows and tongs for a quick heat. [~30 min]
    *if (js_act_beat = 0)
      "Give me the spare tongs," you say, stepping onto the far side of the bench away from Torvald's hammer swing. "I'll hold the collar while you draw it."

      Janna hands over the iron handles with a quick nod. "Mind the wrist turn when I strike." For half an hour you work in tight, wordless rhythm—pumping air into the coals, lifting the glowing band onto the horn, and turning it under her fast, measured blows until the seam shuts cleanly. When she sets the piece into the ash tray to cool, Torvald grunts once from the anvil without looking up, which passes for praise in his forge.

      "Not bad," Janna murmurs, brushing scale from her knuckles as yours brush past. "You haven't forgotten how to time a strike."
    *elseif (js_act_beat = 1)

      Once the iron glows clean yellow, she draws the first bar across to the horn and sights along the eyelet while you work the second pair of tongs to steady the shank under her hammer. Half an hour clears the tray she'd stacked for dusk; Janna drops the finished pins into the water trough with a sharp hiss and taps the rim beside your fingers. "Cleaner eyes than I'd have turned alone," she says quietly.
    *else
      Torvald leaves a twisted cart-tire hoop beside Janna's bench while he steps back to dress a cold chisel, and you catch the far rim with the wide tongs before she has to wrestle it solo. "Tell me which flat needs drawing," you say, bracing your weight against the floorboards.

      For thirty minutes you tilt and brace the heavy iron hoop to her short nods, letting her hammer chase the kink down the curve until the ring sits flat against the bench face. When the last sparks die on the wood, she wipes her cheek on her sleeve and gives a crooked smile. "Saved me an hour of fighting springy iron—and Torvald's lectures about patience."
