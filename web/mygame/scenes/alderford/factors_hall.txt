*if (hall_first)
  The Factor's Hall stands on the upper slope beside the customs yard, with iron-bound doors, a slate roof and narrow windows set above the level of a cart bed. Inside, the river damp gives way to lamp-warm air, cedar shelving and drying ink. A long oak counter divides the public floor from rows of pigeonholes, a locked strongbox and two small offices at the back. Brass balance pans and nested lead weights wait at one end; a notice board of iron-nailed oak hangs beside the door.
*else
  The hall is cool and lamp-warm, smelling of cedar, sealing wax and drying ink. Beyond the counter, the rows of pigeonholes and locked drawers remain just out of reach.

*if (weather_severity >= 3)

*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain taps on the narrow windows, and the flags inside the door are dark with wet boots.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  Fog turns the windows to pale slits and blurs the customs yard beyond them.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Slush has been tracked across the stone flags and gathered in the grooves by the door.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))

*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The stones by the threshold remain dark from the last shower.

*if (not(met_orlov))
  *set met_orlov true
  A narrow man in ink-stained shirtsleeves rises from the desk at the counter's far end. Spectacles sit in thinning grey hair, and a goose quill waits behind one ear. "Chief Clerk Orlov," he says. He looks from you to the door, as though counting the interruptions between here and his next finished page. "If you have an account, a sealed paper, or a contract, bring it to the rail. If you are finding your feet, start with the notices. They are less likely to send you back down the hill."

  *choice
    # Ask what the hall handles for Alderford.
      You rest a hand on the rail. "What passes through this hall?"

      Orlov sets his pen across the page. "The town sends boards, fish and grain downriver. This hall catches the argument after: what was loaded, what was paid, and whose mark proves it. Tolls, storage bonds, letters of credit—the dull things that stop two merchants from calling each other thieves in the yard."
      *page_break Go on to the public floor…
      *goto af_hall_floor
    # Ask whether there is work posted today.
      You glance toward the notice board. "Is there work posted today?"

      Orlov glances at the board, then back at you. "Nothing unclaimed that I would promise you. Still, read it. The river makes liars of a quiet morning often enough, and a posting can go up before the ink on the last one has dried."
      *page_break Go on to the public floor…
      *goto af_hall_floor
    # Say you are only looking around.
      You give the room a measured look. "Only looking around."

      One corner of Orlov's mouth moves, not quite a smile. "A sensible use of a morning. The board is for the public; the drawers are where sensible mornings go to die."
      *page_break Go on to the public floor…
      *goto af_hall_floor

*else

*elseif (hall_roll = 2)

*else

      Orlov dusts sand over the fresh ink before he answers. "Boards, mostly, when the yards are full. Cured fish while it will keep. Grain in sealed lots when the granary can spare it. The Grey is cheaper than wheels, provided the person steering knows where the current means to put them."
