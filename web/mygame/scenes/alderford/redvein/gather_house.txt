[b]◈ THE GATHER HOUSE[/b]
*line_break
The Gather House is a square stone room with a stove at its centre and benches fixed along the walls. A long table stands below a notice rail, where hauling lists, requests for hands and short messages are held under wooden slats. Beside the door, a narrow shelf holds spare lamps, folded blankets and a tin box for the collections made when a household needs help.

*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  The door has been pulled tight against the weather. The stove burns high, and wet boots and dripping coats crowd the stone floor by the entrance.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain ticks against the small windows and runs in dark lines down the outside of the door.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  The windows show only the nearer cottages and the pale blur of the road beyond them.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Meltwater gathers on the threshold stone and the room smells faintly of wet wool.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The floor near the door remains dark with old damp, and grit has been ground into the joints between the stones.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The threshold has dried to a reddish film of mud and ore dust.

*if (rv_hall_first)
  A middle-aged human woman sits at the table nearest the stove, writing names in a narrow book. Her dark hair is streaked with white at the temples and tied beneath a wool cap; a thin scar crosses one cheek, and red dust has settled in the folds of her rolled sleeves. She looks up as you enter.

  "Mara Venn," she says, closing the book over one finger. "Read the rail if that is why you came in. If you need a name put up, or a place out of the weather, tell me before you help yourself."
*else
  Mara Venn sits at the nearest table with the notices and tin box within reach.

  # Ask Mara about the blankets and collection box.
    You nod toward the folded blankets and the tin box by the door. "Are those for people hurt at the Eastcut?"

    Mara sets down her pen. "For anyone a bad day leaves short. If the work below goes wrong, the people on the spot get everyone clear. After that, the yard closes until the ones who know the ground say otherwise."

    You glance at the benches and the notice rail. "And the rest gets settled here?"

    "Pay, repairs, who can spare a hand," Mara says. "The whole village can hear the argument here. The box is for the gap a bad day leaves behind. It's never enough, but it's better than leaving a household to find out alone."
