[b]◈ THE CHAPEL OF SAINT ALTHEA[/b]
*line_break

*comment ── LAYER 1: place. ──
*if (ac_first)
  Worn limestone steps climb from the mill bank to a small chapel on the ridge above the weir. Its oak doors stand beneath an imperial arch, the lettering softened by weather: [i]The Chapel of Saint Althea the Mender.[/i] A solar cross entwined with a flax spindle is cut beside the words. Inside, the thick stone walls turn the roar of the river into a low, steady presence. The nave is narrow and warm with beeswax, lanolin and cedar smoke. At its head, Saint Althea stands in limestone beside a plain altar, a weaving shuttle in one hand and a bowl of spring water in the other. A parish larder and six hand-looms fill the side aisles, and smooth river pebbles lie among the candle stubs at the altar rail.
*else
  The chapel holds its familiar warmth above the weir: limestone, beeswax, wool and cedar smoke, with the river reduced to a low pulse beyond the walls.
*set ac_first false

*comment ── LAYER 2: light, weather and the ground's memory. ──
*if (ac_night)
  A pair of oil lamps keeps the altar and the nearest looms in a gentle amber circle, while the upper rafters disappear into shadow.
*elseif (weather_severity >= 3)
  The narrow windows shake in their lead frames, and the chapel's lamps are lit against the weather-dark.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain makes pale tracks down the outer face of the narrow windows, and their light lies soft across the flags.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  Fog has turned the glass to an even pearl-grey, leaving the candles to give the nave most of its light.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  A cold grey light comes through the windows, but the hearth stones and packed wool keep the nave warm.
*elseif (time_period = "Dusk")
  The last light catches the statue's bowl and the brass pins along the loom frames.
*else
  Daylight falls in narrow bars across the stone floor and makes the dust of flax fibre visible above the looms.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  Earlier rain has left the steps outside dark and shining, and damp wool hangs near the door to dry.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The entry flags remain dark from the last rain, though the hearth has dried the rest of the nave.

*comment ── LAYER 3: people and work. ──
*if (ac_night)
  Two parish guests sleep near the rear wall beneath borrowed blankets, and one small loom waits half-threaded beside them. The rest of the chapel is quiet.
*elseif (ac_pick = 1)
  The looms clatter steadily in the side aisle while a parish worker sorts spun yarn by weight near the larder door.
*elseif (ac_pick = 2)
  A person at the altar rail ties a fresh strip of linen into the old knots, presses it once between both hands, and leaves without speaking.
*else
  A kettle murmurs near the hearth. Two people carry folded cloth from the looms to the larder, careful not to block the narrow aisle.

*if (not(met_corbel))
  *set met_corbel true
  An elderly deacon in an undyed wool habit looks up from a small chancel desk. A limestone spindle-cross rests against his chest, and his spectacles are tied behind his ears with hemp cord. "Peace to your path," he says. "I am Corbel. This roof is open to anyone who leaves their quarrel outside."
*elseif (delivered_chapel_grain)
  Corbel looks up from the chancel desk and gives you a warm, familiar nod. "The larder has not forgotten Bran's rye, nor the person who carried it up those steps," he says. "Come in from the road."
*elseif (corbel_charter_argument)
  Corbel closes the parish ledger when he sees you. "The old clause is copied safe into the charter roll," he says quietly. "Your wall is still standing where we need it."
*else
  Deacon Corbel works at the chancel desk beneath the statue, his ledger shut for the moment and his spindle-cross resting against his habit.

You kneel at the limestone rail. Corbel lays a clean square of linen over the worst of the wound, then presses his cool spindle-cross lightly against it.

"What is torn may be re-woven," he says, and begins the old Meridian invocation.

Warmth spreads beneath the linen. The immediate pain loosens its hold, then recedes; when Corbel lifts the cloth, only a faint gold thread remains where the blood had been.

Corbel exhales and steadies himself on the rail. "Walk carefully. Mending is not the same thing as being unbreakable."

*label af_chapel_vigil
*if (chapel_blessing_received)
  You kneel at the rail and rest your forearms on the worn stone. You know the chapel's silence now: the looms, the warm lamps and the river held at a distance by thick stone. You ask for that stillness plainly and let it be enough.
*else
  You kneel at the rail and rest your forearms on the worn stone. The chapel gives you a silence unlike the road's empty stretches: a quiet made by people working, sheltering and leaving a light on for one another.

The clack of the looms, the warmth of the lamps and the river's low rush settle into a single steady measure. Corbel does not interrupt. Before you rise, he touches two fingers to the bowl of spring water and makes a small blessing over the rail.

"May the stone hold beneath your tread," he says. "May your hand stay from harm when mercy will serve."

*label af_chapel_talk_sanctuary
*set af_chapel_talk_corbel true
*if (delivered_chapel_grain)
  You glance toward the larder, then back to Corbel. "How has the parish held since Bran's rye came up the steps?"

  Corbel's eyes follow yours. "It gave us time. That is what sound food does when a household is close to the edge: it gives people time to mend, find work, or simply make it through a bad week without selling the wrong thing." He folds his hands over the ledger. "The larder is never full enough, but it was not empty when it mattered."
*elseif (corbel_charter_argument)
  You keep your voice low beside the desk. "Has the old charter clause helped the parish?"

  Corbel nods once. "It has helped us prepare, which is not the same as winning. The copy stays quiet in the roll until someone gives us cause to use it. But the Factor's Hall is no longer the only side with paper." His hand rests on the ledger. "That changes how a person sleeps."
*else
  You glance toward the occupied looms and lower your voice. "How does this place stay open with so many people under one roof?"

  Corbel follows your gaze. "The parish sells cloth, pays the town's old commutation, and makes room where it can. Some call that charity. Most days it is work, done together, with fewer people left alone in the cold."

  He touches the spindle-cross at his chest. "The sanctuary is not a promise that no hardship reaches these doors. It is a promise that nobody here is handed over lightly."
