[b]◈ JANNA'S SIDE DOOR[/b]
*line_break

A narrow side door sits behind the smithy, tucked beside the chimney where the mill-race noise fades into the river below. A little warmth reaches the latch from the stones behind it.

*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  The weather presses hard against the outer wall, but the chimney gives off a steady heat.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain ticks against the eaves and gutters along the smithy's rear wall.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  Fog has softened the mill-race to a low, steady rush beyond the buildings.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Meltwater taps from the eaves into the dark behind the smithy.

*choice
  # Knock and tell Janna you came to see her.
    You knock twice on the side door. When the bolt draws back, you say, "I came to see you, if this is a good time."

    Janna opens the door only wide enough to stand in it at first. The forge soot has been washed from her face, though a dark smudge still shadows one thumb. At your words, her mouth curves. "You picked a better time than shouting over Torvald's hammer. Come in, then."
    *goto af_janna_alcove_reason
  # Knock and ask whether you have caught her at a bad time.
    You knock twice on the side door. When Janna opens it, you keep a respectful step back. "Have I caught you at a bad time? I can leave you to your evening."

    Janna studies you for a moment, then unhooks the door farther. "You wouldn't be standing here if you wanted to leave. Come in. If I needed the door shut, I'd say so."
    *goto af_janna_alcove_reason
  # Knock, then decide not to interrupt her evening.
    You knock twice on the side door. When Janna opens it, you give a small shake of your head. "I only wanted to say I was thinking of you. I'll let you keep your evening."

    Janna leans on the frame. "That's kind. Come back when you want to talk, not only when you think you should." She waits until you have turned toward the street before closing the door.

*label af_janna_alcove_reason
The adjoining hearth alcove is small and plainly kept: a narrow table, two chairs, a bed tucked against the warm chimney stones, and a shelf of tools that have followed Janna home from the forge. A kettle waits on the hearth.

*choice
  # Tell her you wanted to know how she is doing, away from the workbench.
    *set janna_first_private_tone "care"
    You take the chair she offers. "I wanted to know how you're doing when nobody needs a pin, a rim, or a miracle from the forge."

    Janna puts water over the coals. "Most people ask after the work. That's fair enough; the work feeds me. But it's good to be asked the other question." She glances back at you. "I'm tired. I'm all right."
    *goto af_janna_alcove_ambition
  # Tell her you wanted time together that was not borrowed from her work.
    *set janna_first_private_tone "company"
    You sit opposite her and say, "I wanted a little time with you that was not borrowed from the forge."

    Janna's fingers rest on the kettle handle. "That sounds simple when you say it." Her smile comes slowly. "It isn't a thing I've had much practice making room for."
    *goto af_janna_alcove_ambition
  # Admit that you were not sure you were welcome, but wanted to ask.
    *set janna_first_private_tone "caution"
    You look to the open door, then back to her. "I was not sure I was welcome. I wanted to ask instead of deciding for you."

    Janna closes the door with her heel and takes the other chair. "That is a better start than guessing. You are welcome tonight."
    *goto af_janna_alcove_ambition

*label af_janna_alcove_ambition
The kettle begins to whisper. Janna pours the hot water into two cups, then settles across from you with both hands around her own.

"Torvald taught me more than he says aloud," she tells you. "The forge is good work, and I'm proud of it. But I want a bench with my own mark cut into the iron. Not because his work's lacking. Because I want to know what I can build under my own name."

*choice
  # Ask what that would take.
    You lean closer over your cup. "What would it take to get there?"

    Janna gives a short laugh. "Coin enough for a lease, good iron, and a roof that won't fall when the river rises. More than that, a few people willing to trust my mark before it's familiar." She taps the table once. "Not tonight's problem. Still mine."
  # Tell her you would like to see that forge when she has it.
    You meet her eyes. "I'd like to see that forge when it's yours."

    Janna looks down into her cup, then back up. "Then you had better keep travelling back through Alderford. I would hate to finish it and find you missed the opening."
  # Tell her she does not need to prove her work to you.
    You rest your hands around the warmth of the cup. "You don't need to prove your work to me. I've seen what you can do."

    Janna holds your gaze. "That matters. It doesn't make the wanting go away, though." Her smile returns, smaller this time. "Maybe it makes it easier to carry."

*if (weather_severity >= 3)

*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The lane behind the smithy is dark with damp from the recent rain, and a trace of wet earth clings to the threshold step.

  You sit together with your shoulders touching while the heat settles in the bricks behind you.

  "Thanks for staying," she says quietly. "The shop closes easier when I'm not the only one locking it."
