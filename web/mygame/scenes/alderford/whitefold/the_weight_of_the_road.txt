The Limestone Cut road leaves the common between the sheep pens and climbs along the shoulder of the slope. You hear the bend before you reach it: men shouting in short bursts, a dog barking without pause, and under it a dry grinding of stone on stone.

At the bend, the downhill half of the road is gone. About twenty feet of the wall that held it up has folded outward and slid away down the slope, and the cart track ends in a ragged shelf with wheel ruts running out into air. Pale facing stones lie scattered through torn turf and grey fill. The covered drain that carried ditch water out under the road once opened through that wall, and its mouth is buried now beneath fallen blocks. Brown water is gathering in the ditch on the uphill side and seeping into the broken edge of the road.

The wall beyond the gap still stands, bellied out over the drop. Every time a boot or a wheel crosses the road above it, chalk crumbs and wet grit trickle from between its stones.

A loaded quarry cart has slewed toward the edge, one wheel sunk to its hub and the bed tilted downhill. Squared pale blocks are stacked on the bed, and they slide a finger's width each time the team lunges. Four men with chalk to the elbows haul on a rope made fast to the cart's rear post, their boots skating in the mud.

Hessa Vail stands at the tail of the cart with her notebook crushed under one arm. "That's the weir order for the Factor's Hall!" she bawls at the men. "Every block on that bed is paid for already! Hold her!"

Below the road, the flock has scattered over the rubble slope. Some animals are wedged among the fallen stones, some are bunched in a hollow, and a dozen or so are bolting downhill with two dogs and a pair of shepherds cutting after them. Nerin Bale stands among the stones with one arm raised, turning what he can. "The old ewe with the red daub on her neck!" he shouts up the slope. "Turn her and the rest will follow!"

Edda Cale is waving the loose hands back from the bowed wall. "Off it! Everyone off it, or it goes the same way!"
