[b]◈ THE GRAIN GREEN[/b]
*line_break
The Green is a long, open strip of packed earth beside the High Grey Road. A beam scale stands under a roof of rough boards, its iron hooks black with use. Rye comes in from the high fields here to be weighed before it goes on to the drying floors, the mill or the raised storehouses behind the fence. Around the road-facing edge, simple timber frames hold canvas awnings for ordinary market stalls, with low stone blocks for sample sacks, baskets and goods brought in from the surrounding farms. The storehouses rise behind the fence with their loading doors above head height and their lower walls rubbed pale by carts passing close.

  *elseif (marlcross_milling_fair_phase = "contest")

  *elseif (marlcross_milling_fair_phase = "afternoon")

*if ((time_period = "Night") or (time_period = "Pre-Dawn"))
  The market is shut, but the road remains open along its edge.
*elseif (mc_market_first)
  Rye sacks stand in small groups beneath the scale awnings, each tied at the neck with cord or a strip of cloth. Along the road, other sellers set out roots, eggs, cheese, bread, cloth and small household goods beside their carts. People lift grain into their palms to compare colour and weight, then carry their sacks toward the scale or back to their carts.
*else
  Rye moves through the Green in a slow exchange of lifted sacks, weighed loads and carts leaving room for one another along the road. Beside that larger trade, the awnings hold the small business of the day: farm produce, bread, cloth, repairs and travellers' goods changing hands.
