[b]◈ THE CROSS HALL[/b]
*line_break
The hall is one long room of dark beams and whitewashed walls, with a cold hearth at one end and three heavy tables pushed together beneath the windows. Benches run along the walls. A rail beside the door holds folded notices under wooden slats: a strayed animal, a cart wanted for a northbound load, a meeting called when enough people can leave their work. The bell rope hangs just inside the entrance.

*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  The outer door is shut against the weather, and the hall's hearth has been lit. Wind presses at the shutters while wet footprints darken the floorboards by the entrance.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain beats a soft, steady rhythm on the roof and runs from the eaves past the hall windows.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  The windows show only the pale blur of the crossing and the nearer market rails.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Meltwater ticks from the eaves, and pale slush gathers on the threshold stones.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The floor by the door is still dark with old mud and the hall holds the smell of damp wool.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  Fine dust has begun to settle over the last tracked-in mud on the boards.

*if (mc_hall_first)
  A lean older human woman sits at the table nearest the door, sorting folded notes into small piles. Her brown hair, threaded with grey, is bound at the nape; deep lines pull at the corners of her weather-browned eyes, and the knuckles of both hands are enlarged from old work. She looks up as you enter.

  "Sella Morren," she says. "I hold the elder's chair. If you've business with the village, say it plain. If you're here for notices, they're by the door."

  # Ask Sella how village business is decided.
    You wait until Sella sets one note down. "When people disagree here, who decides it?"

    Sella folds her hands over the table's edge. "The people who'll live beside the answer. We hear both sides, then the elder and council put a choice before the room. Slower than shouting, but the fields are still here after."

    You nod toward the notices. "And the road work?"

    "Road work follows the same rule," she says. "A broken road belongs to everyone who needs it."
