*label bw_daughter_open
The kitchen door opens just far enough for a young woman to step through with a stack of bowls against her hip. She moves carefully, as though each one might break, then stops when she sees Nessa watching her.

"Iria," Nessa says, not unkindly. "That table has been waiting on those bowls longer than it has patience."

Iria Cale starts, reddens, and carries them on. When she returns, she catches you looking and lets out a small, embarrassed breath.

"I'm sorry," she says. "I keep making work of things that ought to be simple."

After a glance toward her mother, she lowers her voice. "Tovin Rusk has asked me to marry him. His father runs a small tanyard just past the salt store. I want to say yes. My mother says I'll stop coming home once I do."

"I said no such thing," Nessa calls from the counter. "I said people make new lives, and old tables go quiet."

Iria's fingers tighten around the edge of a bowl. "Tovin's place is across the bank, not across the sea. But she won't hear it from me."

Nessa sets down the cloth she has been using on the counter. Up close, the salt cracks across the backs of her hands look deep enough to hurt.

"You heard us," she says. "Then you know the whole of it. Tovin is decent. His father is decent. The tanyard isn't even a ten-minute walk."

She looks past you, toward Iria's bent head at the kitchen door.

"But marriage changes the shape of a family. First it's suppers missed because there's work at the other hearth. Then it's a visit when there's time. Then one day you realize your child treats your home as a stopover. I can't bring myself to hand her over to that."

Nessa turns toward the kitchen. "Iria. Send a boy to the Rusks' tanyard. Tell Tovin to come here before I find an excuse to change my mind."

For a breath Iria only stares. Then she laughs once, sharply, and throws both arms around her mother. Nessa holds her tightly despite pretending not to know what to do with her hands.

When Iria finally turns back to you, her worry has gone out of her face so completely that she seems like another person.

"You've given us more than a yes," she says. "You've given us a beginning." She presses a room key into your hand. "Nessa says the next room is yours. No arguments."

Nessa clears her throat. "One room. One night. Don't make a tradition of it."

Iria smiles at that. "Come back through Brinewick when you can. I know every salt-carrier, lighter crew, and road hand who comes through this house. If there's word worth finding, I'll help you find it."

*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  The weather has driven the wedding indoors, but not dampened it. The Reedhouse tables have been pushed back beneath strings of salt-white ribbon, and the Tide House bell has been borrowed to ring from the sheltered yard gate.
*else
  The Tide House steps have been swept clear above the flats. Salt-white ribbon knots flutter from the bell frame, and the brine beds shine beyond the gathered families.

Iria stands in a new blue wool dress with Nessa beside her, holding one hand so tightly that neither seems to notice. Across from them, Tovin Rusk wears a clean dark coat, his father at his shoulder with the quiet pride of a man who has spent his life working hide and salt into things meant to last.

When the celebrant asks what they promise, Iria looks first at Tovin and then at her mother.

"A place at the fire," she says. "And a way back to it."

Nessa laughs through the tears she has not managed to hide. Tovin repeats the promise, then takes Iria's hands in his. The bell rings, the gathered people answer it with cheers, and a fiddle begins before its final note has faded.

Iria spots you at the edge of the crowd. She lifts her joined hand with Tovin's in thanks, and Nessa gives you a single, firm nod.
