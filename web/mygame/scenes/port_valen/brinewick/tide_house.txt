[b]◈ THE TIDE HOUSE[/b]
*line_break
Stone steps lead up to the Tide House door, raising its main room above the level of the bank path. Inside, benches line the whitewashed walls beneath narrow windows. A long table stands under a board of public notices, and a marked post beside the hearth carries dark lines showing past flood heights. Coiled rope, spare lanterns and folded blankets are stored in a chest beneath the stair.

*if (weather_severity >= 3)
  The outer door is shut against the weather. The hearth burns high, and wet boots and dripping oilskins crowd the space by the entrance.
*elseif (weather = "Rain")
  Rain drums on the roof and draws grey lines down the windows facing the flats.
*elseif (weather = "Fog")
  The view through the narrow windows ends at the nearest reed beds and bank rails.
*elseif ((weather = "Snow") or (weather = "Sleet"))
  Meltwater ticks from the step outside, and slush gathers below the lower stair.

*if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The room still holds a faint smell of wet wool and river mud from the last long rain.
*elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))
  The stair treads have begun to dry, leaving pale salt marks around the door.

*if (bw_tide_first)
  A short, square-built human woman stands at the table with one hand resting on a folded notice. Her weather-browned face is furrowed around pale grey eyes; silver hair is pinned tight at the back of her head, and her heavy wool coat is rubbed shiny at the elbows. She studies you a moment before speaking.

  "Hesta Rill," she says. "I keep the elder's chair here. If the water rises, this house stays open. If you have village business, the notices are there and the table is here."
*else
  Hesta Rill is at the long table with notices, lamp oil and spare rope arranged within reach.

  # Ask Hesta who decides when the tide gates close.
    You nod toward the marked post by the hearth. "Who decides when the gates close?"

    Hesta points to the highest dark line before meeting your eyes again. "The water does. We watch the marks, the wind and the channel. When it is time, whoever is closest shuts the gate. Then we sort the rest together."

    "No waiting for a vote?" you ask.

    "A vote does not stop water," Hesta says. "It only decides who repairs what after."
