[b]A KEY FOR EVERY DOOR[/b]
*line_break
You have been under Kess's roof long enough to know its habits: the sweep of the stair before first bell, the quiet click of doors that turn twice, the smell of beeswax rising from the hall. This afternoon those habits have broken into sharp voices.

Inside Terrace Lodgings, a narrow-shouldered woman in an ink-stained sleeve stands before Kess's desk with both hands flat on the oak. A brass engraver's loupe hangs at her throat. Vael has come in from his shop, his apron still dusted with filings.

"My door was locked when I came back," the woman says. "My window was barred. Yet my work slips were spread across the table, and the little brass seal my sister sent me is gone. Not my purse. Not a thing worth selling. Just that."

"Hesta Rook, engraver," Kess says to you, clipped and formal. "Her room is one of mine. I will not call her careless merely because the lock did its work."

Vael looks at the key in Hesta's palm, then at the closed stairwell. "And I will not call a lock failed when no one has shown me a mark from pick or wedge."

  # "Kess, who could have had cause to enter that room?"
    Kess's eyes harden. "No one with my leave. I keep the spare keys in my office and do not hand them around."

  # "Vael, tell me what your lock rules out."
    Vael takes the key between finger and thumb. "A wedge leaves the strike scarred. A pick leaves the ward bruised if the hand is hurried. Both make a lock complain. If the bolt is clean, someone opened it with a key that belonged there."
    *goto a_key_offer

    Kess gives one short nod. "Then keep your own key close."

*label a_key_decoy
You lay the pieces out without softening them: clean locks, keys briefly collected under false notices, and a runner using Vael's name. The rooms were not the job. They were proof that copied keys could open several of Vael's different cuts before someone risked a larger door.

Vael's mouth thins. "Hollis has one of my counter locks. His daughters keep the day money in a little iron room behind the shop. That is the next door a buyer would want patterned."

Kess reaches for the tenant book. "Then we warn Hollis and shut every door in this close."

Before she can rise, the lodging door bangs open. The slight boy from the descriptions stumbles into the hall with a brass-coloured thread at his wrist and a copied key held up between two shaking fingers.

"This one will turn," he calls toward the stairs, too loudly. "Vael sold you a lock that only looks strong. I can show everyone."

Hesta recoils. Vael strides past you into the courtyard, fury overtaking caution. "Give me that key. Now."

The boy's eyes flick to the open workshop. He looks less triumphant than trapped.
