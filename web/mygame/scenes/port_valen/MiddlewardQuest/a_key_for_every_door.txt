*line_break
Terrace Lodgings runs on rigid habit: the broom swept down the stair carpet before first bell, the double click of brass deadbolts, the smell of beeswax and dried lavender rising from the hall. Today that quiet has a crack in it. Voices carry down from Mistress Kess's desk, low and tight.

Three people stand around it. Master Vael is there with his apron still dusted with filings, his arms folded. Beside him is a narrow-shouldered woman in an ink-stained sleeve, a small brass magnifying lens hanging at her throat, both hands flat on the oak. Kess has her tenant book open and her pen laid across it.

"Mistress Kess. Master Vael," you say.

Vael gives you a short nod. The woman keeps her eyes on the desk.

"${name}." Kess says it the way she would set a coin on a counter. "Good. This is Hesta Rook, engraver at the mint. She has a room on the second floor. I do not believe you two have met."

"Good day," Hesta says. Her voice is level and her knuckles are white against the wood.

*label a_key_briefing

Kess lays two fingers on the tenant book. "Hesta came back to her room today and found it entered. I cannot say by whom."

"My door was locked when I came back," Hesta says. "My window was barred. I keep my work slips squared in a stack, and they were fanned across the table like a hand of cards. Someone has read every one. And the little brass seal my sister sent me is gone."

"Her room is one of mine," Kess says. "I have no wish to call Hesta careless, and I cannot call Master Vael's lock faulty. That leaves me a door that was locked, a room that was entered, and no explanation for either."

Vael unfolds his arms. He looks at the key lying in Hesta's palm, then toward the stairwell. "I fitted that deadbolt myself, Mistress Kess, and I stand behind it. I will not call a lock failed when no one has shown me a mark from pick or wedge. Nor will I hear it said in the street that my work does not hold."

Both of them turn to you, as though you were the one person in the hall who had not yet taken a side.

*label a_key_decoy
You lay the pieces out without softening them: clean locks, keys briefly collected under false notices, and a runner using Vael's name. The rooms were not the job. They were proof that copied keys could open several of Vael's different cuts before someone risked a larger door.

Vael's mouth thins. "Every lock I have fitted in the Middle Ward is written in my pattern book: the cut of each key, the depth of every notch. It lives in the locked drawer under my counter. Hollis has one of my counter locks. His daughters keep the day money in a little iron room behind the shop. That is the next door a buyer would want copied."

Kess reaches for the tenant book. "Then we warn Hollis and shut every door in this close."

Before she can rise, the lodging door bangs open. The slight boy from the descriptions stumbles into the hall with a brass-coloured thread at his wrist and a copied key held up between two shaking fingers.

"This one will turn," he calls toward the stairs, too loudly. "Vael sold you a lock that only looks strong. I can show everyone."

Hesta recoils. Vael strides past you into the courtyard, fury overtaking caution. "Give me that key. Now."

The boy's eyes flick to the open workshop. He looks less triumphant than trapped.
