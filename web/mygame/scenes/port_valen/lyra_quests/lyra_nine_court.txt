The address leads you and Lyra off Lantern Lane into a narrow flagstone cut squeezed between a cobbler's loft and a shed. A faded wooden sign bearing a brass magnifying glass hangs crooked above a heavy oak door.

Inside, the shop smells of dry paper, lamp oil, and old wool. Glass-fronted cabinets crowd the walls, crammed with tarnished silver plate, chipped clay seals, and stacks of mildewed estate inventories. At a scarred workbench sits a stooped man in his late fifties with thinning sandy-grey hair, ink-stained knuckles, and thick brass-rimmed spectacles perched on a crooked nose. He wears a frayed brown wool coat with leather elbow patches and a heavy ring of iron keys at his waist.

He looks up as the latch clicks, his eyes squinting behind the thick lenses. "I'm closed to casual trade," he says curtly. "Try the market square."

"Angel sent us," Lyra says, stepping up to the bench and laying the folded address slip beside his scale.

Simeon Penn looks down at Angel's handwriting, then lifts his head to both of you with a quiet sigh. "Of course she did. Lock the door, then, and show me what she couldn't price herself."

Lyra unwraps the oilcloth bundle and sets the spool of grey cord on the scarred wood.

Simeon picks up a pair of brass tweezers, clips an inch of the stiff thread, and slides it beneath a heavy magnifying loupe on a swing-arm stand. He uncorks a small brown phial, touches a single drop of clear solvent to the cut end, and leans in close.

His bored clerk's posture stiffens. For a long moment, the only sound in the room is the tick of a wall clock and the faint hiss of the solvent eating into the resin.

"Where did you pull this?" Simeon asks quietly, without looking up from the lens.

"A dead woman's floorboards in Dredge-End," Lyra replies. "What is it?"

Simeon straightens slowly, pushing his spectacles up his nose. "The flax is reverse-twisted around a core of cured bone ash and bitter myrrh-gum. That's not guild work, and it isn't from the Meridian Empire either, not the one we fought thirty years ago." He taps the bench lightly with one ink-stained finger. "This is First Dynasty technique. Dawn-era binding work."

"People in the city are still making it," you say.

"Someone is paying to have it made," Simeon corrects grimly. "And I can't tell you who from a single spool. I'll need a little time to boil down the resin residue and discreetly cross-check import manifests with an old contact at the customs tower. If anyone is importing something like this, it'll leave a paper trail."
