Lyra sits on the edge of her end cot in the Vanguard barracks, her dark wool cloak folded over her knee and a leather satchel resting beside her. Her recurve bow is cased and strapped tight against the wall pegs.

She looks up as you approach down the aisle, her expression tighter than usual. "${name}," she says quietly, glancing toward the empty bunks nearby before dropping her voice. "Got a minute?"

You pull up an empty crate beside her cot. "What happened?"

"I've been digging into my aunt's death since I squared up with you," she says, resting her forearms on her knees. "Asking around the lower terraces, checking what was left of her things, following up on who was bringing her work before she died." She pauses, her jaw setting. "I found some troubling stuff, ${name}. Whatever she got tangled up in, it isn't ordinary slum trouble, and it's bigger than one street crew."

She meets your eyes squarely. "I can't do this alone. Not without getting myself cornered before I know who I'm dealing with. I need another pair of hands, someone I can trust to watch my back."

The brass bell over the blue door gives a dry click as you step off Lamp Stair into the Open Hand. Inside, daylight filters across clean woven mats and cedar shelves stacked with tagged tools, wrapped bundles, and a small pair of child's boots hung by their laces.

Lyra is leaning one elbow on the felted counter beside the balance and black touchstone, speaking in a low voice with the woman behind it. The shopkeeper wears a tailored plum velvet waistcoat over cream silk, her glossy raven curls pinned up with a pierced brass comb. Both of them look toward the door as you step inside.

"There you are," Lyra says, straightening up.

*label nine_court_pawn_backroom
*set scenery_subplace "generic_domestic_cellar"

The stairs drop into a dry, stone-walled cellar room beneath the shop. Unlike the clutter upstairs, this room is spare and orderly: a broad oak sorting table sits under a shaded oil lamp, flanked by locked iron chests and racks of sealed wooden cases. The air smells of cedar oil, lamp smoke, and old paper.

Angel takes a seat on the edge of the table and folds her arms, while Lyra unbuckles her leather satchel and lays a small, oilcloth wrapped bundle onto the wood between you.

Lyra peels back the cloth. Inside lies a half-wound wooden spool of coarse, dark-grey cord that smells faintly of bitter resin and cold ash, beside a thin disk of tarnished bronze stamped with nine interlocking rings.

"I pulled the spool out of a loose floorboard beneath my aunt's loom," Lyra says quietly. "The bronze piece was wedged in the crack behind it. I brought both to Angel because if anyone in this city knows what strange goods are worth, it's her."

Angel taps one manicured nail against the bronze disk. "The cord isn't dock hemp or wool," Angel says, her usual warmth cooling into crisp business. "And that stamp isn't a merchant guild mark. I've seen that nine-ring seal pass through private hands up on the Avenue twice in the last five years. Old families."
