*label lyra_hook

Past the red lanterns, the lanes pinch narrow and the timber tenements lean out over the canal on dark shored piles. You take the outside stair with the evening foot traffic, boots hollow on the weathered treads. The walkway at the top sags underfoot, and as you step past the stairhead landing, voices cut through the lapping of the canal below.

A lean man in a steward's apron, grease-stained and grey at the seams, stands blocking Lyra near the railing. Her recurve bow is slung over one shoulder, her arms crossed tight across her chest. In the steward's hand is a folded parchment, creased soft along the edges from being opened and refolded a dozen times.

"Your aunt died four months back, Lyra." His tone is flat, delivered with the dull patience of an eviction speech recited all week. "She was the tenant on the roll. The lease is in her name, the arrears keep piling up, and by city law the rent-office can't clear the rooms while the term is live. The landlord made me check. I checked in spring, I checked last month, and my hands are tied."

"I understand." Her jaw sets hard.

"You think I want to turn two bairns into the mud?" He stuffs the paper into his apron pocket. "Half the canal is sleeping under duckboards as it is. I asked the clerk for leniency on the arrears. They told me no. A dead spinner off the lower terraces isn't their balance sheet."

"How long?"

"Term's up at month's end." He looks her square in the face. "You want those rooms kept, find the silver yourself. I won't carry another petition down from the heights."

He turns and heads down the wooden stairs, his boots thumping in a steady rhythm until the lane swallows him.

You step onto the landing as the steward's footsteps fade down the stair.

"${name}."

Some of the tension leaves her face when she sees you, though she keeps her chin up. "Heard that, did you?"

"Enough."

*label lyra_hook_greet_neutral

You reach the landing as the steward rounds the corner below. Lyra tracks his coat into the gloom before turning your way, her posture guarded, bow gripped tight against her shoulder.

"${name}." Her eyes narrow slightly, measuring. "What are you doing out past the lamps?"

"Heard the steward."

She watches you for a beat, unmoving. Her knuckles tighten on the bow stave, then ease a fraction.

"Family business," she says, her tone flat and final. "Nothing that concerns the file."

The answer is rehearsed. She does not leave, though, and after a beat she goes on.

"Term's up at month's end, and after that the arrears are just a debt with nobody attached to it." She turns the bow a quarter turn in her hand. "Five silver keeps two children in rooms they can sleep in. I've not got it. Vane's chest is a fortnight behind and I don't ask in front of the file."

"I can get you the five silver."

Her eyes narrow slightly. "You're serious?"

"Wouldn't have offered if I wasn't."

She watches you for a moment, like she's waiting for the rest of it. When it doesn't come, some of the stiffness leaves her shoulders.

"Right."

Her thumb runs once along the worn grip of her bow.

"Just... don't make a thing of it. When you've got the silver, find me in quarters."

She starts toward the gangway, then glances back.

"And ${name}?"

"Yeah?"

"Thanks."

The word seems to cost her something. She turns before you can answer and heads for the compound gate, boots knocking against the old boards.

She nods once, slow and deliberate, her features settling back into guarded stillness.

"Aye. Well." She hitches the bowstrap higher on her shoulder and glances toward the rooftops. "Most bastards invent excuses. You said it straight. I can respect that."

She pushes off the rail and heads toward the compound, swallowed by the evening mist without looking back.

You make your way down the barracks aisle with the coin pouch tucked close in your ${hands_prose}. No sense flashing silver in a crowded ward.

Lyra sits on the end cot, her bow across her knees, running a thumbnail along the fletching of an arrow. She looks up as you approach.

"${name}."

You stop beside her and hold out the five silver marks.

Her eyes drop to your hand.

"You're actually giving it to me, then?"

"I said I would."

For once, she doesn't have an answer ready.

She sets the arrow aside and holds out her hand. You tip the silver into her palm, keeping the exchange low between you.

A few bunks down, someone works a blade against an oilstone. The steady scrape of metal fills the silence.

Lyra looks at the coins for a long moment, then closes her fingers around them.

"Five marks," she says quietly.

"Five marks."

Her jaw works once.

"That's..." She stops herself and shakes her head. "All right."

She tucks the silver deep inside her doublet and pulls the lacing closed.

Then she looks at you.

"I meant what I said before. This isn't charity."

"I know."

"I'll pay you back."

"You don't have to."

"I know."

There's a warning in the way she says it.

You leave it there.

"Two weeks," she says. "Find me then."

She looks up from her bow, her gaze dropping to your empty hands before lifting back to your face. Her jaw tightens, the brief hope draining away until only a guarded, flat calm remains.

"Right," she says, rising from the straw and slinging the quiver over her shoulder. "Another way, then."

She buckles her belt and steps past you toward the yard without looking back.

She sits on the end cot with her gear already packed: a narrow shoulder bag buckled at her boots, her dark cloak rolled beneath it, and her recurve cased in oiled leather. Her bedroll and blanket remain on the pallet behind her.

"${name}."

She nods toward the mattress beside her.

Five silver marks lie against the rough grey wool.

"Yours."

You glance from the coins to her. "That was quick."

"Vane's pay-chest opened this morning."

"And the first thing you did was pay me?"

"The first thing I did was eat."

You look at her.

"The second thing."

You gather up the silver. Lyra watches the last coin disappear into your pouch.

"There," she says. "Square."

"Feel better?"

"Much."

She reaches down for the shoulder bag and checks the buckle.

"Going somewhere?"

"Into the city."

Something in her tone makes you wait.

Lyra notices.

"My aunt." Her fingers linger on the buckle. "There are a few things about her death that don't sit right with me. I've been asking around when I can."

"You find anything?"

"Not yet."

She pulls the bag onto her shoulder.

"If that changes, and you need another pair of hands, find me."

Lyra looks at you for a moment.

"I might."

She takes up her cased bow.

"Try not to spend all five marks before I get back."

"No promises."

That earns you the faintest curl at the corner of her mouth before she heads for the aisle.
