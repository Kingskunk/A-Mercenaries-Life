*label slasher_hook_first
You wait for the awl to go through the leather before you speak. "Captain Vane told me to look in on you. He said you've got a job going."

Varren pulls the waxed cord tight with his teeth and looks up, the good eye first and the pale one a moment behind it. "Did he." He lays the strap across his knee. "Aye, there's one. I hadn't made up my mind who'd get it, but I see the Captain has." He nudges a stool toward you with his boot. "Pull that over. You'll want to be sitting."

You sit. Behind the partition the grindstone winds down and stops.

"You've heard about the killings in the city?"

"I haven't. Tell me what you know."

"Seven dead." He says it flat, the way he'd read a tally off a board. "Always at night, always in a back lane, always cut up and left where they fell. The Watch calls him the Slasher, because they've got nothing better to call him."

He takes a rag from the bench and works the awl clean. "Every time the Watch gets hold of something, it goes nowhere. A witness leaves town. A name turns out to belong to a man who died last winter. A door that stood open one day is bricked up the next. They're stretched thin, and everyone in those lanes is too frightened to talk. But it's more than that. It's like somebody is sweeping the floor behind them as they walk."

"You think someone is helping him."

"I think I don't like it." The awl goes back into its sheath. "Could be plain bad luck. Bad luck doesn't usually keep that neat."

"Why is this the company's business?"

Varren snorts. "It isn't. That's the point." He folds the rag over once and sets it down. "Captain Vane wants the Carrion known in this city for something besides hired muscle. If one of ours puts a name to this, the Council owes us a kind of thanks it can't pay in coin. People stop watching the gate when we march out, and they start saying hello. That's worth more to him than any contract on the board."

"And Vane wants me to be the one."

"Vane wants someone off the roster, and that's you. I want someone who'll do it properly." He leans forward, elbows on his knees. "There's a captain in the city militia, Bronner Trent, with a post on the Trunk Road. He's been asking around for hands from outside the Watch and the militia. People who can go where a uniform can't. I don't know what he has. I know he's loud about wanting it solved, and he's put the word out."

[b]◈ THE MILITIA POST[/b]
*line_break
The post is a squat stone building wedged between a wheelwright's and a chandler's, older than both, with the city's colors hanging limp from a pole over the door. Inside, the front room is one long space split by a counter. A stove ticks in the corner, a rack of pikes leans along one wall, and three militiamen sit on a bench and stop talking when you come in.
*if (weather_wet)
  Rain ticks on the slates overhead.

The one behind the counter looks you over without getting up. "Help you?"

"I'm here to see Captain Trent. Sergeant Varren of the Iron Carrion sent me."

"Varren." He jerks his head toward a passage at the back. "End of the passage. He said somebody might come."

The door at the end opens onto a narrow office with a timber floor scuffed pale by boots. A desk takes up most of the room, buried in papers and a tin mug, and a single window looks onto a lane with its shutter propped open. An iron lantern hangs on a hook beside the door.

The man behind the desk is broad and barrel-chested, in a boiled-leather coat in the city's colors with the sleeves pushed up over thick forearms. Two knives with horn handles hang at his belt, cleaner than anything else in the room. He finishes the line he is writing before he looks up.

"Captain Trent?" you say.

"That's me." He doesn't get up. "Shut the door."

You push it closed, and the noise from the front room drops to a mutter.

"Sergeant Varren of the Iron Carrion sent me. He said you were looking for hands."

"Varren." Trent sets the pen down. "Steady man. He listens before he talks, which is more than I can say for most of the Watch." His eyes go over you once, quickly. "He sent one."

"He sent me."

"Name?"

"A hand of Varren's. That's what I'm offering."

A short laugh comes out of him, without much humor in it. "Fair enough. Names go around this building faster than coin. I'd as soon not carry yours." He nudges a stool out from the desk with his foot. "Sit."

You sit. He pulls the tin mug toward him, looks into it, and pushes it away again.

"What did Varren tell you?"

"That there have been seven killings, and the Watch has nothing."

"Seven." He turns the mug a quarter turn on the blotter. "The last was two nights ago. Betty Fairweather. She worked a loom on the lower terrace and was walking home from a late shift. They found her behind Conduit Square, cut open, with a letter carved into her chest. An S."

"What does it stand for?"

"Nobody knows. The Watch took it for a name and started calling him the Slasher, and the city has used it since." He leans back, and the chair complains. "I'm militia, not Watch. They walk the lanes and I keep the walls, if things get really bad, that's when they call on us. But I've the same trouble they have. Every lead I get dies the day I get it." His eyes stay on yours. "I know who he is."

"Who?"

"Ulrich of Avir."

He picks up the mug and drinks. When he sets it down, he goes on. "A merchant. Timber and stone, a house on the Avenue of Estates in the Patrician Quarter, and enough money that the watch asks his opinion before it asks for its own."

"Then why isn't he in a cell?"

"Because I can't show you why." He holds up a hand before you answer. "I'm certain. That's not the same as having something to put in front of a magistrate. The piece that makes it enough is in his house. The knife, and whatever else a man like that keeps once the night's work is done."

"You could search the house."

"With what? The Watch commander dines at tables Ulrich pays for. Half the merchant lords who pay the Watch's wages call him a friend. The day I put a warrant to his gate, he knows. So does everyone who would warn him, and I'd bet a few of them are sitting on that bench." He jerks his chin at the door. "There's no man out there I'd trust with this."

"So you want me to search it."

"I want a pair of hands nobody here has met." He opens the desk drawer and lays a folded paper and a rough sheet on the blotter. "That is a warrant under my hand and seal, made out to the bearer. Show it at the gate of the Patrician Quarter. If the guards honor it, you walk in the front door. If they refuse it, you've learned how far Ulrich's reach goes, and showing it at the manor will do you no better."

"And the other sheet?"

"A plan of the house. I drew it from what I could get." He taps a cross near the back, beside a small square. "The cellar door off the kitchen yard stays unlatched for the coal. If the front is closed to you, that is your way in."

He rests his hand flat on the desk. "His study has a desk. The knife is in it, or something that puts his hand in a lane after dark. Bring me that and I'll carry it to the magistrates myself. Fifteen silver marks when it's on this blotter."
