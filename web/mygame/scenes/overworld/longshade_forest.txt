*comment THE FOREST'S SHARED LAYERS (2026-10-14). A place in the forest is read many times, so its text is built in layers (narrative guidelines sections 3 and 10): a place's own prose (a lit version and a by-touch
*comment version for the dark) says what the place IS; lsf_env adds the two layers every forest place shares, in a line each below it:
*comment   the sky     weather first (rain, snow, fog), then the hour, then the ground's memory of the last rain (ground_state). Light sentences are used only for a lit place, never at night, and nothing
*comment               here has a person in it. In the dark the same layer is heard and felt, never seen.
*comment   an ambient detail  one of eight sounds, smells or sights that rotates with dg_steps, so walking on changes it but a page refresh does not. The sight-and-sound pool is for day and dusk; at night or in the
*comment               dark the touch-and-sound pool is used, so there is no birdsong at midnight.
*comment A place calls it with *gosub lsf_env after its own prose. The forest's own rooms and the trail can all share it.
*comment ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
*label lsf_env

  *if (weather_wet)

  *elseif (weather_cold)

  *elseif (weather_visibility = "poor")
    The air is damp and thick, and it beads on your face.
  *else

  *if ((ground_state = "sodden") and (not(weather_wet)))

  *elseif ((ground_state = "wet") and (not(weather_wet)))

*else
  *if (weather_wet)
    Rain taps on the leaves overhead, and heavy drops fall through the branches to the ground.
  *elseif (weather_cold)

  *elseif (weather_visibility = "poor")
    Fog drifts between the trunks, and the farther trees fade to grey shapes.
  *elseif (le_night)

  *elseif (weather = "Overcast")
