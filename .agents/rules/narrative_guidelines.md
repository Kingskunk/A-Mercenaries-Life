## 1. Strict Subjective POV (Zero Omniscient Leaks)
* **Second-Person Subjective Anchor ("You"):** Narration is strictly limited to what the protagonist directly sees, hears, smells, feels, or recalls from their selected background.
* **No Authorial Knowledge:** Never name a distant location, enemy plan, faction title, or officer's secret past in the prose unless an NPC speaks it, a scout reports it, or the protagonist reads/observes it in-universe.
* **Organic Name Discovery in Prose & Menus:** Never declare an unfamiliar NPC's proper name in narrative prose or `*choice` option buttons before the protagonist hears it spoken in dialogue, reads it on a document/sign, or is introduced. If an NPC has not yet been introduced in-fiction, gate the choice option (`*if (met_NPC) ... *else ...`) so option buttons refer to them strictly by visible role, appearance, rank insignia, or demeanor until their identity is established.
* **No Telepathic NPC Insight:** Never state what an NPC is thinking or feeling in omniscient terms. Describe only their physical tells—voice pitch, posture, clenched jaws, avoidance of eye contact, or nervous habits.
* **Progressive Codex Discovery:** Factions, locations, and historical lore stay locked (`[??? UNDISCOVERED]`) until in-world dialogue or events organically reveal them.

## 2. Ability-Specific Description (No Generic Combat Text)
* **Name the Thing, Not the Category:** When a cantrip, spell, weapon attack, or class feature resolves, the prose must describe *that specific ability* — never a generic stand-in like "you use your magic" or "you attack." Each attack or spell should have its own distinct visual, auditory, and physical presence.
* **Every Option Gets Its Own Text:** If a `*choice` offers multiple abilities to use in the same moment, each branch needs its own success/failure description tied to that ability's flavor — not a shared template with the ability name swapped in.
* **Dice Announcements Are Flavor Too:** The check label shown to the player should match the in-fiction name of what is happening, not an abstracted mechanic name.

## 3. Squad Presence (Show the Unit, Not Just the Protagonist)
* **The Squad Acts Collectively:** In any scene where the protagonist fights or works alongside their assigned squad or unit, the prose must show other squad members doing their job in parallel — landing hits, casting spells, giving cover — not just the protagonist acting in a vacuum with allies as silent scenery.
* **Named NPCs Perform Their Role:** If a commanding or specialist NPC is present for an event, something in the text should reflect their actual specialty in that moment (e.g., an archer officer loosing arrows, an experienced caster weaving visibly stronger magic) rather than just standing nearby.
* **Match Squad Concept to Squad Action:** If a squad is defined by a role (e.g. ranged scouting, heavy vanguard, mystical cadre), scenes involving that squad need at least one beat that actually exercises that role. Don't build a squad identity in setup text and then never pay it off in the scene.

## 4. Continuity Discipline (No Invented Callbacks)
* **Never Reference an Event That Didn't Happen:** Lines like "just as X promised" or "exactly like Y warned you" must trace back to real prior text — an actual line of dialogue or a described event earlier in the game. If no such moment exists, write it as a generic beat ("as the plan called for") instead of inventing a false callback.
* **Information Continuity on Options:** Choices must never prompt the player to reference, reveal, or leverage information, secret names, or items that they never actually discovered or unlocked in prior gameplay.
* **Catch-Up Option Integrity:** If a choice option's resolution provides a fallback or catch-up path to learn lore or mechanics missed earlier in the game (`*if (not(knows_X))`), the choice prompt leading into that branch must never assume or assert prior knowledge of that lore. The prompt must be phrased from the perspective of an uninformed protagonist seeking information, or conditionally split into separate prompts for informed versus uninformed characters.
* **Check Cross-Squad/Cross-NPC References:** A character who never appears in a given squad's path should not be name-dropped as if they had interacted with the player there.
* **When Renaming or Reworking a Character, Sweep for Old References:** Name collisions or leftover mentions of a cut/renamed character must be searched and reconciled across all scene files before considering a change complete.

## 5. Choice Design & Hub Presentation
* **Three Is the Standard:** Meaningful choices should offer three genuine options, not two. A two-option choice is a design smell; a single unconditional option is a bug unless it is deliberate (a class-determining trial, a settings toggle, or a `[Return]` navigation link).
* **New Options Must Be Real, Not Filler:** A third option added to satisfy this rule needs its own distinct flavor, mechanic, and consequence — reusing a stat or reward wholesale from an existing option defeats the purpose.
* **Earned Affordances (No Magic Buttons):** Every option presented in a `*choice` block must be grounded in physical features, sensory cues, items, or conversational openings established in the preceding prose. Never present choices based on unseen objects, unmentioned exits, or sudden unexplained actions.
* **Background & Trait Justification:** When an option is gated by origin, class, or high attribute, the choice prompt and its resolution prose must ground why the protagonist has this insight (e.g. trade experience, street habits, martial drills) rather than treating it as an abstract stat check.
* **Watch for Hidden Stat Traps:** When multiple options resolve via different ability checks at the same nominal difficulty, verify they are actually comparable for the character builds that can pick them.
* **Living Hub Environments (No Menu Catalogs):** Exploration, camp, and settlement hubs must be presented as continuous, living narrative scenes grounded in immediate sensory atmosphere and squad activity. Never format hub prose as an itemized menu, bulleted list, or bolded catalog of choice locations.

## 6. Prose Craft & Vocabulary Discipline
* **Vary Vocabulary Across Adjacent Text:** Outside of proper nouns, class/race terms, and established mechanic names, check general descriptive vocabulary (verbs, adjectives, sensory words) against the current scene and the previous one or two scenes. AI writing reflexively repeats the same words across nearby passages — if a distinctive word or turn of phrase was just used, pick a different one.
* **Substance Over Spice:** Avoid purple prose. Ground scenes in physical sensation — weather, terrain, weight of gear, exhaustion, cold — so the player inhabits the moment rather than reading a description of it from outside.
* **Trust What's Shown:** Don't restate in narration what an action or line of dialogue already made clear. If a beat needs a gloss afterward to land, the fix belongs in the setup, not in an explanatory sentence tacked on after.

## 7. Natural Dialogue & Distinct NPC Voice
* **Imperfect, Not Clinical:** NPC dialogue should sound like a person talking, not a script — fragments, contractions, implication instead of spelled-out exposition. Avoid dialogue where a character explains information both speakers would already know, purely for the player's benefit.
* **Each NPC Has a Register:** Recurring NPCs should each read as a distinct person — word choice, sentence length, what they choose to say versus imply. Before writing a line for a recurring NPC, ask whether it sounds like *them* specifically or could be swapped into any other character's mouth unchanged.

## 8. Avoid AI Prose Tics
* **Earn Silence, Don't Default to It:** Before writing that a character "says nothing" or answers "without a word," write what they actually do or say instead. Described silence is a cop-out unless it is a deliberate, rare beat for a specific character.
* **Frame Actions Positively:** Avoid defining a character's behavior by what they don't do ("he doesn't ask why," "she doesn't look up"). Write the affirmative action or decision instead. The same tic shows up as "Not X. Just Y." sentence fragments — write the direct statement instead of defining it by negation.
* **Skip Standalone One-Word Replies:** "Yeah," "Right," "Good," and similar one-word answers used as a complete response are filler dressed as an emotional beat. Ask what the character actually wants to say and write that.
* **Avoid Borrowed-Comparison Shorthand:** Constructions like "the way he always does" or "the kind of quiet that..." outsource specificity to a vague category instead of stating the direct, concrete detail.
* **Cut Filler Processing Gestures:** Lines like "he files it away" or "let that settle" mark that information landed without showing what the character does with it. If nothing follows, cut it; if something does (a decision, a shift in plan), write that instead.

## 9. Surface Interior Reasoning on Meaningful Choices
* **Mercenary Logic & Motivation:** When the protagonist makes a meaningful decision in narration — spending scarce coin to aid an NPC, picking one tactical gambit over another, trusting or doubting an officer — the prose must carry the specific internal reasoning or motivation behind that choice. A character who acts without visible motive reads as a plot device rather than a living person making a choice in that specific moment.

## 10. Describe NPCs on Introduction
* **Sensory Anchor on First Encounter:** The first time an NPC appears in a scene, provide a concrete physical description—build, face, clothing, visible gear, scars, and bearing—anchored strictly in what the protagonist observes.
* **Identity Follows Interaction:** Establish presence and physical appearance before declaring names; let proper identities emerge through dialogue, badges of rank, or direct introduction.

## 11. Don't Over-Explain Established Mechanics
* Once a racial trait, class feature, or mechanic has been established (in the codex, or from an earlier scene), later uses shouldn't re-explain how or why it works. Show the action and trust the player to already know it. Reserve an explicit explanation for: the first time something is established, a moment where it fails or meets friction, or a beat where it specifically changes what the player learns.

## 12. Action Economy in Choices (Combat vs. Exploration)
* **In Combat (Turn/Action Cost):** High-stakes combat beats must treat casting or tactical abilities as dedicated choice slots/actions in initiative order (e.g. casting a cantrip or spell is a full choice). You cannot swing a melee weapon and cast an action cantrip simultaneously without a specific class feature.
* **Outside Combat (Exploration/Downtime At-Will Prep):** Cantrips are at-will. In exploration, downtime, and investigation hubs, offer a preparatory pre-cast choice (e.g. `[Cantrip: Guidance]`) allowing the player to freely choose which subsequent skill check or dialogue option receives their magical focus.

## 13. ChoiceScript Code Discipline & State Hygiene
* **Strict Line-Separated Flow Control:** Flow-control commands (`*if`, `*elseif`, `*else`, `*set`, `*goto`, `*gosub`) must always start on their own dedicated, indented lines. Never place conditional statements inline within prose paragraphs, as ChoiceScript will fail to parse them as commands and print the raw code directly to the player.
* **No Nested Multireplaces:** ChoiceScript's parser fails on nested multireplaces (`@{var1 @{var2 ...|...}|...}`). For dynamic stat hints based on multiple conditions, always compute them into `*temp` strings (e.g. `${hint_text}`) before the `*choice` block.
* **Engine State Hygiene:** Every temporary roll modifier (`advantage`, `disadvantage`, `guidance_active`) must automatically reset to `false` inside the engine subroutine (`roll_d20_check`) upon return so bonuses never leak across unrelated choices.
* **Refresh/Replay Safety (No Bare `*set var +N` / `-N`):** A browser refresh or a Show Stats/Menu round trip can replay the current page from where it began without the player taking any new action, silently re-running every command on it — including any `*set stat + N` / `*set stat - N`. A bare increment/decrement is not safe to write anywhere a page can pause (a `*choice`, a `*page_break`, before a `*gosub`/`*gosub_scene`) unless it is guarded. In order of preference:
  1. **Prefer a fully derived stat.** If the value can be recomputed from scratch from persistent flags every time (see `recalculate_armor_class` in `startup.txt`), do that instead of nudging it with `+N` at each place gear/state changes — a full recompute lands on the same answer no matter how many times it runs, so it needs no lock at all.
  2. **Otherwise, lock the mutation.** Wrap it in `*if (not(X_locked)) or (not(locked_X_page_id = choice_page_id)) ... *set stat +/- N ... *set X_locked true ... *set locked_X_page_id choice_page_id`, matching `roll_locked`/`damage_roll_locked`/`currency_txn_locked` in `startup.txt` (`choice_page_id` is an engine-maintained counter, reserved `choice_` prefix, that only advances on a genuine new choice/Next click — never on a replay). Reuse `currency_txn_locked` for any silver/copper change; reuse `stat_bump_locked`/`locked_stat_bump_page_id` for everything else non-currency (companion respect/regard/bond, hub "actions remaining" counters, ability scores, `ruthless`/`compassion`, HP heals, and any other bare stat `+N`/`-N`) rather than inventing a new lock per stat or per site — a full sweep of the game found this exact bug at 40+ separate sites across every scene file, all fixed with this one shared pair. Never reuse one lock across two different kinds of effect that can both fire on the same page (e.g. a check's roll and that check's damage roll need separate flags, and so do two independent stat bumps landing on one page with no *choice between them — e.g. a hub spending an actions-left slot and immediately granting a companion regard bump) — the first effect locking would otherwise make the second skip entirely on its own first run. For that specific collision, `stat_bump2_locked`/`locked_stat_bump2_page_id` is the pre-declared second slot — reach for it before declaring yet another one-off lock pair.
  3. **When restructuring a `*if (check_success) *set stat +N ... prose *else *set stat +N ... prose` block to add the guard, split the stat mutation out from the prose.** Compute the bump once inside the guard (`*if (not(stat_bump_locked)) or (...) *if (check_success) *set stat +A *else *set stat +B ... *set stat_bump_locked true ...`), then follow it with a second, unguarded `*if (check_success) ... *else ...` purely for the narrative text. Re-reading `check_success` itself is safe (`roll_d20_check` already locks it), but if the *prose* selection depends on re-checking the stat you just bumped against the same threshold that decided the bump (e.g. `*if (lyra_bond >= 15) *set lyra_bond +2 *else *set lyra_bond +1`), that re-check is a real bug: a replay sees the *already-bumped* value and can flip to the wrong half of the prose. Capture the pre-bump boolean into its own persistent flag *inside* the guard (see `lyra_pay_generous`, declared in `startup.txt` and used in `alderford.txt`'s `taphouse_talk_lyra`) and branch the prose on that flag, never on a fresh re-read of the stat.
  4. **If one page can legitimately trigger the SAME kind of mutation more than once, `choice_page_id` alone isn't enough.** `calendar.txt`'s `advance_time` is the example: some pages fall from one activity straight into another (`mess_done` into `night_end`) without an intervening choice, so both need to actually apply. Add a per-call-SITE identifier on top of `choice_page_id` — the caller sets `*set time_advance_call_id "<unique_id>"` immediately before `*gosub_scene calendar advance_time` (the enclosing `*label` name is normally enough; append a number if that label has more than one call in it, as `alderford_start` does: `"alderford_start_1"`, `"alderford_start_2"`), and the lock compares `(locked_time_advance_page_id = choice_page_id) and (locked_time_advance_id = time_advance_call_id)`. Use a plain `*create`d variable for this, not `*params` — `*params` throws if the label is ever reached without going through a real `*gosub`/`*gosub_scene` (which is exactly what quicktest's own scene-by-scene coverage pass does), where a `*create` default just reads as "unlocked."
* **Resetting a Consumed Input to a Fixed Literal Must Happen Outside the Lock, Not Inside It:** When a subroutine consumes caller-supplied working variables and clears them when done (e.g. `advance_time` zeroing `minutes_to_pass`/`hours_to_pass`/`days_to_pass` after applying them to the clock), don't put that reset inside the same replay guard as the non-idempotent math it follows. A guard correctly skips the math on replay, but if the reset lives inside it too, the skip also leaves the consumed variable stuck at its last input value instead of clean at `0` — and unlike the math, resetting to a fixed literal is *always* safe to repeat, so there's no reason to lock it. Structure it as: guarded math first, then the reset(s) unconditionally afterward, outside the `*if`.
* **Full Slot Coverage:** When gating choices by learned spells or cantrips, always check all potential character slots (e.g. primary cantrip, secondary cantrips) so a player's build choices are never orphaned.
* **Three-Point Metadata Synchronization:** When introducing new lore, locations, factions, or companions, ensure complete synchronization across three points: (1) global variable initialization (`*create var false`), (2) conditional progressive disclosure in the player dossier/stats screen (`*if (var)`), and (3) developer jump/fast-forward menus so testing skips set all prerequisite flags and campaign clock state.
* **One-Time Story Grants Should Guard Themselves:** For a discrete, once-only event (a reward, a relationship bump, an item handoff tied to a specific scene beat), wrap the entire grant — flag, stat bumps, and all — in `*if (not(has_X)) ... *set has_X true ...`, checking the persistent flag *before* any side effect runs. This is naturally replay-safe on its own (a refresh replaying the page re-checks the flag, sees it's already `true`, and skips the whole block) — it doesn't need the `choice_page_id` lock from the Refresh/Replay Safety rule above, which is only for mutations with no natural "already happened" flag to check (e.g. spending currency, where the amount varies by branch).
* **Guard One-Time Init Blocks on a Dedicated Flag, Never a Variable That Already Has a Matching Default:** If a block should run exactly once (e.g. snapshotting a value at character creation), gate it with its own `*create ..._initialized false` and check `*if (not(..._initialized))`. Do not key the guard off some other variable's value (e.g. `*if (some_var = "")`) without first confirming what that variable's `*create` default actually is — if the default already satisfies the "already initialized" reading, the guard is permanently true or permanently false and the block silently never runs (or runs forever). This exact mistake broke the equipment system's starting-weapon snapshot once already: `equipped_weapon_id` *create-defaults to `"starting"`, so a guard checking `equipped_weapon_id = ""` could never be true, and the snapshot never happened for any new character.
* **`*set var -1` Is Decrement Shorthand, Not "Set to the Literal -1".** ChoiceScript treats a leading `+`/`-` on the right-hand side of `*set` as shorthand for "add/subtract this from the variable's current value" — this applies even when the intent was a bare assignment. `*set ca_sign -1` inside `currency_add` was meant to set a sign flag to literal `-1`, but with `ca_sign` freshly `*temp`'d to `1`, it silently computed `1 - 1 = 0` instead — a real bug that sat unnoticed because the one place `ca_sign` got compared (`*if (ca_sign > 0)`) only needed "not positive," so `0` happened to still route correctly. To assign a literal negative number, sidestep the shorthand with an explicit expression whose first token isn't an operator: `*set ca_sign 0 - 1`. Anywhere you write `*set someVar -N` or `*set someVar +N` intending "make it exactly N," stop and use the `0 - N` form instead, or better, use a positive stat and treat sign elsewhere.
* **A Bare `*create X 85` Stores a String; Only `*set X +N` Arithmetic Produces a Real Number.** `*create` with a literal (no operator) stores the token as-is; `typeof` on that stat stays `"string"` until something does real arithmetic on it. This is usually invisible (ChoiceScript's own comparisons coerce loosely), but it burned real debugging time once already: a fuzz-test script that read a stat straight via JS (`scene.stats.foo`) and checked `typeof v === "number"` silently skipped every stat that had only ever been `*create`d, never `*set +N`'d. If you're writing a Node harness or any tooling that inspects `stats` values directly, coerce with `Number(v)` and check `isNaN` rather than trusting `typeof`.

## 14. Grounded Economy & Low-Fantasy Worldbuilding
* **Magic Is Rare, Distrusted, and Practical:** Mundane sellswords, officers, and common folk fear what they don't understand. Prose should reflect this: common soldiers eyeing unnatural bloodlines with suspicion, practitioners sitting slightly apart from the main campfire line, and line troops being rattled by sudden eldritch flares.
* **Economic Grit:** Silver marks, iron shillings, and copper coins are scarce and hard-won. Loot must feel tangible and modest (smoked rations, tallow candles, water-damaged ledgers, preserved frontier coins), reinforcing the mercenary reality that every mark counts.
* **Period-Grounded Worldbuilding Vocabulary:** Use concrete, setting-appropriate terms for labor, logistics, and military roles (e.g. mule-drivers, draymen, carters, barge loaders, quartermasters) rather than modern administrative or anachronistic shorthand.

## 15. Mechanics Confined to Brackets, Never in Prose
* **Strict Separation of Stats and Story:** Numbers, dice designations, DCs, and mechanic names belong strictly in `[bracketed stat hints]` or banner cards.
* **Pure In-Universe Narration:** Prose must never mention game mechanics directly (e.g. avoid *"you took 4 damage"* or *"you passed a DC 12 check"*). Ground the result in physical sensation: torn gambesons, bruised ribs, the cold bite of iron, and the sharp relief of a strike landing true.
* **Mechanical Purity in Choice Brackets:** Bracketed hints on choice options (`[STR DC 12]`, `[5 Silver Marks]`, `[Cantrip: Guidance]`) are strictly reserved for actionable mechanics, skill checks, abilities, and resource costs. Never place narrative plot summaries, quest categories, or thematic tags inside brackets.

## 16. Time Progression & Chronological Discipline
* **Realistic Clock & Activity Alignment:** Every advance of the clock (`hours_to_pass` / `minutes_to_pass`) must directly correlate with the physical actions taking place in the scene (e.g. 15-mile campaign march = 6–8h; skirmish & breach = 1h; post-battle fatigue duty = 3–4h; camp activity = 30–45m; long rest = 8h). Never advance time without narrative and sensory grounding (sweat cooling, muscles stiffening, mud crusting, shadows stretching, weather shifting).
* **Atmospheric & Seasonal Lighting Integrity:** Scene lighting and weather must strictly match the calendar season and current hour (e.g. Autumn / Campaign Season: Dawn `~06:00`, Midday `~12:00–13:00`, Dusk `~17:30–18:00`, Night `~20:00+`). If a battle ends in early afternoon (`14:00`), the text cannot abruptly jump to "dusk cook-fires" without depicting the grueling afternoon labor (hauling gear, tending wounded, building barricades) that fills those hours.
* **Midnight Rollover & Rest Synchronization:** Sleeping or resting across midnight must advance the clock into early morning (`04:30 – 06:00`) so `calendar.txt` automatically rolls `campaign_day` and `calendar_date` to the next day. Morning muster scenes must always align with a pre-dawn/morning timestamp, ensuring status cards never display night hours during a morning beat.
* **Incremental Hub Passage & Dynamic Atmosphere:** Exploration and camp hubs with multiple action slots must pass time incrementally per visit (`minutes_to_pass 30-45`), with the hub text dynamically reflecting the fading twilight, deepening night, and changing sentry watches as activities are spent.

## 17. Equipment & Magic Item Systems
* **New Weapons/Armor Route Through the Loadout System, Not a Bare `*set weapon`:** Direct `*set weapon "..."` / `*set weapon_desc ...` etc. only belongs in the character-creation archetype blocks (`startup.txt`) and inside `apply_weapon_loadout` itself. Anything a player can pick up mid-game (a shop purchase, a battlefield find) must instead: (1) grant a `has_X` ownership flag, (2) add a branch for it in `apply_weapon_loadout` (startup.txt) keyed by a new `equipped_weapon_id` value, and (3) let the player switch to it via `*gosub_scene startup equip_weapon "X"` from the Equipment codex page — never assign `weapon`/`weapon_desc`/`weapon_damage`/`weapon_type`/`weapon_hands` directly at the pickup site. Every weapon needs a `weapon_hands` tag (`"one_handed"` or `"two_handed"`); equipping a two-handed weapon must go through `apply_weapon_loadout`, which auto-drops `shield_equipped` for you.
* **Magic Items Are Attuned, Never Given Outright:** A magic item's bonus must never be applied as a permanent `*set <stat> +N` at the pickup site — beyond the general Refresh/Replay Safety hazard that pattern always carries, attunement specifically has to be *revocable*, which a permanent `+N` can never be. Instead: grant a `has_X` ownership flag, add the item's bonus as a branch in `recalculate_attuned_bonuses` (startup.txt) keyed off a new `X_attuned` flag, and let it be bound/unbound via `*gosub_scene startup attune_item "X"` / `unattune_item "X"`, both capped by `max_attunement_slots` (5). If the item modifies an ability score, that score needs its own `base_<stat>` snapshot (captured once in `update_dnd_stats`, mirroring `base_constitution`) so the bonus can be added and removed cleanly; if it's the first item to touch a *new* ability score, also extract that score's modifier lookup into its own `recalculate_<stat>_mod` subroutine (mirroring `recalculate_con_mod`) so both `update_dnd_stats` and `recalculate_attuned_bonuses` can call it without duplicating the table. On pickup, auto-attune if a slot is free (mirroring the shield's auto-equip-if-hands-free behavior); otherwise the item sits owned-but-dormant until the player frees a slot.

## 18. Reusable Town-Content Primitives (Gambling, Taverns, Currency)
* **Never Hand-Roll a New Ante/Payout/Lock Trio Per Town — Call the Shared Subroutine.** `startup.txt` provides three reusable engine primitives specifically so a dozen future towns don't each reinvent this plumbing:
  * `currency_add` — pure arithmetic, no locking of its own. Input `currency_add_amount` (signed integer, copper-equivalent units: 10 copper = 1 silver, 10 silver = 1 gold); adds/spends it against `gold`/`silver`/`copper` with correct carrying and borrowing. Always call it from inside the caller's own replay-safety guard, same as any other currency mutation — it does not guard itself.
  * `resolve_wager` — one shared subroutine for every gambling table (dice, cards, knuckle-bones, whatever). Set `wager_bankroll_var` (string name of that table's own `*create`d house-bankroll stat), `wager_ante_copper`, `wager_win_copper`, and the usual `check_stat`/`check_dc`/`check_skill`, then `*gosub_scene startup resolve_wager`. It debits the ante from the player and credits it to the bankroll, rolls the check, and on success pays out `wager_win_copper` from the bankroll to the player — capped to whatever's left, so the house can never pay out more than it has. Returns `check_success` as always plus `wager_bankroll_busted` (true once the bankroll hits 0), which the caller should use to hide the wager option from its own `*choice` (`*if (my_table_bankroll > 0)`) and print a "the house is tapped out" line, exactly like `taphouse_dice_table` in `alderford.txt` does. A new gambling den only needs its own `*create <name>_bankroll N` stat and a handful of `*set`s before the gosub — never a bespoke ante/payout lock pair.
  * `buy_tavern_item` — one shared subroutine for a purchasable consumable (food, drink, anything a future tavern sells) that spends coin and optionally heals HP and/or grants the single-slot nourishment buff (`meal_buff_*`). Set `tavern_item_cost_copper`, `tavern_item_hp_heal` (0 for none), and — if granting a buff — `tavern_item_buff_name`/`_desc`/`_stat`/`_bonus`/`_minutes`, then `*gosub_scene startup buy_tavern_item`. Since the nourishment buff is single-slot (no stacking), hide each item's `*choice` option while that item's own buff is already active — `*if (not((meal_buff_active) and (meal_buff_stat = "con")))` — mirroring the Drowned Oar Taphouse bar loop; this also stops a player from re-buying an item whose effect they already have.
  * Each of these three primitives already handles its own two-mutations-one-breath replay hazard internally (`resolve_wager`'s ante and win payout each get their own dedicated lock pair, `wager_ante_locked`/`wager_win_locked`, for the same reason `damage_roll_locked` is kept separate from `roll_locked`). A caller does not need to add `currency_txn_locked` guards around calls into these three — only around currency mutations written by hand outside of them.

## 19. Testing Tools & Verification
* **Three complementary tools exist at the repo root — run the right one for what you're checking, and don't treat any one of them as sufficient on its own:**
  * `quicktest.js` (`node quicktest.js web/mygame`) — parses every scene file and walks every reachable line at least once. Catches parse errors, `*goto`/`*label` typos, and non-existent variables. Doesn't catch logic bugs.
  * `randomtest.js` (`node randomtest.js num=N seed=S`) — plays the game start-to-finish with randomly chosen options, `N` times. Catches crashes and reachability issues across real playthroughs. Does **not** check refresh-safety, since it never revisits a pause point it already passed.
    * `showUnreachedLabels=true` reuses the per-line hit-count instrumentation `showCoverage` already builds, but instead of dumping every line it cross-references every `*label` in every scene file (including scenes/labels the walk never entered at all) against that hit-count map and reports only the ones that stayed at 0 across the whole run. Cheap way to catch genuinely dead content -- an `*if` guard that can never be false (e.g. `*if (not(met_lyra))` when every chargen path already sets `met_lyra true` before that scene is reachable), or a `*label` nothing `*goto`s anymore. A label showing up here isn't automatically dead, though -- it might just be content this particular seed range didn't happen to reach (squad-gated or origin-gated content especially); rerun with a different `num`/`seed` before concluding it's truly unreachable, and check whether the *label* itself is gated the same way its guard is (a guard can be legitimately conditional even when a specific stat happens to always be true for one particular path through it).
  * `refresh_fuzz_test.js` (`node refresh_fuzz_test.js num=N seed=S`) — the one built this session specifically to catch the refresh/replay-duplication bug class the rest of this document guards against. At every `*choice`/`*page_break` a random walk reaches, it snapshots stats, independently replays the page from its recorded start against a clone of that snapshot, and diffs the result — any stat that comes out different is a command that re-ran on the (simulated) replay and shouldn't have. This is what found the 40+ unguarded stat-bump sites fixed this session; run it after any change that touches a bare `*set stat +N`/`-N`, not just once at the end.
    * `mode=smart archetype=str|dex|con|int|wis|cha|balanced` (default: rotate through all seven across iterations) makes the walk build-aware instead of purely random: it statically scans each option body for the `*set check_stat "xxx"` line every skill check sets, always takes an available not-yet-active `[Cantrip: Guidance]` option first, and otherwise prefers whichever option's stat matches the archetype (or, for `balanced`, the build's own actual highest modifier). Pure uniform random mostly attempts checks the current build has no business trying and depends entirely on chargen RNG for whether a given stat's success branch ever gets visited at all — smart mode gives consistent, repeatable coverage of each archetype's actual success paths (the `*rand` inside `roll_d20_check` is untouched, so failure branches for that archetype still get seen too). Add `showChoices=true` to log every pick and, in smart mode, why (`guidance-priority` / `stat-match(stat archetype=... weight=N)` / `no-check`).
* **A headless test harness (any of the three above, or a one-off script in the scratchpad) MUST override `saveCookie` to fire its callback immediately, not defer it through the same `timeout` variable `clearScreen` uses.** `web/scene.js`'s `printLoop()` calls `refreshSavedProgress()` on every single pause, which calls the bare global `saveCookie(function(){}, ...)` purely to persist stats for a refresh that will never happen in a test harness. A harness that (incorrectly) treats every `saveCookie` call as "the next thing to run" — as `randomtest.js` did until this session — has that no-op callback clobber the real `*choice` continuation set moments earlier, silently stopping the walk after its very first choice while still reporting a full pass. `headless.js` already defines `saveCookie` correctly (fire-and-forget); don't shadow it with a `clearScreen`-style deferral when writing a new harness.
* **Any new Node harness must also set `global.safeTimeout = function(fn) { safeCall(null, fn); };`** before loading `mygame.js`. Without it, a freshly constructed `Scene`'s first `execute()` defers its own line-loading through a real (async) `setTimeout`, which a synchronous harness never gets a chance to run — reading exactly like "this page has no content" or "the replay ran straight to `*finish`" instead of the parse error it actually is.

## 20. Reusable Small-Combat Engine (`resolve_player_hit` / `resolve_enemy_attack`)
* **For a short, contained skirmish (a cellar nuisance, not a Black Sinks-scale set piece), reuse the two-primitive combat engine in `startup.txt` rather than hand-rolling a new one per fight.** Built and proven on the Alderford culvert encounter (`alderford.txt`); everything below generalizes to any future small fight.
  * **The player's own attack roll needs zero new code.** It's just `roll_d20_check` with `check_dc` set to the enemy's AC (`combat_enemy_ac`) and `check_skill` set to whatever's being attempted — advantage, guidance, nat 20/1, rogue expertise, meal buffs all already work, for free.
  * **`resolve_player_hit`** — call after a `check_success`, with `combat_player_dmg_dice_sides`/`combat_player_dmg_bonus` set to whatever the weapon/cantrip calls for. Applies the roll to `combat_enemy_hp` (floored at 0), self-locked, reports `combat_player_dmg_dealt` for your prose.
  * **`resolve_enemy_attack`** — rolls the enemy's attack against the player's own `armor_class` and applies damage to `hp_current` on a hit (halved for a raging barbarian, floored at 1 — a random small fight never gets to kill the protagonist outright; let the calling scene narrate a rescue instead). Self-locked. Reads `combat_enemy_atk_bonus`/`combat_enemy_dmg_dice_sides`/`combat_enemy_dmg_bonus` (set once when the fight starts) and `combat_enemy_disadvantage`/`combat_enemy_advantage` (set per-round by the caller for a Dodge action or a pre-fight "you were spotted" penalty — `combat_enemy_advantage` self-clears after one use since it's a one-shot penalty, unlike `combat_enemy_disadvantage` which the caller resets itself each round since it's re-chosen every round). If both are true on the same call they cancel per the normal advantage/disadvantage rule.
  * **A per-round mutation (an action-menu choice, a round counter) still needs the standard replay lock from Section 13** — a combat round is not exempt just because it's "in a loop." The round counter increment specifically must live in exactly one place per page cycle; if you reset it in one label and increment it in another that runs on the same page (no `*choice`/`*page_break` between them), the reset will silently re-fire on a replay of that page while the guarded increment correctly no-ops, leaving the counter stuck one behind. Put the reset (to a fixed literal, e.g. `combat_round 1`) at the fight's true start and the (locked) increment only at the point that genuinely begins a new page cycle (end of the enemy's turn, right before the `*page_break`) — never both on the page that starts the fight.
  * **A reaction spell (Shield, Uncanny Dodge, etc.) doesn't need a new pause point to feel reactive.** Add an "arm it" option that sets a persistent flag (`shield_armed`) and `*goto`s straight back to the same round-choice hub — the same trick the Guidance cantrip option already uses everywhere else, so arming doesn't cost the round's real action. Then have the relevant `resolve_*` primitive check that flag itself and auto-fire (consuming the resource, disarming itself) the moment it would actually matter, inside its own existing single pass — no interrupt, no second `*choice`. This is deliberately less "realistic" than a true reactive prompt (the resource gets spent automatically whenever it would help, not by a conscious in-the-moment call), but it's the only version that needs no new engine surface at all; only reach for a real interrupt-and-ask pattern if that specific tradeoff genuinely matters for a given ability.
  * **A flag the player set before a `*gosub_scene` can get cleared by the very call it's feeding.** `roll_d20_check` resets `advantage`/`disadvantage`/`guidance_active` to `false` before it returns (Section 13's Engine State Hygiene rule), so anything that needs to know "was advantage true going into this specific roll" (Sneak Attack's trigger condition, for one) has to capture it into a `*temp` *before* the `*gosub_scene` call, never read the live flag after.
  * **A spell with genuinely unusual damage shape (Magic Missile's 3d4+3, always-hits, no attack roll) is often clearer as its own small inline block than as a forced fit through `resolve_player_hit`,** which only models "one die type, one roll, gated on a prior `check_success`." Don't generalize the primitive for a one-off; a few explicit `*rand` calls straight into `combat_enemy_hp` (with their own lock) is more honest about the spell actually being different, and cheaper than adding a dice-count parameter nothing else needs yet.
  * **Before wiring a "built for later" dormant subroutine into its first real caller, check it has a replay lock at all.** `second_wind` and `activate_rage` existed in `startup.txt` from an earlier session with zero locking, because nothing had ever called them, so the bug had never been exercised. The moment a combat loop actually calls them, they're exactly as vulnerable as any other bare `*set stat -1` on a page that can pause. Audit dormant code for this the moment you give it a real caller — don't assume "it's been sitting there fine" means "it's replay-safe."

## 21. Fights Are a Consequence of a Failed Roll, Not a Given
* **A quest that can end in combat should offer a real chance to approach it first — jumping straight from "accept the quest" to "combat has started" reads as arbitrary and skips the tension a skill-based approach should build.** Give the player an approach/investigation beat with a real choice of methods before the fight can start, mirroring the culvert encounter's three-way split: a careful approach that improves the fight's odds on success (grants `advantage` for the opening exchange) with no penalty on failure, a clever approach that can resolve the whole encounter without a fight at all on success, and a "just go in" option with no check for anyone who wants to skip straight to combat. Combat itself should be what happens when a bypass attempt *fails*, or when the player chooses not to attempt one — not the encounter's only door.
* **A non-combat resolution still has to satisfy what the quest-giver actually asked for.** If an NPC's stated goal is elimination ("put steel through it"), a clever bypass has to still be a kill — sealing the threat away, relocating it, or otherwise leaving it alive doesn't close the request even if it removes the immediate danger, and reads as the writer solving a different, easier problem than the one posed. Find an in-fiction mechanism for the bypass to still end the threat permanently (an environmental kill — a collapsing structure, a trap, poisoning its water source — works well and reads as clever rather than anticlimactic), and give it the same reward as a combat win, since the practical outcome the quest-giver cares about is identical.
* **A failed bypass check should carry a real, asymmetric cost, not just "the fight starts normally anyway."** In the culvert encounter, failing the stealth approach costs nothing beyond forfeiting the advantage a success would have granted, but failing the investigation approach specifically alerts the creature and hands *it* the advantage for its opening attack — a failure that actively made things worse, not a neutral non-event. Differentiate consequences like this per approach rather than making every failure converge on the same "combat starts, nothing else happened" outcome.
