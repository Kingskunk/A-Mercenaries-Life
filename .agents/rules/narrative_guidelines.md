## 1. Strict Subjective POV (Zero Omniscient Leaks)
* **Second-Person Subjective Anchor:** Narration is limited to what the protagonist directly sees, hears, smells, feels, or recalls.
* **No Authorial Knowledge:** Never name a distant location, enemy plan, faction title, or officer's secret past unless an NPC speaks it, a scout reports it, or the protagonist observes it in-universe.
* **Organic Name Discovery:** Never state an unfamiliar NPC's name in prose or `*choice` buttons before the protagonist hears/reads/is given it. Gate the option (`*if (met_NPC) ... *else ...`) so buttons refer to them by role, appearance, or rank until introduced.
* **No Telepathic NPC Insight:** Never state what an NPC thinks or feels omnisciently — only physical tells (voice pitch, posture, clenched jaws, avoided eye contact).
* **Progressive Codex Discovery:** Factions, locations, and lore entries are gated behind their own `*if (codex_X)` and simply omitted from the list until in-world events set that flag — no locked/undiscovered placeholder is shown, to keep the codex from cluttering up with entries the player hasn't earned yet.

## 2. Ability-Specific Description (No Generic Combat Text)
* **Name the Thing, Not the Category:** A resolving cantrip, spell, weapon attack, or class feature needs its own distinct visual/auditory/physical description — never a generic stand-in like "you use your magic."
* **Every Option Gets Its Own Text:** Multiple abilities offered in one `*choice` each need their own success/failure prose tied to that ability's flavor, not a shared template with the name swapped in.
* **Dice Announcements Are Flavor Too:** The check label shown to the player should match the in-fiction action, not an abstracted mechanic name.

## 3. Squad Presence (Show the Unit, Not Just the Protagonist)
* **The Squad Acts Collectively:** In any scene fought or worked alongside the squad, show other members doing their job in parallel — not just the protagonist acting with allies as scenery.
* **Named NPCs Perform Their Role:** A present commanding/specialist NPC's text should reflect their actual specialty in that moment, not just standing nearby.
* **Match Squad Concept to Squad Action:** A squad defined by a role (ranged scouting, heavy vanguard, mystical cadre) needs at least one beat per scene that actually exercises it — don't set up an identity and never pay it off.

## 4. Continuity Discipline (No Invented Callbacks)
* **Never Reference an Event, Item, or Secret the Player Never Actually Saw:** Lines like "just as X promised" must trace to real prior text; a choice must never assume the player leveraged information or an item they never unlocked. If no such moment exists, write a generic beat ("as the plan called for") instead of inventing a false callback.
* **Catch-Up Option Integrity:** A fallback path to learn lore missed earlier (`*if (not(knows_X))`) must be phrased from an uninformed protagonist's perspective, never assuming the knowledge it's about to grant.
* **Cross-Squad/Cross-NPC & Rename Sweeps:** A character absent from a given squad's path shouldn't be name-dropped as if they'd interacted there. When renaming or cutting a character, search and reconcile every scene file before calling the change complete.

## 5. Choice Design & Hub Presentation
* **Three Is the Standard:** Meaningful choices offer three genuine options; two is a design smell, one unconditional option is a bug unless deliberate (a trial, a settings toggle, a `[Return]` link).
* **New Options Must Be Real, Not Filler:** A third option added to satisfy this needs its own flavor, mechanic, and consequence — not an existing option's stat/reward with new text.
* **Earned Affordances (No Magic Buttons):** Every option must be grounded in physical features, sensory cues, items, or conversational openings already established in the preceding prose.
* **Background & Trait Justification:** An option gated by origin, class, or a high attribute needs prose grounding *why* the protagonist has that insight, not just an abstract stat check.
* **Watch for Hidden Stat Traps:** When multiple options resolve via different checks at the same nominal DC, verify they're actually comparable for the builds that can pick them.
* **Living Hub Environments (No Menu Catalogs):** Exploration/camp/settlement hubs are continuous narrative scenes grounded in sensory atmosphere and squad activity — never an itemized/bulleted catalog of locations.

## 6. Prose Craft & Vocabulary Discipline
* **Vary Vocabulary Across Adjacent Text:** Check descriptive vocabulary (verbs, adjectives, sensory words) against the current scene and the last one or two — AI writing reflexively repeats words nearby; if one was just used, pick another.
* **Substance Over Spice:** Avoid purple prose. Ground scenes in physical sensation — weather, terrain, gear weight, exhaustion, cold — so the player inhabits the moment.
* **Trust What's Shown:** Don't restate in narration what an action or line of dialogue already made clear. If a beat needs a gloss to land, fix the setup, not a tacked-on explanation.

## 7. Natural Dialogue & Distinct NPC Voice
* **Imperfect, Not Clinical:** Dialogue should sound like a person talking — fragments, contractions, implication — not two characters explaining things they'd both already know for the player's benefit.
* **Each NPC Has a Register:** Recurring NPCs read as distinct people — word choice, sentence length, what they say vs. imply. Before writing a line, ask if it sounds like *them* or could be swapped into anyone else's mouth.

## 8. Avoid AI Prose Tics
* **Earn Silence, Don't Default to It:** Before writing that a character "says nothing," write what they actually do or say — described silence is a cop-out unless deliberate and rare.
* **Frame Actions Positively:** Avoid defining behavior by what a character *doesn't* do ("he doesn't ask why") or "Not X. Just Y." fragments — write the affirmative action instead.
* **Skip Standalone One-Word Replies:** "Yeah," "Right," "Good" as a complete response is filler dressed as an emotional beat — write what the character actually wants to say.
* **Avoid Borrowed-Comparison Shorthand:** "The way he always does" or "the kind of quiet that..." outsources specificity to a vague category — state the concrete detail directly.
* **Cut Filler Processing Gestures:** "He files it away" or "let that settle" marks information landing without showing what happens next. Cut it, or write the actual decision/shift that follows.

## 9. Surface Interior Reasoning on Meaningful Choices
* **Mercenary Logic & Motivation:** A meaningful decision (spending scarce coin, picking a tactical gambit, trusting an officer) needs the specific internal reasoning behind it in prose — a character who acts without visible motive reads as a plot device.

## 10. Describe NPCs on Introduction
* **Sensory Anchor on First Encounter:** First appearance needs a concrete physical description — build, face, clothing, gear, scars, bearing — anchored in what the protagonist observes.
* **Identity Follows Interaction:** Establish presence and appearance before declaring names; let identity emerge through dialogue, rank insignia, or direct introduction.

## 11. Don't Over-Explain Established Mechanics
* Once a trait/feature/mechanic is established, later uses shouldn't re-explain it — show the action and trust the player. Reserve explicit explanation for: first establishment, a moment it fails or meets friction, or a beat that specifically changes what the player learns.

## 12. Action Economy in Choices (Combat vs. Exploration)
* **In Combat:** Casting or tactical abilities are dedicated choice slots/actions in initiative order — no swinging a weapon and casting an action cantrip in the same choice without a specific class feature.
* **Outside Combat:** Cantrips are at-will. Exploration/downtime hubs get a preparatory pre-cast choice (`[Cantrip: Guidance]`) letting the player choose which subsequent check receives it.

## 13. ChoiceScript Code Discipline & State Hygiene
* **Strict Line-Separated Flow Control:** `*if`/`*elseif`/`*else`/`*set`/`*goto`/`*gosub` must start on their own indented lines — never inline within prose, or ChoiceScript prints the raw code to the player instead of parsing it.
* **No Nested Multireplaces:** `@{var1 @{var2 ...|...}|...}` fails to parse. Compute multi-condition stat hints into a `*temp` string (`${hint_text}`) before the `*choice` block instead.
* **Engine State Hygiene:** Every temporary roll modifier (`advantage`, `disadvantage`, `guidance_active`) must reset to `false` inside `roll_d20_check` on return, so bonuses never leak into unrelated choices.
* **Refresh/Replay Safety (No Bare `*set var +N`/`-N`):** A refresh or Stats/Menu round trip can replay the current page with no new player action, silently re-running every command on it. A bare increment/decrement is unsafe anywhere a page can pause (`*choice`, `*page_break`, before a `*gosub`/`*gosub_scene`) unless guarded:
  1. **Prefer a fully derived stat** — recomputed from persistent flags every time (`recalculate_armor_class`) instead of nudged with `+N` per change; a full recompute needs no lock at all.
  2. **Otherwise, lock the mutation:** `*if (not(X_locked)) or (not(locked_X_page_id = choice_page_id)) ... *set stat +/-N ... *set X_locked true ... *set locked_X_page_id choice_page_id` (`choice_page_id` only advances on a genuine new choice/Next, never a replay). Reuse `currency_txn_locked` for silver/copper, `stat_bump_locked`/`locked_stat_bump_page_id` for everything else non-currency — don't invent a lock per stat or site (a full sweep found this exact bug at 40+ sites, all fixed with this one shared pair). If two independent mutations can land on the same page with no `*choice` between them (a hub spending an actions-left slot *and* granting a companion bump), give the second its own lock — the pre-declared `stat_bump2_locked`/`locked_stat_bump2_page_id` slot exists for exactly this, so reach for it before declaring a new one-off pair.
  3. **Splitting a `*if (check_success) *set stat +N ...prose... *else...` block to add a guard:** compute the bump once inside the guard, then follow with a second unguarded `*if (check_success)...*else...` purely for prose. Re-reading `check_success` is safe, but if the *prose* re-checks the stat you just bumped against the same threshold that decided the bump, a replay sees the already-bumped value and flips to the wrong prose. Capture the pre-bump boolean into its own persistent flag inside the guard instead (see `lyra_pay_generous`) and branch prose on that flag.
  4. **One page legitimately triggering the same kind of mutation twice** (some pages fall from one activity into another with no intervening choice, e.g. `calendar.txt`'s `advance_time`) needs a per-call-site identifier on top of `choice_page_id`: `*set time_advance_call_id "<unique_id>"` before the gosub, lock compares both. Use a plain `*create`d variable, not `*params` — `*params` throws if reached without a real gosub (which quicktest's coverage pass does), where a `*create` default just reads as "unlocked."
* **Reset a Consumed Input Outside the Lock, Not Inside It:** A subroutine that clears caller-supplied working variables when done (`advance_time` zeroing `minutes_to_pass` etc.) must do that reset *unconditionally after* the guarded math, never inside the same guard — otherwise a replay's skip also leaves the variable stuck at its last input instead of clean, since resetting to a fixed literal is always safe to repeat and needs no lock.
* **A Column-Wise Currency Subtract Can Drive a Higher Denomination Negative Even With Plenty of Total Funds.** `currency_add`'s original spend path decomposed the requested amount into gold/silver/copper and subtracted each column independently, only borrowing when *silver* or *copper* went negative (never gold, the top denomination, since nothing sits above it to borrow from). A player holding 0 gold but 25 silver (250 copper-equivalent, easily enough) paying a 2-gold-denominated cost (200) had 2 subtracted straight from the gold column, landing on `-2 gold, 25 silver` — total value never checked against what the player actually held in *that* denomination. Every transaction in the game happened to stay under 10 (silver/copper only) until the Counting House's Letters of Credit became the first to spend a full gold's worth, which is what finally surfaced this after the mechanic had been live for a whole session. Fixed by redecomposing from the player's new *total* wealth (`(gold*100)+(silver*10)+copper) + currency_add_amount`, then re-splitting into gold/silver/copper from scratch) instead of adding/subtracting per column — there's no individual column left to run short on. Whenever a new spend is large enough to touch a denomination no prior content has exercised, don't assume old, quietly-working arithmetic generalizes; check it against a case where the player's coin sits in the "wrong" column for what's being spent.
* **A Shared Subroutine Called Multiple Times On the Same Page Needs a Per-Call-Site Lock — and a Single Shared "Last Call" Id Is Not Enough If It's Called More Than Twice.** `resolve_enemy_attack` guarded its whole roll+damage body behind one `choice_page_id` lock, but `fight_enemy_turn` calls it up to 3 times on the same page (once per living enemy slot in a multi-enemy fight, no pause between). The guard fired correctly for the first attacking slot, then silently skipped the 2nd and 3rd — the roll and damage never happened, but the narration (unguarded) still printed as if they did, redisplaying slot 1's stale hit/miss and damage number verbatim (a player caught it directly: three "hits," all reading "4 damage | HP: 3/7"). This had been live since multi-enemy fights were built, silently halving the effective damage of `fight_rennick_guards` and `fight_archive_rats` — a real balance bug hiding behind what looked like working combat log output. **The first fix attempt was itself wrong**, and instructive: it added a `combat_enemy_atk_call_id` input (mirroring `calendar.txt`'s `time_advance_call_id`) and compared it against one remembered `locked_combat_enemy_atk_call_id`. That pattern works for a call site invoked once (or a bounded few times with only one "most recent" needing memory) but breaks for 3 *sequential* calls needing 3 *independent* memories: by the time slot 3 finished, the single remembered id held "3," so a replay reconstructing from post-round stats saw slot 1's `call_id` (1) not matching the remembered "3" and re-rolled it fresh — `refresh_fuzz_test` caught this immediately as a new `[refresh-duplication]` finding the moment the first fix made the lock start actually firing multiple times per page. The real fix: three independent per-slot booleans (`combat_enemy_atk_locked_1/_2/_3`), checked via `combat_active_target` (already correctly set by the caller before each call) — each slot genuinely remembers its own state instead of sharing one variable that only the most recent call can occupy. Lesson: a `_call_id` comparison against a *single* remembered value assumes at most one "most recent" caller matters; the moment a loop calls the same guarded subroutine more than twice in one page, check whether earlier calls need to independently stay remembered, not just the last one.
* **A Persistent Stat Read For Display, Then Zeroed Later On The Same (Guarded) Page, Needs the Read Inside the Guard Too — Not Just Before It.** `ch_credit_reclaim` (Letters of Credit voiding) computed its "Refunded: X" display text from `letter_of_credit_value` *before* the guarded block that zeroes that same stat, reasoning the read simply happened first in program order. That's not enough: a refresh of this exact page reconstructs from the *current* persistent stats — which, by the time of the probe, already hold the post-reset value — and re-runs the unguarded read, producing "Refunded: 0" while the real coin transfer (properly locked) stayed correct. `refresh_fuzz_test` caught it as a `[refresh-duplication]` on `coin_amount_desc`. The fix: move the display computation *inside* the same `currency_txn_locked` guard as the reset, so it's captured exactly once, atomically with the mutation that would otherwise erase its input — on any later replay the whole block is skipped and the already-computed display string simply persists unchanged, the same way every other value that guard protects does. Contrast with `ch_credit_deposit`'s analogous display line, which sits *after* its guard reading a value that was only ever *incremented* (never reset) — safe either way, since the persistent stat holds the correct total whether the guard fires this pass or not. The risk is specific to reading a value for display right before something in the same guarded block *zeroes* it, not to post-guard reads in general.
* **Full Slot Coverage:** When gating by learned spells/cantrips, check every slot (primary, secondary) so no build is orphaned.
* **Three-Point Metadata Synchronization:** New lore/location/faction/companion needs all three: (1) `*create var false`, (2) progressive disclosure in the dossier (`*if (var)`), and (3) dev jump menus setting all prerequisite flags/clock state.
* **One-Time Story Grants Guard Themselves:** Wrap a discrete once-only grant (reward, relationship bump, item handoff) entirely in `*if (not(has_X)) ... *set has_X true ...`, checked before any side effect. Naturally replay-safe on its own — no `choice_page_id` lock needed, that's only for mutations with no natural "already happened" flag (e.g. currency, where the amount varies by branch).
* **Guard One-Time Init on a Dedicated Flag, Never a Variable With a Matching Default:** Gate a run-once block (e.g. a character-creation snapshot) with its own `*create ..._initialized false`, not another variable's value, without confirming its `*create` default first — if the default already reads as "done," the guard is permanently stuck one way. (This broke the equipment system once: `equipped_weapon_id` defaults to `"starting"`, so a guard checking `= ""` could never fire.)
* **`*set var -1` Is Decrement Shorthand, Not "Set to -1":** A leading `+`/`-` on `*set`'s right-hand side always means "adjust the current value," even when a literal assignment was intended (`currency_add`'s `ca_sign` bug: `*set ca_sign -1` on a freshly-`1` temp computed `0`, not `-1`). To assign a literal negative, use an expression whose first token isn't an operator: `*set ca_sign 0 - 1`.
* **A Bare `*create X 85` Stores a String; Only `*set X +N` Produces a Real Number.** `typeof` stays `"string"` until real arithmetic runs on it (ChoiceScript's own comparisons coerce loosely, so this is usually invisible). A Node harness inspecting `stats` directly must coerce with `Number(v)`/`isNaN`, not trust `typeof` — a fuzz script once silently skipped every `*create`-only stat this way.
* **`/` Never Floors — Halving an Odd Number Produces a Fractional Stat ChoiceScript Will Happily Print.** A plain `*set damage_taken damage_taken / 2` for a resistance effect looks right and passes every test that only checks "did the number go down," but an odd input (e.g. 3) yields `1.5`, and that fraction shows up verbatim in an HP banner (`HP: 10.5 / 14`) with nothing catching it — caught this exact bug in two independent copies of a barbarian Rage-resistance halving (`startup.txt`'s `resolve_enemy_attack` and `battle_black_sinks.txt`'s older `battle_take_damage`), both live for a full session before being noticed. Any `/ N` meant to floor (resistance, "half damage" effects) needs the same `(X - (X modulo N)) / N` pattern already used for `currency_add`'s carry math and `resolve_dissonant_whispers`'s failed-save half-damage — plain `/` is only safe when the numerator is provably always a multiple of the divisor.
* **When auditing a system for missing mechanics, the dossier (`choicescript_stats.txt`'s `print_ability_card`/class-features section) is the fastest source of truth for what's *promised* — grep it for "advantage"/"disadvantage"/"until"/"can't"/"reaction" and cross-check each hit against the actual combat code.** This is exactly how Ray of Frost/Vicious Mockery/Shocking Grasp's missing riders got found, and the same sweep on the Fighter Fighting Style section turned up a bigger one: `Dueling` (+2 one-handed melee damage) and `Great Weapon Fighting` (reroll 1s/2s on a two-handed weapon's damage die) were both selectable at character creation and fully described in the dossier, but did nothing in an actual fight — only `Defense` (+1 AC) had ever been wired in. Same sweep found Barbarian `Unarmored Defense` (10 + DEX + CON while not wearing armor) with zero implementation at all. Not every promise is worth chasing, though — `Protection` (impose disadvantage on an attack against a *nearby ally*) and Bardic Inspiration (*grant an ally* a die) both need an ally combatant this engine doesn't model at all, and `Arcane Recovery`/other "Short Rest" abilities need a short-rest event that doesn't exist anywhere in the campaign flow (only long rest does) — building either is a real feature, not a cheap fix, and implementing one short-rest ability while Second Wind/Warlock Pact Magic still silently only refresh on long rest would leave the game in a *more* inconsistent state than leaving all of them alone. Fix what maps onto mechanics that already exist; flag what would require inventing a new one, don't half-build it.

## 14. Grounded Economy, Metallurgy & Low-Fantasy Worldbuilding
* **Magic Is Rare, Distrusted, and Practical:** Common folk fear what they don't understand — suspicion of unnatural bloodlines, practitioners sitting apart at camp, line troops rattled by eldritch flares.
* **Economic Grit:** Silver, iron, and copper are scarce and hard-won. Loot is tangible and modest (smoked rations, tallow candles, water-damaged ledgers) — every mark counts.
* **Period-Grounded Vocabulary & Material Culture:** Concrete, setting-appropriate labor/logistics/military terms (mule-drivers, draymen, quartermasters), never modern or anachronistic shorthand.
* **Period Metallurgy (No Chemical-Era Elements):** Blacksmiths and armorers classify steel and iron by physical process, origin, or temper — never by modern chemical composition. Terms like "high-carbon," "carbon content," or "titanium" are strictly forbidden. Use authentic period terms: *shear-steel*, *blister-steel*, *crucible steel*, *steely iron*, *refined tool-steel*, *bog-iron*, *case-hardened iron*, or *quenched billet*.
* **Period Mechanical Engineering (No Industrial Modernisms):** Watermills, trip-hammers, and frontier engineering rely on timber and hand-forged iron primitives. Modern industrial words like "turbine," "drive-cam," "drive-shaft," "clutch," "flywheel," or "hydraulic assembly" belong to the 19th/20th centuries. Use period millwright terms: *waterwheel*, *mill-wheel*, *paddle-wheel*, *camshaft*, *trip-cams / wipers / tappets*, *wheel axle*, *intake sluice*, *flume*, *headrace*, *trundle wheel / iron gearing*, *timber housing*.
* **Fuel & Smelting Authenticity:** Fuels are *charcoal*, *peat-turf*, *pit-coal*, or *hard stone-coal*. Avoid post-Industrial Revolution shorthand like "coke-smelted" or nonsensical hybrids like "anthracite steel."

## 15. Mechanics Confined to Brackets, Never in Prose
* **Strict Separation of Stats and Story:** Numbers, DCs, and mechanic names stay in `[bracketed stat hints]`/banner cards — prose never states them directly ("you took 4 damage"). Ground results in physical sensation instead.
* **Mechanical Purity in Choice Brackets:** Bracketed hints (`[STR DC 12]`, `[5 Silver Marks]`, `[Cantrip: Guidance]`) are reserved for actionable mechanics only — never narrative summaries or thematic tags.

## 16. Time Progression & Chronological Discipline
* **Realistic Clock & Activity Alignment:** Every clock advance must correlate with the physical action (15-mile march = 6–8h; skirmish = 1h; camp activity = 30–45m; long rest = 8h), grounded in sensory detail (sweat cooling, mud crusting, shadows stretching).
* **Atmospheric & Seasonal Lighting Integrity:** Lighting/weather must match season and hour (Autumn: Dawn ~06:00, Dusk ~17:30–18:00, Night ~20:00+). A battle ending at 14:00 can't jump straight to "dusk cook-fires" without the intervening afternoon labor.
* **Midnight Rollover & Rest Sync:** Resting across midnight advances into early morning (~04:30–06:00) so `calendar.txt` rolls `campaign_day`/`calendar_date` — morning muster scenes must always land on a morning timestamp.
* **Incremental Hub Passage:** Multi-slot hubs pass time incrementally per visit (30–45m), with prose reflecting the changing light and watch as slots are spent.

## 17. Equipment & Magic Item Systems
* **New Weapons/Armor Route Through the Loadout System, Never a Bare `*set weapon`:** Direct `*set weapon`/`weapon_desc` etc. belongs only in chargen archetypes and inside `apply_weapon_loadout` itself. Anything picked up mid-game must instead: grant a `has_X` flag, add a branch in `apply_weapon_loadout` keyed by a new `equipped_weapon_id`, and let the player switch via `*gosub_scene startup equip_weapon "X"` from the Equipment codex. Every weapon needs a `weapon_hands` tag; equipping two-handed auto-drops `shield_equipped` via `apply_weapon_loadout`.
* **Magic Items Are Attuned, Never Given Outright:** A bonus must never be a permanent `*set <stat> +N` at pickup — attunement has to be *revocable*. Instead: grant `has_X`, add the bonus as a branch in `recalculate_attuned_bonuses` keyed on `X_attuned`, bind/unbind via `attune_item "X"`/`unattune_item "X"`, capped by `max_attunement_slots` (5). An ability-score bonus needs a `base_<stat>` snapshot (from `update_dnd_stats`) so it can be added/removed cleanly; the first item touching a new score also needs its own `recalculate_<stat>_mod` subroutine. Auto-attune on pickup if a slot is free, mirroring the shield's auto-equip.

## 18. Reusable Town-Content Primitives (Gambling, Taverns, Currency)
* **Never Hand-Roll a New Ante/Payout/Lock Trio Per Town — Call the Shared Subroutine.** `startup.txt` provides three so future towns don't reinvent this plumbing:
  * `currency_add` — pure arithmetic, no locking of its own. Input `currency_add_amount` (signed, copper-equivalent); adds/spends against `gold`/`silver`/`copper` with correct carry/borrow. Always call from inside the caller's own replay guard.
  * `resolve_wager` — one subroutine for any gambling table. Set `wager_bankroll_var` (that table's own bankroll stat name), `wager_ante_copper`, `wager_win_copper`, plus the usual `check_*`, then gosub. Debits the ante, credits the house, pays out on success capped to the bankroll. Returns `check_success` and `wager_bankroll_busted` — use the latter to hide the option once the house is tapped out (`taphouse_dice_table`). A new den just needs its own bankroll stat and a few `*set`s.
  * `buy_tavern_item` — one subroutine for a purchasable consumable that spends coin and optionally heals HP and/or grants the single-slot nourishment buff. Set `tavern_item_cost_copper`, `tavern_item_hp_heal`, and (if buffing) `tavern_item_buff_*`, then gosub. Hide an item's option while its own buff is already active, so players can't re-buy an effect they have.
  * All three handle their own internal replay locks already (`resolve_wager`'s ante and payout each get a dedicated pair) — a caller doesn't need `currency_txn_locked` around calls into them, only around hand-written currency mutations elsewhere.

## 19. Testing Tools & Verification
* **Eight complementary tools/techniques — use the right one, and don't treat any one as sufficient alone:**
  * `quicktest.js` (`node quicktest.js web/mygame`) — parses every scene and walks every reachable line once. Catches parse errors and `*goto`/`*label`/variable typos. Doesn't catch logic bugs.
  * `randomtest.js` (`node randomtest.js num=N seed=S`) — plays start-to-finish with random options, `N` times. Catches crashes/reachability across real playthroughs; does **not** check refresh-safety (never revisits a pause point it already passed).
    * `showUnreachedLabels=true` cross-references every `*label` against the run's hit-count map and reports the ones that stayed at 0 — cheap way to catch dead content (an `*if` guard that can never be false, a `*label` nothing `*goto`s anymore). Not automatically dead, though — may just be content this seed range didn't reach (especially squad/origin-gated); rerun with a different `num`/`seed` before concluding it's truly unreachable.
  * `refresh_fuzz_test.js` (`node refresh_fuzz_test.js num=N seed=S`) — catches the refresh/replay-duplication class the rest of this doc guards against. At every pause, snapshots stats, independently replays the page against a clone, diffs the result; any stat that comes out different re-ran on replay and shouldn't have. Run after any change touching a bare `*set stat +N`/`-N`, not just once at the end.
    * `mode=smart archetype=str|dex|con|int|wis|cha|balanced` makes the walk build-aware: scans each option for its `check_stat`, always takes an available `[Cantrip: Guidance]` first, otherwise prefers the archetype's matching stat (or the build's actual highest for `balanced`). Gives repeatable coverage of each archetype's real success paths instead of depending on chargen RNG. `showChoices=true` logs every pick and why.
    * `[refresh-duplication]` findings are always worth investigating. `[shadow-diverged]` and `[shadow-no-pause]` are diagnostic-only categories (see below) — don't chase them as bugs.
  * `tools/lint_thin_prose.js` (`node tools/lint_thin_prose.js [minWords=10]`) — static scan flagging `*choice` options that resolve with little or no actual narration before the next `*goto`/`*gosub_scene`/choice (something happened — damage dealt, an item bought — with no prose wrapped around it). Catches what none of the above can: a thin resolution is perfectly valid ChoiceScript and won't crash or diverge on replay, it just reads as an empty beat. Report-only (`THIN` findings are the punch list; `SILENT` — zero printed output — is mostly this codebase's normal "selection-menu with header-only flavor, shared narration after the `*choice` block" pattern and isn't printed at all by default, pass `showSilent=true` for the list; `DELEGATED` options whose prose comes from a `${variable}` — this project's shared-flavor-string pattern, see `combat.txt` — can't be scored statically and aren't flagged).
  * `tools/lint_mechanics_leak.js` (`node tools/lint_mechanics_leak.js`) — greps narration (outside `[brackets]`/`${...}`/`[b]...[/b]` banner spans) for mechanic vocabulary leaking into prose per Section 15: `DC <N>`, `<N> damage`, `<N> HP`, `NdN` dice notation, "armor class" or an ability check named directly ("a STR check"). Caught a real instance this way (a Relentless Endurance beat narrating "clinging to 1 HP" — fixed by grounding it in sensation instead, the HP number was already shown in the banner line right after). Report-only; expect the occasional false positive in the dossier's own stat-card lines if a new one doesn't use the `[b]...[/b]` banner convention.
  * `tools/lint_prose_tics.js` (`node tools/lint_prose_tics.js [includeNegativeFraming=false]`) — greps for the specific filler phrases Section 8 names by pattern ("says nothing"/"without a word", "Not X. Just Y.", a standalone one-word dialogue reply, "the way he always"/"the kind of quiet that", "files it away"/"let that settle"). The "doesn't/didn't `<verb>`" negative-framing check is off by default — on this codebase it produced ~53 hits, nearly all legitimate (weather/fact descriptions, or already-compliant "didn't X. Did Y instead" framing) — opt in only when specifically hunting that pattern. Report-only.
  * `tools/lint_weapon_assumption.js` (`node tools/lint_weapon_assumption.js`) — flags Section 22's forbidden phrases (pommel, hilt, scabbard, bare steel, iron grip, half-drawn, clutching your weapon) and walks each match's enclosing `*if`/`*elseif` chain (stopping at a `*label`) to check whether anything actually gates on `weapon`/`weapon_type`. `VIOLATION` = no such gate anywhere in scope (confident). `REVIEW` = some weapon-related gate exists but the tool can't confirm it's scoped to bladed weapons specifically (Section 22's own trap: `character_class = "fighter"` alone doesn't prove a blade). Caught and fixed several real instances in `alderford.txt`'s bailiff-confrontation scene this way. Expect real false positives from ambiguous English ("iron grip" as a political-control metaphor, "hilt" as a mature-content idiom, or a correctly-typed NPC weapon mention like Lyra's bow/knife) — read every hit in context, this is a candidate list, not a verdict. Report-only.
  * `tools/lint_anachronisms.js` (`node tools/lint_anachronisms.js`) — greps narration and dialogue for chemical and industrial engineering anachronisms forbidden by Section 24 (carbon, turbine, drive-cam, drive-shaft, cast-iron assembly, pneumatic, flywheel, anthracite steel, etc.). Report-only.
  * `tools/lint_blank_landing.js` (`node tools/lint_blank_landing.js [maxScan=50]`) — catches a bug class none of the above can: a `*page_break` whose destination prints nothing at all before its `*choice`, so the player sees a genuinely empty screen (just buttons) right after a hard page transition. Distinct from `lint_thin_prose.js`'s `SILENT` category, which only looks *inside* a `*choice` option's own body — a silent option is fine as long as it's on the same scrolling page as text that already printed above it, but `*page_break` forces a fresh page, so if the destination's own header is empty there's nothing left on screen at all. Found live: one of four "ask Maura a question" options in `alderford.txt` had a stray `*page_break` its three siblings didn't, landing on a header-less revisit hub (`taphouse_bar_loop`) — invisible to every other check because nothing crashes and no option is individually thin. A full sweep afterward found the same shape at 22 sites across 5 header-less hubs (`lyra_dialogue_hub`, `counting_house_menu`, `taphouse_bar_loop`, `taphouse_dice_menu`, `taphouse_smiths_menu`) — a systemic authoring habit (page-break after every dialogue/transaction beat), not a one-off. Two different fixes depending on the hub's shape, both applied: **remove the `*page_break`** for a one-off beat so its closing prose stays visible with the reappearing menu right below it (21 of the 22 sites — matches its siblings that never had the page break in the first place); **add a short, stable header to the hub itself** only where the pause is doing real work — `taphouse_dice_menu` is a repeatable mini-game loop where a fresh page per round is the *better* UX (an unbroken wall of accumulated roll text only gets worse the more someone plays), so its 3 sites kept the page break and got one generic table-grounding line instead. Method (a text-line heuristic, not a real parse — doesn't evaluate `*if`/`*else` branch logic, just whether *any* line in the reachable span looks like prose) also distinguishes a genuinely **BLANK** landing from a **BANNER-ONLY** one (a `[bracket]` stat/status dashboard with no narrative sentence, e.g. combat's per-round HP banner in `fight_round_hub`) — the latter is usually intentional design, not a bug, and is reported separately, low-priority, spot-check-don't-fix-by-default. Report-only; read every hit, this is a candidate list.
  * **A one-off directed trace** for a specific mechanic the other three are unlikely to reach on their own (a particular class+spell combo, a specific fight outcome). Bootstrap identically to `randomtest.js`, but override `Scene.prototype.choice` to pick options by substring match against a scripted `PICKS="Cadre Dread Warlock|Jump to Chapter 2|...|[Spell: Armor of Agathys]"` list (`|`-split, falls back to the first available option once exhausted). Can force a dev-menu preset straight into any fight/branch; a `stats[k] = v` injection pass (once `stats.intake_completed`) can set a spell/cantrip no preset combines, without hand-writing a new one. Delete the scratch script when done — it's a throwaway, not something to commit.
* **Harness setup requirements** for any of the above written fresh: override `saveCookie` to fire its callback immediately (not deferred through the same `timeout` var `clearScreen` uses — `printLoop()` calls it on every pause purely to persist stats for a refresh that'll never happen in a harness; a deferred no-op silently clobbers the real `*choice` continuation and stops the walk after one choice while still reporting a pass); and set `global.safeTimeout = function(fn) { safeCall(null, fn); };` before loading `mygame.js` (without it, a fresh `Scene`'s first `execute()` defers through a real async `setTimeout` a synchronous harness never gets to run, reading as "no content" instead of the parse error it actually is).
* **`refresh_fuzz_test.js`'s replay simulation has a blind spot at a pause point reached through `*gosub_scene`** (any fight run through `combat.txt`): reconstructing a bare `Scene` at the recorded line/scene without rebuilding `stats.choice_subscene_stack` means the simulated `*return` has nothing to return to, so it runs off the end to `*finish` instead of re-pausing — reported as `[shadow-no-pause]`, not a confirmed bug. To actually verify refresh-safety there, simulate a **real** cold refresh: capture `choice_page_start_scene`/`_line`/`_indent` at the pause (exactly what a real reload reads), construct a fresh `Scene(choice_page_start_scene, stats, nav, false)` at those coordinates with fresh `temps`, execute, and confirm it re-pauses identically — no subscene-stack reconstruction needed, since that already lives on `stats` and survives the new Scene object for free.

## 20. Reusable Small-Combat Engine (`startup.txt` primitives + `resolve_*`)
* **For a short, contained skirmish, reuse the combat primitives in `startup.txt` rather than hand-rolling a new one per fight.** Built and proven on the Alderford culvert encounter.
  * **The player's own attack roll needs zero new code** — just `roll_d20_check` with `check_dc` = the enemy's AC (`combat_enemy_ac`). Advantage, guidance, nat 20/1, expertise, meal buffs all work for free.
  * **`resolve_player_hit`/`resolve_player_weapon_hit`** — call after `check_success` with `combat_player_dmg_dice_sides`/`_bonus` set. Applies to `combat_enemy_hp` (floored at 0), self-locked, reports `combat_player_dmg_dealt`. The weapon variant additionally resolves Sneak Attack.
  * **`resolve_enemy_attack`** — rolls the enemy's attack against `armor_class`, applies damage to `hp_current` on a hit (halved raging, floored at 1 — let the caller narrate a rescue instead of a kill). Self-locked. Reads `combat_enemy_atk_bonus`/`_dmg_dice_sides`/`_dmg_bonus` (set once at fight start) and `combat_enemy_disadvantage`/`_advantage` (per-round caller flags; `_advantage` self-clears after one use, `_disadvantage` the caller resets each round). Both true cancels per normal rules.
  * **A per-round mutation (round counter, action-menu choice) still needs the standard replay lock** — not exempt for being "in a loop." Reset the counter to a fixed literal at the fight's true start; put the (locked) increment only where a genuinely new page cycle begins (end of enemy turn, before `*page_break`) — never both on the fight-start page.
  * **A reaction spell (Shield, etc.) doesn't need a new pause point.** An "arm it" option sets a persistent flag and `*goto`s back to the same round hub at no action cost (mirroring the Guidance-cantrip trick); the relevant `resolve_*` primitive checks the flag and auto-fires/disarms itself the moment it matters, inside its own existing pass. Less "realistic" than a true interrupt-and-ask, but needs no new engine surface — only build a real interrupt if that tradeoff specifically matters for an ability.
  * **A flag set before a `*gosub_scene` can get cleared by the very call it's feeding.** `roll_d20_check` resets `advantage`/`disadvantage` before returning, so "was advantage true going into this roll" (Sneak Attack's trigger) needs capturing before the call — or better, read it as the primitive's own output: `roll_had_advantage`, set inside `roll_d20_check`'s own locked fresh-roll branch, is naturally replay-safe the same way `check_success` is, with no caller-managed flag needed at all.
  * **A spell with a genuinely unusual damage shape** (Magic Missile: 3d4+3, always-hits, no attack roll) is often clearer as its own small inline block (a few `*rand` calls into `combat_enemy_hp`, own lock) than forced through `resolve_player_hit`, which only models "one roll, gated on `check_success`." Don't generalize the primitive for a one-off.
  * **Audit a "built for later" dormant subroutine for a replay lock before giving it its first real caller** — `second_wind`/`activate_rage` sat unlocked for a session because nothing had called them yet; the bug had simply never been exercised.
  * **A resource-spending spell that reports one computed number (temp HP, HP healed) doesn't need its own output variable** — `combat_cast_result` is shared across False Life and Healing Word. Likewise, buff casts that can never be chosen in the same round as each other can share one lock (`combat_cast_locked` covers Hex, Agathys, False Life, Healing Word) instead of a dedicated pair each.
  * **An ongoing damage-rider (Hex's +1d6 "on every hit") belongs inside every damage-dealing primitive itself**, not bolted onto each choice branch — a future attack option then gets correct interaction for free.
  * **Temporary hit points need their own pool (`temp_hp`), separate from `hp_current`, absorbed first on a hit before any overflow.** Keeps ordinary HP clamping ignorant of temp HP, and lets a shattering effect (Armor of Agathys) detect "temp_hp hit exactly 0" cleanly.
  * **Bonus-action spells loop back to the round hub free; full-action spells consume the round.** Don't retrofit this onto abilities that already shipped consuming the round despite being bonus actions in 5e (Second Wind, Rage) — that's a deliberate, tested tradeoff, not an oversight.
  * **Attack cantrips need their own bespoke `*choice` option each, in the shared engine file — never a single generic "cast a cantrip" option reading back a detected name/stat/dice-sides.** A generic cascade was tried first (`detect_attack_cantrip` populating `has_attack_cantrip`/`attack_cantrip_name`/`_stat`/`_dice_sides`, one "Channel ${attack_cantrip_name}" option reading them back) and broke two things at once: every cantrip printed the same "the working..." text regardless of which one it was (a direct Section 2 violation — see `combat.txt`'s `fight_round_hub` for the fix, six separate options, one per cantrip), and because the detection had to be an `*else` chain (only one winner), a build with two attack cantrips could only ever reach whichever was checked first — the second was silently unreachable in combat. Six bespoke options costs the same one-time write as the shared buff-spell options already do (Hex, Agathys, etc.) and both bugs disappear; a fight never needs its own copy, so this doesn't reopen the "5 fights, 10 spells, do we duplicate everything" scalability concern the generic cascade was built to dodge in the first place.
  * **Where a cantrip/spell's RAW secondary effect maps cleanly onto an existing engine flag, wire it in — check the dossier's `print_ability_card` (`choicescript_stats.txt`) for what's already being promised to the player first.** Ray of Frost's "target's speed reduced by 10 ft" and Vicious Mockery's "throw off their next strike" both became `*set combat_enemy_disadvantage true` on a hit — the same flag `resolve_enemy_attack` already reads for a Brace action, so no new mechanic was needed, just reusing the one that exists. Don't force a translation that doesn't fit (Chill Touch's "can't regain HP" — this engine never grants enemies healing at all, so there's nothing to prevent) — a vivid, distinct description on its own already satisfies Section 2; a mechanical rider is a bonus where one actually maps, not an obligation to invent one everywhere.
  * **Every hit/miss line needs real variety (3 randomized phrasings, not one fixed sentence), and a killing blow needs its own distinct text — a fight that reuses one line every round, with no acknowledgment when a hit is the one that actually ends it, reads as flat and mechanical no matter how good the individual sentence is.** Built two layers: (1) `combat_flavor_weapon_hit/miss_1/2/3` (melee), `_ranged_1/2/3`, and `combat_flavor_enemy_hit/miss_1/2/3` (plus optional per-slot `enemy2/3` override triplets) are picked by a fresh `*rand 1 3` every time, via `pick_weapon_flavor` (player) and `fight_narrate_enemy_turn`'s own selection logic (enemy) — each fight writes all three variants per line, and all three must vary the actual *physical action* (a thrust vs. timing an overextension vs. a parry-and-counter), not just reshuffle adjectives around the same motion, or the repetition just moves up a level. (2) A death blow — `combat_enemy_hp <= 0` checked immediately after the damage primitive returns, at *every* damage source (the weapon attack including Sneak Attack, all six cantrips, Magic Missile, Dissonant Whispers, and Armor of Agathys' retaliation) — swaps in `pick_weapon_death_flavor`/`pick_spell_death_flavor` instead of the ordinary hit line, plus a `💀 ... Defeated` banner in place of the usual `🩸 Hit` one. Unlike the per-round hit/miss triplets, the death-blow lines are deliberately generic and enemy-agnostic (parametrized with `${combat_enemy_epithet}`/`${weapon}` only, no per-fight override) — mirroring how buff-spell flavor already stays enemy-agnostic on purpose, since a full per-fight *and* per-attack-type finisher set (3 fights × up to 9 damage sources × 3 variants) would be a much larger writing cost than one good shared set for comparatively little payoff. `*create` rejects a `${}`-interpolated default value ("value must be a simple string") — the death-blow text lives inline inside the two `pick_*_death_flavor` subroutines in `combat.txt`, not as named `startup.txt` constants, for exactly that reason.
  * **A rider that depends on a per-enemy trait (not just a hit/miss), like Shocking Grasp's advantage against a metal-armored target, needs its own tag on the enemy — cheap to add given the existing per-slot fields.** `combat_enemy_metal_armor` (`startup.txt`) follows the exact same pattern as `combat_enemy_epithet`: a `combat_enemy1_metal_armor`/`_2`/`_3` per slot (defaulting `false`), snapshotted by `fight_setup` and mirrored by `fight_sync_target_in` alongside every other per-slot field, so `combat.txt`'s Shocking Grasp option just reads the plain `combat_enemy_metal_armor` name like any other primitive would. No existing fight sets it true yet (a Silt-Lurker and casual cudgel-wielding dock-guards are both honest "no" answers) — it's there for the next fight that's actually against armored soldiers/knights/constructs, verified by injecting it true in a directed trace rather than waiting for real content to exist.

## 21. Fights Are a Consequence of a Failed Roll, Not a Given
* **A quest that can end in combat needs a real approach beat first** — jumping straight from "accept" to "combat started" skips the tension a skill-based approach builds. Mirror the culvert's three-way split: a careful approach granting `advantage` on success with no failure penalty, a clever approach that can resolve the whole thing without a fight, and a no-check "just go in" for anyone skipping straight to combat.
* **A non-combat resolution still has to satisfy what the quest-giver asked for.** If the stated goal is elimination, a bypass has to still be a kill (an environmental kill reads as clever, not anticlimactic) — leaving the threat alive doesn't close the request even if it removes the danger. Same reward as a combat win, since the practical outcome is identical.
* **A failed bypass needs a real, asymmetric cost, not "the fight starts anyway."** The culvert's stealth failure costs only the forfeited advantage; its investigation failure specifically alerts the creature and hands *it* the advantage. Differentiate per approach rather than converging every failure on the same neutral outcome.

## 22. Weapon-Class-Agnostic Prose (No Assumed Weapon Type)
* **Never assume a bladed weapon in shared prose not gated on class/weapon.** "Hand drops to your pommel," "bare steel from your scabbard," "grip the hilt," "blade half-drawn" are meaningless for a longbow, quarterstaff, crossbow, or arcane focus — including inside class-gated branches, since a Fighter can still have picked a spear or bow.
* **A threatening/intimidation beat should read from posture and presence, not from a hand-on-weapon gesture at all — even a correctly weapon-neutral one.** Swapping the bladed phrase for `${weapon}` ("hand resting on your ${weapon}") only fixes the physical-accuracy bug; it's still narratively off for a mage or archer, whose actual first line of defense isn't the mundane backup dagger at their belt, and for whom "resting a hand on it" doesn't read as a threat the way gripping a blade does. Write the menace as stillness, voice, or bearing instead ("you go utterly still, meeting his gaze," "radiating quiet menace," "your gaze level and unflinching") — this reads correctly for every archetype with zero weapon reference needed, and is usually the better line anyway (a real instance of this exact substitution got caught and rewritten across `alderford.txt`'s bailiff-confrontation scene).
* **The `${weapon}` variable is still the right tool for carry/proximity beats** ("your ${weapon} within arm's reach," "settling your ${weapon} across your knees") — those aren't claiming anything about how the character *uses* the weapon, just that they're carrying it. The distinction is whether the beat is about the object's presence (fine) or about it being the source of a threat (write posture instead).
* **Scan trigger:** before finalizing any passage containing *pommel*, *hilt*, *scabbard*, *bare steel*, *iron grip*, *half-drawn*, *clutching your weapon*, or a hand/grip resting "on your ${weapon}" as a threat gesture, confirm the surrounding `*if` actually gates the specific weapon (for the bladed-phrase case) and, separately, whether a posture-based rewrite serves the beat better regardless of gating.

## 23. Combat as a Standalone Scene File (`combat.txt`) — Fight Library Pattern
* **The round-by-round combat loop lives once in `combat.txt`**, entered via `*gosub_scene combat fight_<name>`. It returns with `combat_outcome` (`"victory"`/`"rescued"`/`"fled"`) and prints no start/end-of-fight prose itself — the caller branches on the outcome and owns 100% of the narrative reaction, so the kill/retreat beat and the quest-giver's response read as one continuous scene.
* **A new fight costs one short `fight_<name>` entry label** — enemy `combat_enemy_*` stats and a handful of `combat_flavor_*` strings, then `*gosub fight_setup` / `*goto fight_round_hub`. Everything else (every spell option, enemy-turn resolution, retreat/rescue) is already shared.
* **Most ability-casting flavor is enemy-agnostic and belongs directly in the shared `fight_round_hub`, not parametrized per fight** — Hex, Agathys, False Life, Healing Word, Magic Missile, Shield, Second Wind, and Rage all describe the player's own casting motion, not the creature (strip any stray environmental wording so they read fine anywhere). What *is* genuinely bespoke, and needs a `combat_flavor_*` stat per fight: weapon hit/miss, cantrip hit/miss, the enemy's own attack, Dissonant Whispers' narration (assumes a monster, not a human foe), and `combat_enemy_epithet` (how spell lines refer to the target — "the creature" vs. "the bandit").
* **Reusing the same enemy across two quests just means two scenes calling the same `fight_<name>` label** — `*gosub_scene`'s return address is dynamic (pushed onto `stats.choice_subscene_stack` at call time, popped by `*return`), so it always hands control back to whichever quest actually called it. For encounters that should *feel* distinct despite reusing a monster type, copy the label and retune its flavor strings.
* **Up to 3 simultaneous enemies are supported (`combat_enemy_count`), and a single-enemy fight needs zero changes to work with it.** Slot 1 IS the original `combat_enemy_*` names a single-enemy fight already sets; `fight_setup` snapshots whatever's there into `combat_enemy1_*` (its real permanent home) automatically. A multi-enemy fight additionally sets `combat_enemy_count 2` (or `3`) plus `combat_enemy2_*`/`combat_enemy3_*` (and optionally `combat_flavor_enemy2_hit/miss`/`_enemy3_hit/miss` — falls back to the shared `combat_flavor_enemy_hit/miss` when a slot doesn't define its own, so identical mooks only need flavor written once) before `*gosub fight_setup`. Every existing damage/attack primitive in `startup.txt` stays single-target and untouched — `combat.txt`'s `fight_sync_target_in`/`fight_sync_target_out`/`fight_pick_target` mirror whichever slot is "active" into the canonical `combat_enemy_*` names right before/after a primitive touches them, so the primitives never had to learn multiple enemies exist. The player always auto-targets the first living slot (order 1/2/3, never picks a dead one back up); each living slot gets its own attack roll every round in `fight_enemy_turn` (a defeated slot's turn is skipped entirely — no roll, no prose); the round-hub banner lists every slot's HP with a `(Down)` tag once defeated. `fight_cleanup` resets `combat_enemy_count`/`combat_active_target` back to 1 so multi-enemy state never leaks into the next fight. First built and proven on `fight_rennick_guards` (`alderford.txt`'s curing-shed standoff) — **tune per-enemy damage down from what a solo fight would use**, since N attackers roughly multiplies a round's expected incoming damage by N; the first pass at this fight (guard stats sized like a solo encounter) nearly dropped a 10-HP caster to the rescue floor in round one against two guards, and needed a real reduction in `atk_bonus`/`dmg_bonus` to land as a fair multi-round fight instead of a coin-flip near-knockout.
* **A `*gosub_scene`-only library scene like `combat.txt` must NOT be added to `*scene_list`** — but it does still have to be told to `compile.js` by name, in a *second*, separate place. List order also builds the implicit "`*finish` continues into the next declared scene" chain (`nav.nextSceneName` is a flat `scene_list[i] → [i+1]` map). Inserting `combat` after `startup` once silently hijacked character creation's `ready_camp` `*finish` into `combat.txt` instead of `camp_night.txt`, crashing on an unrelated bare `*return` deep in the file. `*gosub_scene` doesn't require list membership to target a scene at runtime — but fixing that bug by removing `combat` from `*scene_list` broke something else: `compile.js` has **no directory-scan fallback** (its own comment: "as we can't read the dir") and only bundles files it's told about — `*scene_list` entries, plus a short hardcoded list (`verifyFileName("calendar.txt")` and friends) for exactly this "subroutine scene, not in the list" case. `combat.txt` silently stopped being bundled into `play_game.html` the moment it left `*scene_list`, with zero error at compile time — `quicktest`/`randomtest`/`refresh_fuzz_test` all load scenes a different way (straight off disk) and never touched this path, so nothing caught it until a real compiled build hit `*gosub_scene combat ...` and failed with "scene doesn't exist." **Any scene excluded from `*scene_list` for the `*finish`-chain reason above must be added to `compile.js`'s hardcoded `verifyFileName(...)` list instead** (mirror the `calendar.txt` line right next to it) — and re-check the actual compiled `play_game.html` for the scene's data (`grep '"combat":'` or similar), not just `quicktest`/`randomtest`, since those two tools can't catch this class of bug at all.

## 24. Technological, Chemical & Period Integrity (Zero Anachronisms)
* **Chemical & Elemental Terminology:** In a low-fantasy medieval setting, chemical concepts discovered in the 18th–20th centuries do not exist in common parlance.
  * **Forbidden:** "high-carbon," "carbon steel," "carbon content," "titanium," "chemical reaction," "calories."
  * **Setting-Grounded Equivalents:** *shear-steel*, *blister-steel*, *crucible steel*, *refined tool-steel*, *steely iron*, *quenched steel*, *bog-iron*, *bloomery iron*, *spring steel*.
* **Mechanical & Industrial Engineering Terminology:** Power in this setting comes from waterwheels, draft beasts, counterweights, and muscle. Industrial Revolution mechanisms and terminology break world immersion.
  * **Forbidden:** "turbine," "drive-cam," "drive-shaft," "clutch," "flywheel," "cast-iron assembly," "pneumatic," "combustion."
  * **Setting-Grounded Equivalents:** *waterwheel*, *mill-wheel*, *paddle-wheel*, *camshaft*, *trip-cams / wipers / tappets*, *wheel axle*, *timber shaft*, *intake sluice*, *flume*, *headrace*, *trundle wheel / iron gearing*, *counterweight ratchet*.
* **Smelting Fuels & Materials:**
  * **Forbidden:** "coke-smelted," "forged anthracite steel" (anthracite is a coal, not a metal).
  * **Setting-Grounded Equivalents:** *charcoal*, *peat-turf*, *pit-coal*, *hard stone-coal*, *bloomery hearth*, *crucible melt*.
* **Scan Trigger:** Before finalizing any craft, mechanical, or workshop passage, run `tools/lint_anachronisms.js` to flag modern engineering and chemical terms.
